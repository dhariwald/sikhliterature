# Panth Prakash, Episode 33

## The Story of the Village of Sehar Khand

### (...maiṅ hoṅ usī purakh ko dās: ...I am the servant of that same Man)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਤਬ ਬੰਦੋ ਚੜ੍ਹ ਉਤਰਿਓ ਸੇਹਰ ਖੰਡੋ ਗ੍ਰਾਮ ॥\
ਚਿੱਠੇ ਲਿਖੇ ਪੰਜਾਬ ਨੂੰ ਲੈ ਲੈ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਨਾਮ ॥੧॥

Then Banda (having dealt with the commander of Kaithal) marched on and made his camp at the village of Sehar Khand,[^1]\
and from that camp he wrote letters to the Singhs of the Punjab, writing to the foremost of them one by one, each by his own name. (1)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਤਬ ਬੰਦੇ ਯੌ ਲਿਖ ਫੁਰਮਾਯੋ ॥ ਦੇਸ਼ ਲੈਨ ਗੁਰ ਮੋਰ ਪਠਾਯੋ ॥\
ਸਿਰਹੰਦੀਯਨ ਕੀ ਜੜ੍ਹ ਪੁੱਟਣ ਧਾਯਾ ॥ ਪ੍ਰਬਤੀਅਨ ਕੋ ਮੈਂ ਮਾਰਨ ਆਯਾ ॥

Then Banda wrote out his command to the Singhs in these words:\
"The Guru has sent me here to take possession of this land.\
I have marched out to tear up the men of Sirhind[^2] by the roots,\
and I have come to kill the rajas of the hills. (2)

ਮੈਂ ਹੋਂ ਉਸੀ ਪੁਰਖ ਕੋ ਦਾਸਾ ॥ ਕਰਯੋ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਜਿਸੈ ਪ੍ਰਕਾਸਾ ॥\
ਜਿਨ ਗੁਰ ਜੀ ਸੋਂ ਬੈਰ ਕਮਾਯੋ ॥ ਬਦਲੋ ਲੈਨ ਤਿਸੈ ਮੈਂ ਆਯੋ ॥੩॥

"I am the humble servant of that same Man, Guru Gobind Singh,\
who brought the Khalsa into being and into the light.\
Whoever made himself an enemy of the Guru Ji and worked against him,\
I have come to take my revenge on him. (3)

ਤਿਨ ਤਿਨ ਕੌ ਮੈਂ ਮਾਰ ਗਵਾਊਂ ॥ ਤੌ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਕੋ ਬੰਦਾ ਸਦਾਊਂ ॥\
ਯੌ ਪੁਨ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਲਿਖੀ ਅਰਦਾਸ ॥ ਭੇਜੇ ਜੋ ਗੁਰ ਨਿਜ ਸਿਖ ਖਾਸ ॥

"Only when I have struck down and destroyed every one of them\
shall I have earned the right to be called the Satguru's baṅdā,[^3] his true servant."\
And then he wrote out an ardās[^4] to the Khalsa, a call to all the Singhs,\
and sent it by the hands of the Sikhs the Guru had sent with him, his own chosen men. (4)

ਜੌ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਸੋਂ ਸਿੱਖ ਹੋ ਸਾਬਤ ॥ ਸਹਿਬਜ਼ਾਦੇ ਬੈਰਹ ਬਾਬਤ ॥\
ਆਇ ਦਯੋ ਬੰਦੈ ਕੋ ਸਾਥ ॥ ਗੁਰ ਕੋ ਸਿਖ ਸੋ ਸਿੱਖਨ ਭ੍ਰਾਤ ॥੫॥

"Let every Sikh who stands whole and unshaken in his faith in the Satguru,\
for the sake of the feud of the Sahibzadas,[^5] the Guru's murdered sons,\
come now and stand at Banda's side in this work;\
for a Sikh of the Guru is a brother to every other Sikh. (5)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਜੋ ਖਾਲਸੇ ਮੈਂ ਆ ਰਲੇ ਪਤਿਸ਼ਾਹੀਓਂ ਵੱਡਾ ਹੋਇ ॥\
ਜੋ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਸੋਂ ਨਹਿਂ ਮਿਲੈ ਰਹਿ ਪਛਤਾਵਤ ਓਇ ॥੬॥

"Whoever comes now and joins the Khalsa shall rise to be greater than any Patshahi;\
and whoever does not join the Khalsa shall be left behind, to sit and regret it ever after." (6)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਕਾਬਲ ਔ ਕੰਧਾਰ ਲੌ ਠੱਠੈ ਭੱਖਰ ਦਾਇ ॥\
ਪੂਰਬ ਦੱਖਣ ਉਤਰ ਸੁ ਚਿੱਠੇ ਦਏ ਪਹੁੰਚਾਇ ॥੭॥

As far as Kabul and Kandahar, and away toward Thatta and Bhakkar,[^6]\
to the east, to the south and to the north, he had his letters carried to the Guru's Singhs. (7)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਜੋ ਜੋ ਸਿਖ ਹੈ ਗੁਰ ਕੋ ਪੂਰੋ ॥ ਸ਼ਜ਼ਾਦਨ ਬੈਰ ਲੈਨ ਅਯੋ ਜ਼ਰੂਰੋ ॥\
ਜੋ ਜੀਵਗੁ ਸੋ ਪਾਉਗੁ ਰਾਜ ॥ ਜਾਉਗੁ ਮਰ ਹੋਊਗੁ ਅਗਲੋ ਕਾਜ ॥੮॥

"Every Sikh who is wholly the Guru's, and true to him in everything,\
must come without fail to take revenge for the Sahibzadas.\
Whoever lives through this war shall win the rule of the land;\
and whoever dies in it shall find his work fulfilled in the world to come." (8)

ਐਸੀ ਭੇਜੀ ਲਿਖ ਅਰਦਾਸ ॥ ਭੇਜੀ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਪਾਸ ॥\
ਸੋ ਸੁਨ ਖਾਲਸੇ ਸਿਰ ਪਰ ਧਰੀ ॥ ਤੁਰਤ ਰਲਨ ਕੀ ਤਯਾਰੀ ਕਰੀ ॥੯॥

Such was the ardās that Banda wrote and sent out to the Singhs,\
and the Khalsa passed it on, from one body of the Khalsa to the next.\
The Khalsa heard it and placed it upon their heads, as a command to be obeyed,\
and at once they began to make ready to go and join him. (9)

ਜਿਸੈ ਸਿਖ ਪਹਿ ਖਰਚ ਨ ਹੋਇ ॥ ਬਿਨਾ ਖਰਚ ਬੰਡ ਖਾਵੈ ਸੋਇ ॥\
ਅਸਿਖ ਸਿਖਨ ਕੋ ਦੇਵੈਂ ਨਾਹੀਂ ॥ ਕਹੈਂ ਸਿੱਖ ਸਭ ਊਹਾਂ ਮਰਾਹੀਂ ॥੧੦॥

A Sikh who had nothing of his own to spend on the road\
was fed from what the others had, for the Singhs shared their food with him.\
But those who were not Sikhs would give the Sikhs nothing at all,\
saying, "Every one of these Sikhs will be killed out there in the war." (10)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਹੱਥੀਂ ਪੈਰੀਂ ਪੈ ਲਵੈਂ ਕਰ ਦੂਨੋ ਚੌਨ ਕਰਾਰ ॥\
ਜੀਵਤ ਆਵੈਂ ਆਇ ਦਿਵੈਂ ਮੁਏ ਅਗਲੇ ਸੰਸਾਰ ॥੧੧॥

So they pleaded with joined hands and fell at men's feet to borrow, promising to pay it back twice over, four times over:\
"If we come back alive, we will come and repay you in full; and if we die, we shall pay our debt in the world to come." (11)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਕਈ ਸਿੱਖ ਦੇਵੈਂ ਗੁਰਸ੍ਵਾਰਥ ॥ ਤਨ ਮਨ ਧਨ ਦੇਹੁ ਗੁਰਹਿ ਪ੍ਰਮਾਰਥ ॥\
ਮਝੈਲ ਰਸਤੋ ਜੋ ਸਿਧੋ ਤਕਾਵੈਂ ॥ ਤੁਰਕ ਫੌਜ ਬਹੁ ਰਾਹਿ ਰੁਕਾਵੈਂ ॥੧੨॥

Many Sikhs gave freely of what they had, for the Guru's sake alone,\
giving body, mind and wealth to the Guru, for the highest good.\
But the Majhail Singhs,[^7] who looked to come by the straight road from the Majha,\
found that many armies of the Turks[^8] stood across their path and barred the way. (12)

ਪਹਿਲੋ ਸਿੰਘ ਬਨਜਾਰੇ ਰਲੇ ॥ ਫੜ ਹਥ ਬਰਛੇ ਬੈਲ ਲਦ ਭਲੇ ॥\
ਮਾਲਵੋ ਉਹਾਂ ਥਾਂ ਨੇੜੇ ਦੇਸ ॥ ਓਇ ਭੀ ਮਿਲੇ ਹੁਇ ਬੰਦੇ ਪੇਸ਼ ॥੧੩॥

At first some of the Singhs got through by slipping in among the Banjaras,[^9]\
carrying spears[^10] in their hands like guards and driving the well-laden oxen.\
The Malwa[^11] was the country that lay close to where Banda was camped,\
so its Singhs too came in quickly and presented themselves before Banda. (13)

ਔ ਜੋ ਸਿੰਘ ਮਧ ਮਾਝੈ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ॥ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਲੰਘਨ ਮਧ ਬਹੁਤ ਖਰਾਬੀ ॥\
ਬੰਧ ਜਥਾ ਵਲ ਪ੍ਰਬਤ ਸਿਧਾਏ ॥ ਬੇਲੋ ਓਲੋ ਪ੍ਰਬਤ ਤਕਾਏ ॥੧੪॥

But for the Punjabi Singhs in the heart of the Majha\
there was great danger and trouble in crossing through the lands the Turks held;\
so they gathered into a jatha[^12] and set out together toward the hills,\
keeping to the riverside forests and out of sight, making for the mountains. (14)

ਇਮ ਕਰ ਕੀਰਤਿਪੁਰ ਵਲ ਆਏ ॥ ਅਗੈਂ ਰੋਪੜੋਂ ਲੰਘਨ ਨ ਪਾਏ ॥\
ਊਹਾਂ ਡੇਰਾ ਦਯੋ ਲਗਾਈ ॥ ਸੋ ਬੰਦੇ ਪੈ ਖਬਰ ਪੁਚਾਈ ॥੧੫॥

In this way they came round through the hills toward Kiratpur,[^13]\
but beyond it they could not get past Ropar[^14] (for the Turks held the road there);\
so there they set up their camp and stayed,\
and sent word to Banda that they had come that far. (15)

ਬੰਦੇ ਕਹਯੋ ਰਹੁ ਡੇਰਾ ਲਾਇ ॥ ਹਮ ਆ ਨੇੜੈ ਲੇਹਿਂ ਬੁਲਾਇ ॥\
ਸੋਊ ਬਾਤ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਮੰਨ ਲਈ ॥ ਰਹਯੋ ਊਹਾਂ ਦਿਨ ਖਾਲਸੋ ਕਈ ॥੧੬॥

Banda sent back word to them: "Stay where you are, and keep your camp there;\
when we have come near to you, we will call you to join us."\
The Khalsa accepted his word and did as he said,\
and they waited there, camped in the hills, for many days. (16)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਅੰਮ੍ਰਿਤਸਰ ਥੋ ਚਲਤ ਲੌ ਔ ਬਹੈ ਜੁ ਬਿਚ ਪਹਾਰ ॥\
ਪਿਸ਼ੋਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਰੋੜਾ ਹੁਤੋ ਤਿਨ ਦੀਨੋ ਖਰਚ ਬਿਚਾਰ ॥੧੭॥

From the day they set out from Amritsar, and all the while they sat waiting in the hills,\
there was one Pishor Singh, an Aroria[^15] and a man of means, who thought it through with care and met every cost they had. (17)

### Notes

[^1]: Sehar Khand: the poet's Sehar Khanda, a village of the Bangar, the dry upland north of Delhi, now in Haryana. BSS: it lies about thirty kos (a kos is about 3 km) on the near side of Delhi.
[^2]: Sirhind: the Mughal city and province whose governor, Wazir Khan, had the Guru's two younger sons put to death.
[^3]: baṅdā: servant; it is also Banda's own name, and the line plays on it.
[^4]: ardās: the Sikh prayer of petition; here a written appeal. BSS calls these letters hukamnamas.
[^5]: Sahibzadas: the Guru's four sons. The two elder fell in battle at Chamkaur; the two younger were put to death at Sirhind.
[^6]: Thatta and Bhakkar: towns on the Indus in Sindh. Kulwant Singh places Bhakkar in Afghanistan.
[^7]: Majhail Singhs: the Singhs of the Majha, the country between the Beas and the Ravi around Amritsar.
[^8]: Turks: the poet's word for the Muslim rulers and their armies. BSS: Lahore, Kasur, Jalandhar and Sirhind all had Muslim governors, and the whole road was in their hands.
[^9]: Banjaras: the travelling carriers who moved grain and goods on pack oxen. BSS: the Singhs passed the guard posts by posing as guards of the Banjaras' goods.
[^10]: spears: the poet's barchhe.
[^11]: the Malwa: the country south of the Satluj.
[^12]: jatha: a band of Singhs under its own leader.
[^13]: Kiratpur: the poet's Kiratipur, a town on the Satluj near Anandpur, founded by Guru Hargobind. BSS: they came this way because it lay close to Sirhind, and in the end camped at Anandpur Sahib.
[^14]: Ropar: a town on the Satluj below Kiratpur. BSS: the Turks had set a guard post there and would not let them through.
[^15]: Aroria: the poet's Roṛā, the Arora trading caste of the western Punjab. BSS: he took it as a chance for seva and sent money and rations to every jatha camped there.

# Panth Prakash, Episode 36

## The Episode of the Slaughter at Sadhaura

### ('jār usai dayo rākh uḍāī'...: 'I shall burn him and scatter his ashes'...)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਯੌ ਜਬ ਬੰਦੈ ਚਿੱਤ ਬਿਚਾਰੀ ॥ ਬਜੀਰਾ ਲੁਟਾਯੈ ਕਰ ਫੌਜ ਸਾਰੀ ॥\
ਮਤ ਮਝੈਲ ਸਿੰਘ ਰਹੈਂ ਸੁ ਖਾਲੀ ॥ ਸੋ ਆਵੈਂਗੇ ਦੂਰੋਂ ਚਾਲੀ ॥੧॥

So Banda turned the matter over in his mind and reasoned it out:\
*We shall gather the whole army together first, and only then plunder Wazira,[^1]*\
*so that the Majhail Singhs[^2] are not left out of the spoils,*\
*for they are coming a long way to join us.* (1)

ਜਮਨ ਉਵਾਰ ਜੁ ਸ਼ਹਰ ਤੁਰਕਾਣੋ ॥ ਕੁੰਜ ਪੁਰ ਮਾਰੀਏ ਬੈਰ ਪੁਰਾਣੋ ॥\
ਪਹਿਲੋ ਮਾਰ ਉਨੈਂ ਕੋ ਲੀਜੈ ॥ ਤਉ ਲੌ ਮਝੈਲ ਰਲਾਇ ਸੁ ਲੀਜੈ ॥੨॥

*There is a town of the Turks[^3] on this side of the Jamuna,[^4]*\
*Kunjpura,[^5] an old enemy of ours; let us strike it down.*\
*Let us take that town and finish it first,*\
*and by the time that is done, the Majhails will have come in and joined us.* (2)

ਸ਼ਹਰ ਸਢੌਰੋ ਤਿਨ ਸੁਨ ਲਯੋ ॥ ਪੀਰ ਕਰਮਾਤੀ ਤਿਹ ਮਹਿ ਅਹਿਯੋ ॥\
ਉਸੈ ਪ੍ਰਛਾਵੈਂ ਛੁਹੈ ਨ ਆਗ ॥ ਕਹਯੋ ਬੰਦੈ ਉਸ ਦਯੋਂ ਮੈਂ ਦਾਗੁ ॥੩॥

Then Banda heard about the town of Sadhaura,[^6]\
where a miracle-working pir[^7] had once lived, and was honoured still,\
and people said that fire would not touch anything that passed beneath his shadow.\
Banda said, "I shall burn this pir on a pyre, the way the Hindus burn their dead. (3)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਆਗ ਲਾਗ ਐਸੋ ਜਰੇ ਜੈਸੇ ਰੂਈ ਜਰਾਇ ॥\
ਬਿਨ ਹੀ ਆਸਰ ਔਰ ਕੇ ਚਲੈ ਸਮਾਲੈ ਦਾਇ ॥੪॥

"Once the fire has caught him, he will burn as quickly as cotton catches and burns;\
with nothing else to feed the flame, the fire itself will run through him and take him. (4)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਜਾਰ ਉਸੈ ਦਯੋ ਰਾਖ ਉਡਾਈ ॥ ਤੌ ਜਾਨਯੋ ਹਮ ਕਲਾ ਸਵਾਈ ॥\
ਯੌ ਕਹਿ ਕੀਨੀ ਬੰਦੈ ਧਾਈ ॥ ਦੀਨੋਂ ਸੋਊ ਸ਼ਹਿਰ ਲੁਟਾਈ ॥੫॥

"When I have burned him and scattered his ashes to the wind,\
then you will all know that my power is greater than his."\
Saying this, Banda launched his attack upon Sadhaura\
and gave the whole town over to the Singhs to plunder. (5)

ਹਿੰਦੂਅਨ ਕੇ ਘਰ ਲੀਨੇ ਰਾਖ ॥ ਮੁਸਲਮਾਨ ਦਈ ਕਤਲੋ ਆਖ ॥\
ਬੋਦੀ ਵਾਲੈ ਨਾਂਹਿ ਸਤਾਯੋ ॥ ਬਿਨ ਬੋਦੀ ਦਿਖ ਮਾਰ ਗਵਾਯੋ ॥੬॥

Banda spared the houses of the Hindus and kept them safe,\
but he gave the order that every Muslim was to be put to the sword.[^8]\
No man who wore a bodī[^9] on his head was harmed in any way;\
but any man they saw without one, they cut down and killed. (6)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਜੋ ਹਿੰਦੂਅਨ ਘਰ ਜਾ ਛਿਪੇ ਤੇਊ ਲਏ ਉਬਾਰ ॥\
ਜੋ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕੇ ਹਥ ਚੜ੍ਹੇ ਸੋ ਉਨ ਦੀਨੇ ਮਾਰ ॥੭॥

Those Muslims who ran and hid in the houses of the Hindus were spared as well;\
but the Singhs put to death every one of them who fell into their hands. (7)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਪੀਰ ਤਬੂਤ ਹੁਤ ਪੌਰ ਟਿਕਾਯਾ ॥ ਤਿਸਕੇ ਹੇਠ ਥੋ ਰਾਹਿ ਚਲਾਯਾ ॥\
ਜੋ ਤਿਸਕੈ ਪਰਛਾਵੈਂ ਆਵੈ ॥ ਉਸਕੋ ਅਗਨੀ ਨਾਹਿ ਜਲਾਵੈ ॥੮॥

The coffin of the pir had been set up high above one of the gateways of the town,\
and a road ran beneath it (the road the Hindus took to carry their dead to the burning ground);\
it was said that any dead body carried through beneath its shadow\
would never catch fire, for the flames of the pyre would refuse to burn it. (8)

ਹਿੰਦੂ ਹੋਇ ਭਾਵੇਂ ਮੁਸਲਮਾਨ ॥ ਹੋਇ ਮਰੈ ਸੋ ਤੁਰਕ ਸਮਾਨ ॥\
ਸੋ ਦਰਵਾਜਯੋਂ ਲਯੋ ਉਤਾਰ ॥ ਕੱਢ ਸੰਦੂਖੋਂ ਵਹਿ ਦਯੋ ਜਾਰ ॥੯॥

Whether the dead man had been a Hindu or a Muslim,\
he went to his end like a Turk and had to be buried in the earth.[^10]\
So the Singhs took the pir's coffin down from the gateway,\
pulled the pir's body out of the box, and set fire to it. (9)

ਛੁਹੀ ਅਗਨ ਤਿਸ ਘ੍ਰਿਤ ਜਿਉਂ ਜਰਯੋ ॥ ਜਨਕ ਬਰੂਦਹਿ ਤੋੜਾ ਧਰਯੋ ॥\
ਭਈ ਰਾਖ ਉਸ ਛੁਹਤੇ ਆਗ ॥ ਭਈ ਖਬਰ ਯਹਿ ਮੱਕੇ ਤਾਗ ॥੧੦॥

The moment the fire touched him, he blazed up like ghee poured on a flame,\
as fast as gunpowder flares up when a burning match[^11] is set to it;\
the instant the flame reached him he was nothing but ash,\
and the news of it travelled all the way to Mecca. (10)

ਤਬ ਬੰਦੇ ਯੌ ਮੁਖੋਂ ਉਚਾਰਾ ॥ ਹਮ ਪੈ ਮੰਤ੍ਰ ਅਲੂਣਾ ਸਾਰਾ ॥\
ਇਸ ਮੈਂ ਮੰਤ੍ਰ ਆਹਿ ਚੌਥਾਈ ॥ ਯਾਂਤੇ ਹੋਤੀ ਅਗਨ ਬੁਝਾਈ ॥੧੧॥

Then Banda spoke out to the Singhs and said,\
"I hold the whole of Aluna's mantra,[^12] every word of it;\
this pir knew only a quarter of that same mantra,\
and with that much he was able to keep the fire from burning." (11)

*Sri Gur Panth Prakash, Rattan Singh Bhangu, 1841. Gurmukhi text of record from the edition of the Institute of Sikh Studies, Chandigarh, volume 1, episodes 1 to 81, with the English translation of Kulwant Singh. Punjabi commentary from the Prachin Panth Prakash Steek, part 1, of Singh Sahib Baba Santa Singh Akali, Jathedar of the 96 Krori Budha Dal. Kulwant Singh is named in full in the notes; Baba Santa Singh Akali is BSS. Cite as Panth Prakash 36.4.*

### Notes

[^1]: Wazira: the poet's scornful short form (also Bajira) for Wazir Khan, the nawab of Sirhind, who had the Guru's two younger sons put to death.
[^2]: Majhail Singhs: the Singhs of the Majha, the country around Amritsar, who were waiting near Kiratpur to come through (Episode 33).
[^3]: Turks: the poet's word for the Muslim rulers and their people.
[^4]: the Jamuna: the poet's Jaman.
[^5]: Kunjpura: a Muslim town near Karnal, on the Punjab side of the Jamuna. Kulwant Singh: the birthplace of Wazir Khan. BSS: an old enemy of the Singhs.
[^6]: Sadhaura: a town near Naraingarh, in the Ambala country. Pir Buddhu Shah of Sadhaura had fought for Guru Gobind Singh at Bhangani and was later put to death by the Mughals.
[^7]: pir: a Muslim holy man, a Sufi master.
[^8]: BSS: Hindus and Muslims lived in separate quarters; Banda kept the Singhs off the Hindu quarter and ordered a general slaughter of the Muslims, because they had had Pir Buddhu Shah, the Guru's follower, put to death.
[^9]: bodī: the tuft of hair a Hindu leaves uncut on the crown of the head; Muslims kept none.
[^10]: BSS: Muslims do not burn their dead, so a body the fire would not take had to be buried like a Turk's, and could not have the Hindu rites.
[^11]: match: the poet's toṛā, the slow-burning cord of a matchlock. BSS reads it as a flask of gunpowder.
[^12]: Aluna: the adept (sidh) whose book of powers Banda kept (Episode 28). Kulwant Singh: a disciple of Gorakhnath. BSS: Banda had the whole mantra in his book, and by heart, for kindling fire and putting it out.

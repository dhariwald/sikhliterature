# Panth Prakash, Episode 37

## The Story of the Killing of Wazira

### (banūṛ val chaṛhāī; vazīr khāṅ dā badh: The march on Banur; the killing of Wazir Khan)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਭਈ ਧੂਮ ਯਹ ਜਗਤ ਮੇਂ ਭਯੋ ਹਿੰਦੂ ਅਵਤਾਰ ॥\
ਨਿਹਕਲੰਕ ਯਹਿ ਹੀ ਭਯੋ ਕਰੈ ਜੁ ਮੁਸਲੈ ਮਾਰ ॥੧॥

Banda's fame spread through the whole world, and men cried out, "An avatar of the Hindus has come down to earth;\
this is Nihkalank[^1] himself, born to strike the Musle[^2] down." (1)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਕੋਊ ਕਹੈ ਯਹਿ ਹੈ ਜਾਦੂ ਕਰ ॥ ਕੋਊ ਕਹੈ ਯਹਿ ਹੈ ਰਸਾਇਨਗਰ ॥\
ਕੋਊ ਕਹੈ ਯਹਿ ਹੈ ਹਥ ਬੀਰ ॥ ਕੋਊ ਕਹੈ ਯਹਿ ਸਿਖ ਗੁਰ ਧੀਰ ॥੨॥

Some people said, "He is a sorcerer, a worker of magic spells";\
some said, "He is an alchemist,[^3] who can turn base metal into gold";\
some said, "He has mighty spirits[^4] at his command, always ready at his hand";\
and some said, "He is the Guru's own steadfast Sikh, faithful to the Guru." (2)

ਖੂਨੀ ਬਜੀਰੇ ਦੇ ਦਿਲ ਆਈ ॥ ਹੈ ਹੋਣੀ ਯਹਿ ਹਮ ਸਿਰ ਆਈ ॥\
ਤਬਹਿ ਬਜੀਰੈ ਚਿੰਤਾ ਪਈ ॥ ਹਮ ਕੋ ਬੰਦਾ ਛਾਡਤ ਨਹੀਂ ॥੩॥

Then a thought came into the heart of Wazira,[^5] the murderer (of the Sahibzadas):\
*This is my fate, my doom that has come down upon my head.*\
Then dread and worry fell upon Wazira:\
*Banda is never going to let me go.* (3)

ਇਸ ਕੈ ਪਾਸ ਅਹੈਂ ਦੋਊ ਬੀਰ ॥ ਔਰ ਪੰਚ ਗੁਰ ਦੀਨੇ ਤੀਰ ॥\
ਪ੍ਰਿਥਮੈ ਬੰਦਾ ਕਾਲਾ ਨਾਗ ॥ ਤੀਰ ਭਏ ਤਿਸ ਫੰਘੇ ਲਾਗ ॥੪॥

*He has two mighty spirits at his side, ready to do whatever he commands,*\
*and on top of that he has the five arrows[^6] the Guru gave him;*\
*Banda was a deadly black cobra to begin with,*\
*and now the Guru's arrows have given that cobra wings to fly.* (4)

ਇਸ ਪਰਛਾਵੈਂ ਪਰੈ ਨ ਧਰਨ ॥ ਕਹੈਂ ਧਰਨ ਉਸ ਲਗੈ ਨ ਚਰਨ ॥\
ਰਿੱਧਿ ਸਿੱਧਿ ਮੈਂ ਹੈ ਸੋ ਪੂਰੋ ॥ ਬਾਨ ਬਾਹਿ ਯਹਿ ਹੈ ਅਤਿ ਸੂਰੋ ॥੫॥

*They say his body casts no shadow upon the earth,*\
*and that his feet never touch the ground when he walks.*\
*He is full of every kind of wealth and power, and he lacks nothing,*\
*and in battle he is a mighty hero at loosing arrows from the bow.* (5)

ਉਤਰੈ ਚਢੈ ਗਟੀ ਮਨ ਮਾਂਹੀ ॥ ਚਪੇ ਚੋਰ ਜਿਮ ਢੀਠ ਕਰਾਹੀ ॥\
ਸੋ ਹਮਕੋ ਅਬ ਛਾਡਤ ਨਾਹੀ ॥ ਨਠ ਕਰ ਬਚਨ ਹਮ ਜਗਾ ਨ ਪਾਹੀ ॥੬॥

His heart rose and sank within him, now hoping, now despairing,[^7]\
like a thief caught fast in the hole he has broken through a wall, groaning, yet still trying to brazen it out.\
*Banda will not let me go now,*\
*and if I run, there is no place left where I can save myself.* (6)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਜੌ ਬੰਦਯੋਂ ਹਮ ਭਜ ਬਚੈਂ ਪਤਸ਼ਾਹੈ ਤੇ ਕਹਿਂ ਜਾਉਂ ॥\
ਬਾਈ ਸੂਬਨ ਮੈਂ ਕਹੂੰ ਲੁਕਨ ਨ ਮੋਕਉ ਥਾਉਂ ॥੭॥

*And even if I run from Banda and save my life, where can I go to escape the Patshah?*\
*In all the twenty-two provinces[^8] of Hindustan there is not one place left where I can hide.* (7)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਤੌ ਵਹਿ ਪਾਪੀ ਮਨ ਪਛੁਤਾਵੈ ॥ ਬੀਤਯੋ ਵਖਤ ਹਾਥ ਨਹਿਂ ਆਵੈ ॥\
ਮੈਂ ਭੁਲ ਗਯੋ ਕਹੇ ਸੁਚਿ ਨੰਦ ॥ ਭਏ ਭਾਗ ਤਬ ਮੇਰੇ ਮੰਦ ॥੮॥

Then that sinner repented in his heart of what he had done,\
but the time that is gone never comes back into a man's hand.\
*I was led astray when I listened to what Suchi Nand[^9] told me,*\
*and my luck turned evil on the day I did as he said.* (8)

ਤਬ ਇਕ ਛਲੀਯੇ ਬਜੀਰੇ ਕਹਯੋ ॥ ਛਲ ਕਰ ਮਾਰੋਂ ਬੰਦੈ ਅਹਯੋ ॥\
ਹਜਾਰ ਸਿਪਾਹੀ ਮੈਂ ਲੈ ਜਾਊਂ ॥ ਸੋ ਬੰਦੇ ਕੇ ਸਾਥ ਰਲਾਊਂ ॥੯॥

Then a trickster, a man skilled in deceit, came to Wazira and said,\
"I know how to kill Banda for you; I shall do it by a trick.\
I shall take a thousand of your soldiers with me\
and join them to Banda's army, as if we had come to fight on his side. (9)

ਜਬ ਆਇ ਪਰ ਹੈ ਗਾਢ ਲਰਾਈ ॥ ਦਿਹੋਂ ਲੂਟ ਮੈਂ ਬਿਚੇ ਮਚਾਈ ॥\
ਫਿਰ ਬੰਦੈ ਫੜ ਲੈਗੁ ਫਕੀਰ ॥ ਤੌ ਤੁਮ ਤੇ ਵਡ ਲਯੋਂਗੁ ਜਗੀਰ ॥੧੦॥

"Then, when the battle is at its fiercest and the fighting at its thickest,\
I shall start a scramble for loot right in the middle of his army;\
and in the confusion that faqir,[^10] Banda, will be seized,\
and for that I shall have a great estate from you as my reward." (10)

ਵਹੀ ਬਜੀਰੈ ਲੀਨੀ ਮਾਨ ॥ ਦਰਬ ਖਰਚ ਕੌ ਦੀਨੀ ਆਨ ॥\
ਬੰਦੇ ਮਾਰ ਆਵੇਂ ਮੁਹਿ ਪਾਹਿ ॥ ਦੇਉਂਗ ਬਡੀ ਜਗੀਰ ਦੁਆਇ ॥੧੧॥

Wazira, in his desperation, agreed to that very plan\
and brought out money to pay for it all:\
"Kill Banda and come back to me," he said,\
"and I shall see to it that you are given a great estate."[^11] (11)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਸੋ ਭਜ ਬੰਦੈ ਆ ਮਿਲਯੋ ਕਹੈ ਆਯੋ ਹਿੰਦੂ ਮੈਂ ਜਾਣ ॥\
ਰੰਘੜ ਬੇਈਮਾਨ ਥੋ ਸੋ ਉਨ ਲਯੋ ਪਛਾਣ ॥੧੨॥

So the trickster came hurrying to join Banda, saying, "I have come to you as a Hindu, to help my Hindu brothers";\
but he was a faithless Ranghar,[^12] a Muslim Rajput, and Banda saw through him. (12)

ਤਬ ਬੰਦੈ ਉਸਕੋ ਕਹਯੋ ਤੁਮ ਦਿਲ ਸਾਬਤ ਨਾਹਿ ॥\
ਜੋ ਤੂੰ ਕਰੈਂ ਸੁ ਪਾਇ ਹੈਂ ਹਮ ਤੁਮ ਫੇਰਤ ਨਾਹਿਂ ॥੧੩॥

Then Banda said to him, "Your heart is not true to us, and I can see it;\
but whatever you do, you will reap the reward of it yourself, so we will not turn you away." (13)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਤਬ ਬੰਦੇ ਖੀਸੇ ਹਥ ਪਾਯੋ ॥ ਕਈ ਮੁਠੀ ਦਈ ਮੁਹਰ ਫੜਾਯੋ ॥\
ਲੈ ਜੇਤੀ ਤੁਹਿ ਸਾਥ ਸਿਪਾਹ ॥ ਲੂਣ ਹਮਾਰੋ ਤੂ ਪੇਟ ਪਾਇ ॥੧੪॥

Then Banda put his hand into his pocket\
and gave him gold coins,[^13] fistful after fistful, to keep his men:\
"Take as much as you need for every soldier you have brought with you,\
and eat our salt; let our salt go into your belly. (14)

ਲੂਣ ਖਾਇ ਜੋ ਕਰੇ ਬੁਰਾਈ ॥ ਮਰੈ ਤੁਰਤ ਸੋ ਬਿਨ ਹੀ ਆਈ ॥\
ਤਬ ਬੰਦੈ ਨੇ ਕੂਚ ਕਰਾਯਾ ॥ ਮਾਰ ਬਨੂੜ ਆ ਡੇਰਾ ਲਾਇਆ ॥੧੫॥

"Whoever eats another man's salt, living on his bread and his pay, and then turns to do him harm,\
dies at once, before his time has come, for his treachery."\
Then Banda gave the order to break camp and march on from Sadhaura,\
and he sacked the town of Banur[^14] and pitched his camp there. (15)

ਹਿੰਦੂ ਭੀ ਉਸ ਲਏ ਬਚਾਇ ॥ ਮੁਸਲਮਾਨ ਚੁਨ ਦੀਨੇ ਘਾਇ ॥\
ਸਿੰਘ ਮਝੈਲਨ ਲਯੋ ਬੁਲਾਇ ॥ ਕਹਿਓ ਆਓ ਪਹਾੜਨ ਦਾਇ ॥੧੬॥

At Banur too, Banda spared the Hindus and kept them safe,\
but he picked out the Muslims one by one and put them to death.\
Then he sent for the Majhail Singhs[^15] who were waiting near Kiratpur,\
telling them, "Come to us now, and come by way of the hills." (16)

### Notes

[^1]: Nihkalank: the Spotless One, the avatar of Vishnu who is to come at the end of the Kaliyuga riding a white horse and destroy the wicked; also called Kalki.
[^2]: Musle: a scornful word for Muslims.
[^3]: alchemist: the poet's rasāingar. BSS: they said he turned iron and copper into gold.
[^4]: spirits: the poet's bīr, spirit servants. BSS: spirits strong enough to turn a city upside down, held in his power.
[^5]: Wazira: the poet's scornful short form (also Bajira) for Wazir Khan, the nawab of Sirhind. BSS: he said to himself that Banda was no Nihkalank and no magician, but his own death come for him.
[^6]: arrows: the poet's tīr (and bān at verse 5); the five arrows Guru Gobind Singh gave Banda when he sent him to the Punjab (Episode 29).
[^7]: The first half of the line is obscure; the reading follows BSS, who says Wazira lay awake day and night, one thought rising and another falling, like a thief caught in the hole he has broken through a wall, looking for some trick to get out.
[^8]: twenty-two provinces: BSS: Hindustan then had twenty-two provinces, and none would give him refuge; and the Patshah would ask why he had fled his post.
[^9]: Suchi Nand: usually called Sucha Nand, the Hindu official at Wazir Khan's court who, in Sikh tradition, urged the death of the younger Sahibzadas, saying the young of a snake are snakes. BSS: the Khalsa calls him Jhutha Nand, False Nand.
[^10]: faqir: a holy beggar, a wandering ascetic; to his enemies Banda was no more than one.
[^11]: BSS: Wazira was so afraid of Banda that he did not ask for him alive; he did not want to set eyes on him. He promised to speak for the trickster to the Patshah.
[^12]: Ranghar: the Muslim Rajputs of the country around Delhi and the Bangar. BSS: he passed himself off as a Hindu, come to help his Hindu brothers.
[^13]: gold coins: the poet's muhar (mohar).
[^14]: Banur: a town south of present-day Chandigarh, on the road to Rajpura, with the village of Chhat close by.
[^15]: Majhail Singhs: the Singhs of the Majha, waiting near Kiratpur and Anandpur (Episode 33).

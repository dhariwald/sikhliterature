# Panth Prakash, Episode 85

## Moman Khan's Campaign

### (jitnak būṅd siṅghan kī parai, titnak siṅgh phir haiṅ kharai: As many drops of the Singhs' blood as fall, so many Singhs stand up again.)

>**Questions**
>
>**Define (1 line each):** Moman Khan (ਮੋਮਨ ਖਾਂ), qazi (ਕਾਜੀ), the Qazi's saying about the Singhs' blood (ਜਿਤਨਕ ਬੂੰਦ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕੀ ਪਰੈ, ਤਿਤਨਕ ਸਿੰਘ ਫਿਰ ਹੋਤ ਹੈਂ ਖਰੈ), the Singhs of Lahore (ਸਿੰਘਨ ਲਹੌਰੀਅਨ), Kal (ਕਾਲ, Time and Death)
>
>**Summarize (2–3 sentences each):**
>
>1. How the Nawab's court took the news from Patti.
>2. The warning that reached Tara Singh, and his answer.

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਕਰ ਸਲਾਹ ਇਕੱਠੇ ਭਏ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਕੀ ਕਰ ਆਸ ॥\
ਪੜ੍ਹੈਂ ਬਾਣੀਆਂ ਗੁਰੂ ਕੀ ਇੱਕਣ ਹੁਇ ਕਰ ਰਾਸ ॥੧॥

After making a resolve, the Sikhs assembled (at Tara Singh's bunga), placing all their hope in the Satguru;\
they began reciting the sacred hymns of the Guru, so that they might succeed in their cherished mission.[^1] (1)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਅਬ ਫਿਰ ਬਾਤ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਵਲ ਆਈ ॥ ਸੁਨੋ ਸੰਤ ਅਬ ਗੁਰਮੁਖ ਭਾਈ ॥\
ਪੱਟੀ ਵਾਲ ਜਬ ਲਹੌਰੇ ਗਯੋ ॥ ਹਵਾਲ ਆਪਨੋ ਨਿਬਾਬ ਦਿਖਯੋ ॥੨॥

Now let us look at the scene in the camp of the Turks;[^2]\
listen to their account, my dear Gursikh readers.\
When the man of Patti[^3] went to the authorities at Lahore,\
he laid before the Nawab the account of his shameful defeat. (2)

ਦੇਖ ਨਿਬਾਬਹਿ ਲਗ ਗਈ ਆਗ ॥ ਸੁੱਤੋ ਸ਼ੇਰ ਜਨ ਉਠਿਓ ਜਾਗ ॥\
ਜਿਮ ਜਿਮ ਨਾਮ ਵੈ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਸੁਨਾਵੈ ॥ ਜਨ ਆਤਸ ਪਰ ਵੈ ਬਰੂਦ ਪਾਵੈ ॥੩॥

Seeing this, the Nawab of Lahore caught fire with rage,\
like a sleeping lion woken from its sleep.\
The more the man of Patti read out the names of the Singhs,\
the more it was like pouring gunpowder on a fire. (3)

ਹਮ ਤੌ ਸਿੰਘ ਛਡਯੋ ਕਿਤ ਨਾਹੀ ॥ ਅਬ ਕਿਤ ਉਠ ਖੜੈ ਹੋਹਿ ਬਲਾਈ ॥\
ਹਮ ਮਾਰਤ ਮਾਰਤ ਥਕ ਗਏ ॥ ਵੈ ਅਗਯੋਂ ਬਹੁ ਬਧਤੇ ਭਏ ॥੪॥

He had hounded and wiped out every Singh, he said, and left none anywhere;\
he was baffled how these plagues had sprung up again.\
The more they had worn themselves out killing the Singhs,\
the more had the Singhs multiplied in their strength. (4)

ਤੌ ਲਾਂਭੇ ਕਾਜੀ ਨੇ ਕਹੀ ॥ ਇਨ ਕੀ ਖਬਰ ਤੁਮੈਂ ਕਛੁ ਨਹੀਂ ॥\
ਜਿਤਨਕ ਬੂੰਦ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕੀ ਪਰੈ ॥ ਤਿਤਨਕ ਸਿੰਘ ਫਿਰ ਹੋਤ ਹੈਂ ਖਰੈ ॥੫॥

At this the Qazi, taking the Nawab aside, remarked\
that he knew nothing of the mystery of the Singhs' rising again.\
He said that as many drops of the Singhs' blood as fell on the ground,\
so many Singhs rose from the ground once again.[^4] (5)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਫੜ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਈਹਾਂ ਮੰਗਾਈਐ ਦਈਐ ਮੁੰਗਰਨ ਸੋਂ ਕੁਟਵਾਇ ॥\
ਰਕਤ ਨ ਨਿਕਸਨ ਉਨ ਪਵੈ ਫਿਰ ਹੁਵੈ ਨ ਸਿੰਘ ਵਧਾਇ ॥੬॥

He advised the Nawab that the Singhs be captured alive and brought here, and their bodies beaten and crushed with wooden mallets;[^5]\
not a drop of blood should be allowed to spill from their bodies, for then alone could the growth of their strength be checked. (6)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਤੌ ਇਕ ਸਿਪਾਹੀ ਐਸੇ ਕਹੀ ॥ ਕਾਜੀ ਬਾਤ ਕਰਤ ਹੈ ਸਹੀ ॥\
ਇਸਕੋ ਮੁਹਰੈ ਦੇਉ ਲਗਾਇ ॥ ਲਯਾਵਗੁ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਯਹਿ ਫੜਵਾਇ ॥੭॥

Then, at this suggestion of the Qazi, a soldier broke in\
that the revered Qazi was really speaking the truth;\
for this (holy) cause the Qazi should be made to lead the troops,\
so that he could have the Singhs caught and bring them in.[^6] (7)

ਇਨਹਿ ਨ ਦੇਖੇ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਹਾਥ ॥ ਆਇ ਮਿਲਗੁ ਯਹਿ ਜੋਰੂ ਸਾਥ ॥\
ਜਨ ਕਾਜੀ ਸਿਰ ਪਾਣੀ ਪਇਆ ॥ ਬੋਲਨ ਜੋਗ ਨ ਕਾਜੀ ਰਹਿਆ ॥੮॥

He had never felt the hands of the mighty Singhs;\
he would never come home to his wife after meeting them.[^7]\
At this remark it was as if water had been poured over the Qazi's head,\
and he could not speak a single word in his defence. (8)

ਤਬ ਨਿਬਾਬ ਫਿਰ ਐਸ ਸੁਨਾਈ ॥ ਮਾਰੋ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕੋ ਅਬ ਜਾਈ ॥\
ਹੈ ਕੁਈ ਬਡੋ ਬਹਾਦਰ ਮੇਰਾ ॥ ਮਾਰੇ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕੋ ਕਰ ਘੇਰਾ ॥੯॥

Then the Nawab of Lahore made his pronouncement,\
that they must now go and crush the Singhs.\
He asked if there was any brave warrior among his troops\
who could crush the Singhs by laying siege to them. (9)

ਦੀਨ ਮਜ੍ਹਬ ਕੇ ਬਦਲੇ ਲੇਵੈ ॥ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਲੁਕਣ ਨ ਨੱਠਣੇ ਦੇਵੈ ॥\
ਕਰ ਮਾਰੋ ਹਿੰਦੂਅਨ ਕਸਮ ਹਜਾਰ ॥ ਹੈ ਲਿਖਯੋ ਰਸੂਲ ਹਮੈ ਯੌ ਕਾਰ ॥੧੦॥

Was there anyone who would take revenge for the faith,[^8]\
who would not let the Singhs escape and take refuge?\
The Hindus must be killed, even if one broke a thousand vows;\
such had been the command of the Prophet. (10)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਘਲਯੋ ਮੋਮਨ ਖਾਂ ਫੌਜਦਾਰ ਕੈ ਸੰਗ ਬਾਈ ਸੈ ਅਸਵਾਰ ॥\
ਚਾਲੀ ਜੰਬੂਰੇ ਫੀਲ ਪੰਜ ਔਰ ਰਹਿਕਲੇ ਚਾਰ ॥੧੧॥

Thus Moman Khan, the commander, was sent on this crusade, at the head of twenty-two hundred horsemen,\
with forty swivel guns and five elephants, as well as four field guns.[^9] (11)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਭਈ ਤਿਆਰੀ ਨਕੀਬ ਫਿਰ ਆਯੋ ॥ ਡੇਰਾ ਲਹੌਰਹ ਬਾਹਰ ਪਾਯੋ ॥\
ਸਿੰਘਨ ਲਹੌਰੀਅਨ ਕਿਤ ਸੁਨ ਲਈ ॥ ਤਾਰੈ ਸਿੰਘ ਪੈ ਚੜ੍ਹਤੀ ਭਈ ॥੧੨॥

As the herald[^10] announced the launch of this expedition,\
the troops set up camp on the outskirts of Lahore.\
The Singhs of Lahore came to hear of it somehow,\
and understood that the campaign was launched against Tara Singh.[^11] (12)

ਉਨ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕਿਮ ਦਿਵਸ ਬਿਤਾਯਾ ॥ ਰਾਤ ਪਈ ਕਰਨ ਖਬਰੈ ਧਾਯਾ ॥\
ਪੁਜਯੋ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਪੈ ਫਤੈ ਬੁਲਾਈ ॥ ਕਹੀ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਸੋਂ ਫੌਜ ਤੁਰਕ ਆਈ ॥੧੩॥

These Singhs somehow got through the day in great distress,\
but as darkness fell, one of them rode off to warn (Tara Singh).\
Reaching Tara Singh's band, he greeted them with the Fateh[^12]\
and told them that a force of Turks was coming against them. (13)

ਮੈਂ ਤੌ ਅਬ ਮੁੜ ਪਵੌਂ ਲਹੌਰ ॥ ਔਰ ਬਚਨ ਕੀ ਮੁਹਿ ਨਹਿਂ ਠੌਰ ॥\
ਪੰਦ੍ਰਾਂ ਕੋਸ ਮੋਰੀ ਘੋੜੀ ਆਈ ॥ ਬਚੌਂ ਤਉ ਮੁੜ ਲਹੌਰੈ ਜਾਈ ॥੧੪॥

He told them further that he had to return to Lahore at once,\
since he had no other refuge but Lahore.\
His mare had covered a distance of fifteen kos,[^13]\
and he must be back in Lahore (before daybreak) if he was to survive. (14)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਔਰ ਬਾਤ ਤੁਮ ਨਹਿਂ ਬਚੋ ਕਿਤ ਲੁਕ ਝਲ ਜਾਨ ਬਚਾਇ ॥\
ਰਾਤ ਰਾਤ ਤੁਮਰੀ ਅਹੈ ਦਿਨ ਤੁਰਕ ਪਰੈਂਗੇ ਆਇ ॥੧੫॥

He advised Tara Singh and his companions that they had no way to survive but to hide themselves somewhere in the bush;\
they had only this one night for it, for the Turks would be upon them with the daybreak. (15)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਯੋ ਕਹਿ ਕੇ ਵਹਿ ਤੌ ਮੁੜ ਗਯੋ ॥ ਸੁਨੋਂ ਅਗੈ ਜੋ ਤਾਰੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਕਹਯੋ ॥\
ਮੁਖ ਤਾਰੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਲਾਲੀ ਆਈ ॥ ਹਮ ਆਪ ਚਹੈਂ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਸਿਰ ਲਾਈ ॥੧੬॥

The messenger turned back after giving this warning;\
but (dear readers), listen to how Tara Singh answered.\
Tara Singh's face flushed red, and he said\
that he himself longed to spend his life against the Turks. (16)

ਹਮ ਤੌ ਲਰ ਮਰਿ ਹੈਂ ਯਹਿ ਠਾਇ ॥ ਨਹਿਂ ਨਠਿ ਕੈ ਕਿਤ ਔਰਹਿ ਜਾਇ ॥\
ਔ ਨਹਿਂ ਲੇਵੈਂ ਕਿਤ ਝਲ ਓਟ ॥ ਸਹੈਂ ਸੀਸ ਅਬ ਕਾਲਹਿ ਚੋਟ ॥੧੭॥

He would fight to the finish at his own camp;\
he would not desert his place to find escape elsewhere.\
Instead of seeking refuge in the thick bush,\
he would take the blow of death on his own head. (17)

ਅਬ ਤੌ ਨਠ ਕਰ ਕਿਤ ਲੁਕ ਪਾਹਿਂ ॥ ਜਹਾਂ ਕਾਲ ਤੇ ਢੂੰਡੇ ਨ ਜਾਹਿਂ ॥\
ਜਹਿਂ ਜਹਿਂ ਜਈਐ ਤਹੀਂ ਤਹਿਂ ਕਾਲ ॥ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਦੀਨੈ ਬਚਨ ਦਿਖਾਲ ॥੧੮॥

Where could one now run and find shelter,\
where death could not track one down?\
Wherever one goes, death is there,\
as the revered Satguru's words have shown.[^14] (18)

ਸ੍ਵੈਯਾ *Savaiya*

> *Sri Dasam Granth, Bachittar Natak, Chapter 1, Savaiya 96.*
>
> ਜੌ ਕਹੂੰ ਕਾਲ ਤੇ ਭਾਜ ਕੈ ਬਾਚੀਯਤ ਤੌ ਕਿਹ ਕੁੰਟ ਕਹੋ ਭਜ ਜੈਯੈ ॥\
> *jau kahūṅ kāl te bhāj kai bāchīyat tau kih kuṅṭ kaho bhaj jaiyai*\
> If one could escape Kal[^15] by taking flight, then tell me, in which direction should one flee?
>
> ਆਗੈ ਹੂੰ ਕਾਲ ਧਰੇ ਅਸਿ ਗਾਜਤ ਛਾਜਤ ਹੈ ਜਿਹ ਤੇ ਨਸਿ ਐਯੈ ॥\
> *āgai hūṅ kāl dhare asi gājat chhājat hai jih te nas aiyai*\
> Wherever one goes, Kal stands ahead, roaring, sword in hand; how can one run back from him?
>
> ਐਸੋ ਨ ਕੈ ਗਯੋ ਕੋਈ ਸੁ ਦਾਵ ਰੇ ਜਾਂਹਿ ਉਪਾਵ ਸੋ ਘਾਵ ਬਚੈਯੈ ॥\
> *aiso na kai gayo koī su dāv re jāṅhi upāv so ghāv bachaiyai*\
> No one has ever devised a trick by which one could escape his fatal blow.
>
> ਜਾਂਤੇ ਨ ਛੁਟੀਐ ਮੂੜ੍ਹ ਕਹੂੰ ਹਸ ਤਾਂਕੀ ਨ ਕਯੋਂ ਸਰਣਾਗਤ ਜੈਯੈ ॥\
> *jāṅte na chhuṭīai mūṛh kahūṅ has tāṅkī na kayoṅ saraṇāgat jaiyai*\
> Since there is no escaping him, you fool, why not go gladly, smiling, to take refuge in him?[^16]

### Notes

[^1]: bunga: a fortified house of the Singhs. BSS: they settled a gurmata and gathered, read Gurbani and made ardās that the work they had begun would succeed and that they would be granted martyrdom.
[^2]: Turks: the poet's word for the Muslim rulers and their armies.
[^3]: Patti: a town of the Majha, south of Amritsar, seat of the Mughal ruler of the district (Episode 84). BSS: his brother and nephews had been killed by the Singhs, and he laid their bodies before the court at Lahore.
[^4]: BSS reads ਲਾਂਭੇ as standing nearby in the court. He says the Qazi was boasting and carrying tales, since the qazis were glad the more the Singhs were made to suffer, and the Singhs mocked them in turn. He gives the Singhs' saying: Mannu is our sickle and we are his crop; the more he cuts us, the more we grow (Mir Mannu, governor of Lahore 1748 to 1753).
[^5]: ਮੁੰਗਰ, mallets. BSS: beaten until their bones were crushed to pieces.
[^6]: BSS: the soldier spoke in mockery; let the Qazi walk in front and seize the Singhs in his arms, and the soldiers would put on the handcuffs.
[^7]: ਆਇ ਮਿਲਗੁ ਯਹਿ ਜੋਰੂ ਸਾਥ, read as Kulwant Singh reads it. BSS: so far he has only held his wife in his arms; once he grapples with the Singhs he will find out.
[^8]: dīn and mazhab, faith and religion · Prophet: rasūl. BSS reads the next lines as killing the Hindus by swearing false oaths to them.
[^9]: commander: fujdār · swivel guns: ਜੰਬੂਰੇ; BSS: small cannon · field guns: ਰਹਿਕਲੇ, light guns on carts; BSS: long-barrelled guns. BSS: many foot soldiers were sent as well, and the court knew the Singhs were only twenty-two.
[^10]: naqīb. BSS: the herald who goes before an army or a nawab, calling out orders and warnings so that people stand and show respect.
[^11]: BSS: Singhs who carried on their business in the city of Lahore.
[^12]: Fateh: the Khalsa greeting, Vahiguru ji ka Khalsa, Vahiguru ji ki Fateh.
[^13]: kos: about 3 km, so fifteen kos is about 45 km · mare: ਘੋੜੀ. BSS: he had to be in Lahore before daylight, so that no one would learn he had warned Tara Singh.
[^14]: The words are the Savaiya that follows, printed in the source with no verse number.
[^15]: Kal (ਕਾਲ): Time and Death, Akal Purakh's name in the Bachittar Natak · sword: asi.
[^16]: BSS: why not take refuge, laughing and glad, in Sri Kal Ji. Kulwant Singh reads the line as smilingly submitting to death.

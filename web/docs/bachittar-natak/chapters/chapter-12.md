# Bachittar Natak, Chapter 12

## The Battle of Jujhar Singh

>**Questions**

ਚੌਪਈ ॥ *Chaupai*

ਜੁੱਧ ਭਯੋ ਇਹ ਭਾਂਤਿ ਅਪਾਰਾ ॥ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਕੋ ਮਾਰਿਓ ਸਿਰਦਾਰਾ ॥\
ਰਿਸ ਤਨ ਖਾਨ ਦਿਲਾਵਰ ਤਏ ॥ ਇਤੈ ਸਊਰ ਪਠਾਵਤ ਭਏ ॥੧॥

So a boundless battle was fought,\
and the leader of the Turks[^1] was killed;\
Dilawar Khan burned with fury\
and sent horsemen[^2] this way. (1)

ਉਤੈ ਪਠਿਓ ਉਨ ਸਿੰਘ ਜੁਝਾਰਾ ॥ ਤਿਹ ਭਲਾਨ ਤੇ ਖੇਦ ਨਿਕਾਰਾ ॥\
ਇਤਿ ਗਜ ਸਿੰਘ ਪੰਮਾ ਦਲ ਜੋਰਾ ॥ ਧਾਇ ਪਰੇ ਤਿਨ ਊਪਰ ਭੋਰਾ ॥੨॥

From over there they sent Jujhar Singh,[^3]\
and he drove them out of Bhalan;[^4]\
on this side Gaj Singh and Pamma[^5] gathered an army\
and fell on them at dawn. (2)

ਉਤੈ ਜੁਝਾਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਭਯੋ ਆਡਾ ॥ ਜਿਮ ਰਨ ਖੰਭ ਭੂਮਿ ਰਨਿ ਗਾਡਾ ॥\
ਗਾਡਾ ਚਲੇ ਨ ਹਾਡਾ ਚਲਿ ਹੈ ॥ ਸਾਮੁਹਿ ਸੇਲ ਸਮਰ ਮੋ ਝਲਿ ਹੈ ॥੩॥

Over there Jujhar Singh stood in their way\
like a pillar of war driven into the battleground;\
a post sunk in the ground might move, but the Hada[^6] would not;\
he took the lances[^7] full on in the fight. (3)

ਬਾਟ ਚੜ੍ਹੈ ਦਲ ਦੋਊ ਜੁਝਾਰਾ ॥ ਉਤੈ ਚੰਦੇਲ ਇਤੈ ਜਸਵਾਰਾ ॥\
ਮੰਡਿਓ ਬੀਰ ਖੇਤ ਮੋ ਜੁੱਧਾ ॥ ਉਪਜਿਉ ਸਮਰਸੂਰ ਮਨ ਕ੍ਰੁੱਧਾ ॥੪॥

Both armies of fighters came on in their divisions,[^8]\
the Chandel over there, the Jaswal on this side;[^9]\
the heroes joined battle on the field,\
and fury rose in the hearts of the warriors. (4)

ਕੋਪ ਭਰੇ ਦੋਊ ਦਿਸ ਭਟ ਭਾਰੇ ॥ ਇਤੈ ਚੰਦੇਲ ਉਤੈ ਜਸਵਾਰੇ ॥\
ਢੋਲ ਨਗਾਰੇ ਬਜੇ ਅਪਾਰਾ ॥ ਭੀਮ ਰੂਪ ਭੈਰੋ ਭਭਕਾਰਾ ॥੫॥

Great warriors on both sides were filled with fury,\
the Chandels on this side, the Jaswals over there;\
drums and war drums sounded without end,\
and Bhairav[^10] in his terrible form roared. (5)

ਰਸਾਵਲ ਛੰਦ ॥ *Rasaval Chhand*

ਧੁਣੰ ਢੋਲ ਬੱਜੇ ॥ ਮਹਾਂ ਸੂਰ ਗੱਜੇ ॥\
ਕਰੇ ਸਸਤ੍ਰ ਘਾਵੰ ॥ ਚੜ੍ਹੇ ਚਿੱਤ ਚਾਵੰ ॥੬॥

The drums boomed,\
great warriors roared;\
they struck with their weapons,\
their hearts eager for the fight. (6)

ਨ੍ਰਿਭੈ ਬਾਜ ਡਾਰੈ ॥ ਪਰੱਘੈ ਪ੍ਰਹਾਰੈ ॥\
ਕਰੇ ਤੇਗ ਘਾਯੰ ॥ ਚੜ੍ਹੇ ਚਿੱਤ ਚਾਯੰ ॥੭॥

Fearless, they drove their horses on\
and swung their iron clubs;[^11]\
they struck with the tegh,\
their hearts eager for the fight. (7)

ਬਕੈ ਮਾਰ ਮਾਰੰ ॥ ਨ ਸੰਕਾ ਬਿਚਾਰੰ ॥\
ਰੁਲੈ ਤੱਛ ਮੁੱਛੰ ॥ ਕਰੈ ਸੁਰਗ ਇੱਛੰ ॥੮॥

They cried "Kill, kill,"\
and gave no thought to fear;\
they lay hacked to pieces,\
longing for heaven. (8)

ਦੋਹਰਾ ॥ *Dohra*

ਨੈਕ ਨ ਰਨ ਤੇ ਮੁਰਿ ਚਲੈਂ ਕਰੈ ਨਿਡਰ ਹ੍ਯੈ ਘਾਇ ॥\
ਗਿਰ ਗਿਰ ਪਰੈ ਪਵੰਗ ਤੇ ਬਰੇਂ ਬਰੰਗਨ ਜਾਇ ॥੯॥

Not one step back from the battle did they turn; they struck without fear;\
they fell and fell from their horses, and went to be wed by the maidens of heaven.[^12] (9)

ਚੌਪਈ ॥ *Chaupai*

ਇਹ ਬਿਧਿ ਹੋਤ ਭਯੋ ਸੰਗ੍ਰਾਮਾ ॥ ਜੂਝੇ ਚੰਦ ਨਰਾਇਨ ਨਾਮਾ ॥\
ਤਬ ਜੁਝਾਰ ਏਕਲ ਹੀ ਧਯੋ ॥ ਬੀਰਨ ਘੇਰ ਦਸੋ ਦਿਸ ਲਯੋ ॥੧੦॥

In this way the battle was fought,\
and a man named Chand Narain[^13] fell fighting;\
then Jujhar charged on his own,\
and the heroes hemmed him in on all ten sides.[^14] (10)

ਦੋਹਰਾ ॥ *Dohra*

ਧਸਿਯੋ ਕਟਕ ਮੈ ਝਟਕ ਦੈ ਕਛੂ ਨ ਸੰਕ ਬਿਚਾਰ ॥\
ਗਾਹਤ ਭਯੋ ਸੁਭਟਨ ਬਡੇ ਬਾਹਿਤ ਭਯੋ ਹਥਿਆਰ ॥੧੧॥

With a sudden rush he plunged into the army, giving no thought to fear;\
he trampled through great warriors and swung his weapons. (11)

ਚੌਪਈ ॥ *Chaupai*

ਇਹ ਬਿਧਿ ਘਣੇ ਘਰਨ ਕੋ ਗਾਰਾ ॥ ਭਾਂਤਿ ਭਾਂਤਿ ਕੇ ਕਰਿ ਹਥੀਯਾਰਾ ॥\
ਚੁਨਿ ਚੁਨਿ ਬੀਰ ਪਖਰੀਆ ਮਾਰੇ ॥ ਅੰਤਿ ਦੇਵ ਪੁਰ ਆਪ ਪਧਾਰੇ ॥੧੨॥

In this way he brought ruin on many households,\
wielding weapons of every kind;\
he picked off heroes on armoured horses one by one,\
and at the last went himself to the city of the gods. (12)

---
ਇਤਿ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਬਚਿਤ੍ਰ ਨਾਟਕ ਗ੍ਰੰਥੇ ਜੁਝਾਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਜੁਧ ਬਰਨਨੰ ਨਾਮ ਦ੍ਯਾਦਸਮੋ ਧਿਆਇ ਸਮਾਪਤਮ ਸਤੁ ਸੁਭਮ ਸਤੁ ॥੧੨॥ ਅਫਜੂ ॥੪੩੫॥

*Here ends the twelfth chapter of the holy book Bachittar Natak, called the Account of the Battle of Jujhar Singh. All is true, all is blessed. Chapter 12; verses so far, 435.*

### Notes

[^1]: Turks, the poet's word for the Muslim rulers and their armies; the leader is Husaini (chapter 11).
[^2]: ਸਊਰ, horsemen (Persian suwār). Narain Singh: Dilawar Khan sent a large new army, under his son Rustam Khan.
[^3]: Jujhar Singh, a Rajput (Narain Singh), sent by the other side.
[^4]: Bhalan, the village where the Khanzada had camped (chapter 10). Narain Singh reads that Jujhar Singh came to Bhalan and drove out the force posted there.
[^5]: Gaj Singh and Pamma (Narain Singh: Paramanand), commanders sent by Bhim Chand (Narain Singh); he also calls Gaj Singh a Jaswal.
[^6]: ਹਾਡਾ, read here as the Hada, a clan of Chauhan Rajputs, for Jujhar Singh; the line plays on ਗਾਡਾ, sunk in the ground. Narain Singh: this Rajput warrior could not be moved.
[^7]: ਸੇਲ *sel*, a lance.
[^8]: ਬਾਟ, divided; Narain Singh: both sides split their forces and came on.
[^9]: The Chandel and the Jaswal: the rajas of Chanderi and of Jaswan. Verse 4 puts the Chandel over there and verse 5 puts him on this side, as printed.
[^10]: Bhairav (ਭੈਰੋ), the terrible form of Shiva, the god of war and the burning ground.
[^11]: ਪਰੱਘ *paragh*, an iron-bound club; Narain Singh reads axes.
[^12]: ਬਰੰਗਨ, the maidens of heaven (apsaras), who in the old poetry wed the warriors who fall.
[^13]: Chand Narain, on Jujhar Singh's side (Narain Singh).
[^14]: ਦਸੋ ਦਿਸ, the ten directions: the eight points of the compass, above and below.

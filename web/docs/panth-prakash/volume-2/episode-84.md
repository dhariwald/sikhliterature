
# Panth Prakash, Episode 84

## Episode About Tara Singh: The Sikh Martyr

### (daṅgioṅ ham kim ṭaraiṅ daṅgo hamrī jāt: How can we desist from fighting, as fighting is systemic in our Being)

>**Questions**
>**Define:** Tara Singh of Van (ਤਾਰਾ ਸਿੰਘ ਵਾਂ), bunga (ਬੁੰਗਾ), Sahib Rai Chaudhary (ਸਾਹਿਬ ਰਾਇ ਚੌਧਰੀ), Baghel Singh (ਬਘੇਲ ਸਿੰਘ), 
>
>**Summarize (2–3 sentences each):**
>
>1. How the quarrel with Sahib Rai began.
>2. Tara Singh's choice after the first fight, and who stayed with him


ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਸਾਖੀ ਤਾਰਾ ਸਿੰਘ ਕੀ ਸੁਨ ਸੁ ਬੁੱਧਿ ਨਿਧਾਨ ॥\
ਬਾਂਇ ਡਲ ਮਧ ਸਿੰਘ ਭਯੋ ਬੁੱਟਰ ਗੋਤ ਪ੍ਰਧਾਨ ॥੧॥

(Now) my dear enlightened, wise Gursikh readers,\
Listen to the episode of Tara Singh (the great Sikh martyr).\
He belonged to a village known as Bain-Dal[^1],\
Which had a majority of Buttar[^2] Jat Sikh residents. (1)

ਛਪੈ ਛੰਦ *Chhapai Chhand*

ਹਠੀ ਤਪੀ ਜਤਵਾਨ ਰਹਿਤ ਵਹਿ ਰਹੈ ਸੁ ਚੰਗੀ ॥ ਨੀਲ ਬਸਤ੍ਰ ਤਨ ਸਜੈ ਸਜੈ ਤਨ ਸ਼ਸਤਰ ਜੰਗੀ ॥\
ਗੁਰਬਾਣੀ ਹਿਤ ਰਖੇ ਸਿਦਕ ਗੁਰ ਚਰਨਨ ਧਾਰੇ ॥ ਮੁਖ ਤੇ ਕਢੈ ਜੁ ਬਚਨ ਸਤੀ ਜਿਮ ਸੋ ਪ੍ਰਿਤਪਾਰੇ ॥\
ਦੰਗੈਯਨ ਮਹਿਂ ਪ੍ਰਿਥਮ ਉਸੈ ਧਰੀਐ ਨਾਮ ਗਿਣਾਇ ॥ਜਾਇ ਬਲਾਇ ਜੁ ਘਰ ਪਰੈ ਲਏ ਅਪਨੇ ਠੀਕ ਟਿਕਾਇ ॥੨॥

Leading a morally upright, clean and highly disciplined life,\
He donned (the Khalsa's) blue robes[^3] and battle ready armour.\
Reposing his (full) faith in Gurbani and the Sikh Gurus,\
He always kept his word as that kept by a Sati[^4].\
Deserving to be placed at the top among the fearless/fighters,\
He would even fight for a cause that concerned others. (2)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਸੀਸ ਧਰੇ ਹਥ ਪੁਰ ਫਿਰੈ ਨਹਿ ਮਰਨੋ ਸੰਕਾਇ ॥\
ਸਿਰੋਂ ਪਰੇ ਡੇਰਾ ਤਿਸੈ ਐਸੋ ਰਹੈ ਸੁਭਾਇ ॥੩॥

Being ever ready for a fight (for a cause),\
He never hesitated from sacrificing his life.\
Being ready to pick up a fight on the slightest pretext,\
Such was the stuff that he was made of. (3)

ਜਬ ਬੰਦੋ ਹੁਤ ਜੀਵਤੋ ਉਨ ਲੀਨੋ ਤੁਰਕ ਹਰਾਇ ॥\
ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ਾਮਦ ਕਰ ਕਰ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕੀ ਲੇਵੈ ਮੁਲਕ ਬਸਾਇ ॥੪॥

When Banda Singh Bahadur was alive (and powerful),\
He had defeated all the (ruling) Mughal [Turk][^5] forces.\
The Mughals then kept the Singhs in good humour,\
In order to remain in power in the whole country. (4)

ਲੇਟ ਪੇਟ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਰਖੈ ਤੱਦੀ ਲਖ ਟਲ ਜਾਇ ॥\
ਬੰਦੋ ਮਾਰਯੋ ਜਬ ਉਨੈਂ ਬਿਨ ਖਤਿਓਂ ਸਿੰਘ ਖਪਾਹਿਂ ॥੫॥

They maintained good cordial relations with the Singhs,\
In order to tide over the emergent moment of crisis,\
But once they had eliminated Banda Singh Bahadur,\
They killed (many) Singhs without any offence. (5)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਹੁਤੀ ਤਾਰੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਦਈ ਜਗੀਰ ॥ ਬੁੰਗੋ ਚਿਣਯੋ ਥੌ ਤਿਹ ਸਿੰਘ ਬੀਰ ॥\
ਤਾਰੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਨਹਿਂ ਸਮਾਂ ਨਿਹਾਰਯੋ ॥ ਵਹੀ ਰਖਯੋ ਉਨ ਸਿੱਡਾ ਧਾਰਯੋ ॥੬॥

The Mughals had made Tara Singh a custodian over an area [jagīr][^6],\
Where this brave warrior had raised a small mansion [bunga][^7].\
Tara Singh, taking no notice of the changed times,\
Continued with his same violent, aggressive stance. (6)

ਪਹਿਲੀ ਬਾਣੋਂ ਟਲੈ ਸੁ ਨਾਂਹਿ ॥ ਦੰਗੋ ਫੰਗੋ ਰਖੈ ਮਚਾਇ ॥\
ਰਾਹ ਜਾਂਦੀ ਲਏ ਕਾਂਇਸ ਮੋਲ ॥ ਦਸ ਪੰਜ ਦੰਗੱਯਨ ਰਖੈ ਸੁ ਕੋਲ ॥੭॥

Refusing to budge from his earlier violent behaviour,\
He kept indulging in acts of violence and rioting.\
Picking up a fight even for the stranger's cause,\
He kept on commanding a small battery of violent fighters. (7)

ਜਿਤ ਕਿਤ ਤੇ ਕੋਊ ਲਯਾਵੈ ਉਲਾਂਭੇ ॥ ਬਹੁਤ ਹਿਤ ਕਰ ਤਿਸ ਵਹਿ ਸਾਂਭੇ ॥\
ਸੋ ਮਰਨੇ ਤੇ ਡਰੈ ਨ ਮੂਲ ॥ ਇਮ ਕਰ ਦੰਗੋ ਰਖੈ ਜ਼ਰੂਰ ॥੮॥

Whosoever approached him with any kind of complaint,\
He would eagerly take upon himself to redress that issue.\
Having no fear of being killed in any violent encounter,\
He kept on indulging in violent acts compulsively. (8)

ਔਰ ਤਰਫ ਜੋ ਕਾਇਸ ਜਾਵੇ ॥ ਸੱਦ ਪਾਸ ਦੁਇ ਪਹਰ ਕਟਾਵੈ ॥\
ਐਸੀ ਪਰੀ ਤਾਰੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਬਾਣ ॥ ਨਹਿਂ ਮਾਨੈ ਵੈ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਆਣ ॥੯॥

Even if a contentious issue concerned others (unknown to him),\
He would get himself involved unnecessarily into it.\
Having become such a compulsive addict to violence,\
He refused to abide by the Mughal's sovereign writ. (9)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਕਹੈ ਦੰਗਿਓਂ ਹਮ ਕਿਮ ਟਰੈਂ ਦੰਗੋ ਹਮਰੀ ਜਾਤ ॥\
ਦੰਗੇ ਖ਼ਾਤਰ ਹਮ ਕੀਏ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਜੀ ਆਪ ॥੧੦॥

He boasted that he would never desist from violence,\
Since violence was systemically built in the Khalsa's body.\
They had been ordained to be violent (for a cause),\
By the express Will of the great Guru (Guru Gobind Singh). (10)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਦੰਗੈ ਹੀ ਤੇ ਪੈਯੁਗ ਪਤਿਸ਼ਾਹੀ ॥ ਦੰਗੈ ਹੀ ਤੇ ਹੋਗ ਸੀਸ ਲਾਈ ॥\
ਬਿਨ ਦੰਗੈ ਕੋਊ ਪੁਛੈ ਨ ਬਾਤ ॥ ਹਮ ਦੰਗੋ ਮਚਾਵੈਂ ਯੌ ਲਖ ਘਾਤ ॥੧੧॥

(The Khalsa) would attain sovereignty [Patshahi] through violence,\
And fight alone would prod them to make sacrifices.\
Since nobody bothered about their cause without violence,\
They would indulge in violence myriad times. (11)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਹੁਤੋ ਨੁਸ਼ਹਿਰੋ ਪੁਰ ਬਡੌ ਮਧ ਪੰਨੂ ਜੱਟ ਬਸਾਹਿਂ ॥\
ਸਾਹਿਬ ਰਾਇ ਬਡ ਚੌਧਰੀ ਹੁਤੋ ਉਸੈ ਕੈ ਮਾਂਹਿ ॥੧੨॥

There is a big town known as Naushehra (Pannuan)[^8],\
Which is inhabited by Pannu Jat Sikh clan.\
There was one feudal lord known as Sahib Rai Chaudhary,\
Who was also a resident of the self same town. (12)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਦੁਈ ਘੋੜੀ ਉਸ ਘਰ ਥੀ ਚੰਗੀ ॥ ਪਰ ਖੇਤੀ ਚਰਾਵਤ ਨਹਿਂ ਸੰਗੀ ॥\
ਤਹਿਂ ਭੀ ਦੁਇ ਸਿੰਘ ਖੇਤੀ ਕਰਾਵਤ ॥ ਸੋ ਘੋੜੀ ਚੌਧਰੀ ਚਰ ਜਾਵਤ ॥੧੩॥

He possessed a couple of two high-pedigree horses,\
Which he let lose to graze the people's crops.\
The crops which the two Singh farmers had raised,\
Were these lapped off by the Chaudary's horses. (13)

ਤੌ ਸਿੰਘ ਸਾਹਿਬ ਰਾਇ ਪਹਿ ਗਏ ॥ ਬੰਨ੍ਹੋ ਘੋੜੀ ਤੌ ਹਮ ਖੇਤ ਰਹੇ ॥\
ਤੌ ਚੌਧਰੀ ਕਹੀ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਉਚਾਰ ॥ ਹਮ ਚਹੈ ਮੁਨੈ ਤੁਮ ਸਿਰ ਕੇ ਵਾਰਿ ॥੧੪॥

Then the two Singhs approached Sahib Rai (Chaudhary) and appealed,\
That their crops could be saved only if the latter kept his horses under leash.\
The feudal chief (feeling incensed at their audacity) threatened,\
That he intended to shave off their hair to make a leash for his horses. (14)

ਸੁਨ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਤਨ ਮਨ ਲਗੀ ਆਗ ॥ ਕਹੈਂ ਕਹਾਂ ਕਰੈਂ ਕਿਛ ਕਾਬੂ ਨ ਲਾਗ ॥\
ਬਹੁ ਟੱਬਰਦਾਰੀ ਹੈ ਪਿੰਡ ਮਾਂਹਿ ॥ ਨਹੀਂ ਮਾਰ ਮਰਤ ਤੁਮ ਕੌ ਅਬ ਥਾਂਹਿ ॥੧੫॥

The Singhs, though feeling outraged at such a provocation,\
Remarked that they were desperately helpless to deal with him.\
They had large families to feed and support in the village,\
Otherwise they would have beaten him to death instantly. (15)

ਸੋ ਗਏ ਸਿੰਘ ਦੁਖਤ ਭੁਸਯੋ ਗਿਰਾਇ ॥ ਹੁਤ ਬਘੇਲ ਸਿੰਘ ਅਮ੍ਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਢਿੱਲੋ ਥਾਇ ॥\
ਸੱਦ ਉਨ ਕੋ ਦਈ ਘੋੜੀ ਕਢਾਇ ॥ ਉਨ ਅਗੇ ਦਈ ਘਰਯਾਲੇ ਪੁਚਾਇ ॥੧੬॥

So these aggrieved Singhs proceeded to a village Bhusa[^9],\
Which inhabited two Singhs named Baghel Singh and Amar Singh Dhillon.\
Inviting these two Snghs, the aggrieved Singhs got the Chaudhary's horses lifted,\
Who dispatched the stolen horses further to village Ghariala. (16)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਅਗੋਂ ਲੈ ਲਖਮੀਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਸੰਧੂ ਜੰਗਲ ਬੇਚੀ ਜਾਇ ॥\
ਫਿਰ ਬਾਂਇ ਤਾਰੈ ਸਿੰਘ ਪਹਿ ਦਏ ਲੰਗਰ ਪੈਸੇ ਪਾਇ ॥੧੭॥

From there Lakhmir Singh Sandhu got the custody of those horses,\
And sold these to somebody in the forested region [Jangal][^10] of (Malwa).\
He offered all the proceeds from the sale of the horses,\
To the free community kitchen [laṅgar][^11] run by Tara Singh at 'Wan'. (17)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਮਗਰ ਸਾਹਿਬ ਰਾਇ ਸੇਲੂ ਦੌੜੇ ॥ ਲੈ ਪੁਜੇ ਖੋਜ ਸੋ ਭੀ ਤਿਸ ਠੌੜੇ ॥\
ਉਸ ਖੋਜੀ ਸਿੰਘ ਚੋਰ ਬਨਾਏ ॥ ਉਨ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਲੁਕਨ ਨ ਠੌਰ ਲਭਾਏ ॥੧੮॥

Sahib Rai's detectives [selū][^12], picking up the trail of stolen horses,\
Also reached the spot where the horses had been kept,\
As detectives held the two Dhillon Singhs responsible for the horses' theft,\
The latter were at their wits' ends to find a place to take refuge. (18)

ਤੇ ਨਠ ਆਇ ਬਹੇ ਤਾਰੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਪਾਸ ॥ ਵਹ ਜੋ ਜੀਵਨ ਰਖੇ ਨ ਆਸ ॥\
ਤਹਿ ਹੀ ਆਪ ਸਾਹਿਬ ਰਾਇ ਆਯਾ ॥ ਆਇ ਤਾਰੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਬੋਲ ਸੁਨਾਯਾ ॥੧੯॥

Being desperate, they ran and found refuge at Tara Singh's 'Bunga',\
The (brave) Tara Singh who had shed all fears of death.\
(Following their trail), Sahib Rai also reached Tara Singh's place,\
And addressed Tara Singh (to heed his warning). (19)

ਹਮਰੇ ਦੇਹੁ ਤੂੰ ਚੋਰ ਫੜਾਇ ॥ ਨਹਿਂ ਦੇਹੈਂ ਲਿਊਂ ਲਹੌਰ ਮੰਗਾਵਾਇ ॥\
ਅਬ ਤੂੰ ਰਖ ਇਨ ਖੂਬ ਸੰਭਾਲ ॥ ਮਤ ਕਤ ਲੁਕ ਜਾਵੈਂ ਤੁਮ ਗਾਲ ॥੨੦॥

He threatened that either Tara Singh should handover the two cattle lifters,\
Or he would get them arrested by the authorities at Lahore.\
He warned Tara Singh to keep the thieves under his custody,\
Lest they should escape and put him into trouble. (20)

ਸਿੰਘ ਜੀ ਕਹੀ ਏਹ ਸਿੰਘ ਨਹਿਂ ਚੋਰ ॥ ਚੋਰ ਤੂੰਹੀ ਚਰਾਵਹਿ ਖੇਤੀ ਕਰ ਜੋਰ ॥\
ਕਰੇ ਇਨਸਾਫ ਲਏ ਤੈਨੂੰ ਲੂਟ ॥ ਹਮਰੀ ਜਿੰਦ ਤੇ ਨਬਾਬ ਕਯਾ ਲੈ ਕੂਟ ॥੨੧॥

Tara Singh retorted that the two Singhs were not thieves at all,\
Instead he (Sahib Rai) was a criminal who destroyed others' crops.\
They had rather done justice by stealing his horses,\
And he cared a fig for the Nawab at Lahore. (21)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਯਹਿ ਤੋ ਹਮ ਨੈ ਹੈ ਮਿਥੀ ਸਿਰ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਲਾਯਗੁ ਦੇਹ ॥\
ਲੁਕ ਭਜ ਕੇ ਨਹਿਂ ਬਚੈਂ ਹਮ ਮਰੈਂ ਬਹੁਤਨ ਸੰਗ ਲੇਹ ॥੨੨॥

Tara Singh declared that he had made a resolution,\
That he would sacrifice his life while fighting the Mughals.\
That he would never seek any refuge to save his life,\
And shed his mortal frame after killing so many others. (22)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਵਧਤੀ ਵਧਤੀ ਗਲ ਵਧ ਗਈ ॥ ਬੁਰੀ ਸਾਇਤ ਪਰ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਆਈ ॥\
ਜਟ ਜਾ ਪੱਟੀ ਫ਼ਰਯਾਦਹਿ ਕਰੀਂ ॥ ਤੁਮ ਈਹਾਂ ਬੈਠੇ ਸੁਖ ਸੋਂ ਘਰੀਂ ॥੨੩॥

Thus, a little incident flared up into a conflagration,\
As it proved to be an inauspicious moment for the Singhs,\
The Jat chief went and complained to the authorities at Patti[^13],\
That they were living safely in the safety of their houses. (23)

ਹਮ ਤੋ ਲੀਨੇ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਉਗਰਾਹਿ ॥ ਹਮ ਤੋਂ ਟਕੇ ਇਕ ਹੀ ਦੇ ਥਾਹਿ ॥\
ਸੁਨਿ ਹਾਕਮ ਤਿਸ ਲਗ ਗਈ ਆਗ ॥ ਸੁੱਤੋ ਸ਼ੇਰ ਜਨੁ ਉਠਿਓ ਜਾਗ ॥੨੪॥

He informed that since the Singhs were making collections forcibly[^14],\
The people could pay their revenue to only one authority.\
The ruler of Patti felt so outraged at the insinuation,\
As if a sleeping lion was provoked out of its sleep. (24)

ਤੌ ਪੁਛਯੋ ਹਾਕਮ ਸਿੰਘ ਹੈਂ ਕਿਤ ਥਾਇ ॥ ਕਹੀ ਜੱਟ ਜਹਿਂ ਬੁਟਰਨ ਬਾਂਇ ॥\
ਤਹਿਂ ਬਹਯੋ ਤਾਰਾ ਸਿੰਘ ਬੁੰਗੋ ਚਿਣਾਇ ॥ ਦਯੋ ਰੌਲ ਉਨਿ ਗਿਰਦੈ ਪਾਇ ॥੨੫॥

When the Patti ruler asked Sahib Rai about the Singhs' location,\
He informed that they were picketing at a village Wan of Buttar Jats.\
There the (Tat Khalsa) Tara Singh had constructed his Mansion [bunga],\
And had spread his terror all around the place. (25)

ਤੌ ਹਾਕਮ ਉਸ ਪੁਛੀ ਸਲਾਹਿ ॥ ਕਿਤਕ ਜਮਯਤ ਹੈ ਤਿਹ ਪਾਹਿ ॥\
ਜੱਟ ਕਹੀ ਉਸ ਪੈ ਗੜ੍ਹ ਨਾਂਹੀ ॥ ਦੋ ਇਕ ਚੁਬਾਰੇ ਹੈ ਉਨ ਪਾਹੀ ॥੨੬॥

Then, the Patti ruler asked him for further information,\
As to how much force he had managed together there.\
The Chaudhary Jat informed him that Tara had no fort of his own,\
But a couple of two storeyed rooms for his shelter had he. (26)

ਦਸ ਬੀਸ ਸਿੰਘ ਤੋ ਸਦ ਹੀ ਰਹੈਂ ॥ ਦਸ ਪੰਜ ਔਰ ਭੀ ਆਵਹਿ ਜਹੈਂ ॥\
ਕਿਸ ਨੇਜੋ ਕਿਸ ਪਹਿ ਤਲਵਾਰ ॥ ਕਈ ਰਹੈਂ ਤਹਿਂ ਬਿਨ ਹਥਿਆਰ ॥੨੭॥

He kept a force of ten to twenty Singhs regularly,\
While Singhs, in small batches, kept visiting his place.\
Some of these Singhs were armed with swords and spears,\
While some others stayed there without any weapons. (27)

ਸੁਨ ਹਾਕਮ ਚੜ੍ਹਯੋ ਪੱਟੀਓਂ ਧਾਇ ॥ ਮੁਹਰੇ ਤੋਰਯੋ ਕਰ ਸਾਹਿਬ ਰਾਇ ॥\
ਅੱਸੀ ਪਯਾਦੋ ਪੰਝੀ ਅਸਵਾਰ ॥ ਤੁਰੇ ਧੁਰੋਂ ਕਰਤ ਮਾਰੋ ਮਾਰ ॥੨੮॥

Hearing this, the Patti ruler advanced (towards Tara Singh's seat),\
With Sahib Rai leading this force from the front.\
This whole force, consisting of five[^15] horse riders and eighty foot soldiers,\
Launched on an expedition enthused with high spirits. (28)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਪਹਿ ਫੁਟਤੀ ਤੇ ਵਹਿ ਆਯੋ ਮਤਿ ਸਿੰਘ ਸੁਨ ਨੱਠ ਜਾਇਂ ॥\
ਲਈਅਗੁ ਘੇਰ ਸਵੇਰ ਤੌ ਦਈਅਗੁ ਲਹੌਰ ਪਹੁੰਚਾਇ ॥੨੯॥

This force rushed post haste[^16] (from their headquarters),\
Lest the Singh should escape (before their arrival).\
They intended to surround the place before day break,\
And dispatch the Singhs to Lahore (after capturing them). (29)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਇਤ ਬਘੇਲ ਸਿੰਘ ਬਾਣੀ ਕੋ ਭੋਗ ਪਾਯੋ ॥ ਕਰ ਅਰਦਾਸ ਸਿੰਘ ਜੰਗਲੈ ਧਾਯੋ ॥\
ਆਵਤ ਫੌਜ ਤੁਰਕ ਨਦਰ ਉਸ ਆਈ ॥ ਥੀ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਸਿਰ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਅਵਾਈ ॥੩੦॥

Here, S. Baghel Singh concluding his Gurbani recitation,\
Proceeded to answer the call of nature after a morning prayer [ardās][^17].\
He spotted a battery of the Mughal forces marching forward,\
As the Singhs had already an inkling of their impending attack. (30)

ਉਸ ਹਥ ਨੇਜੋ ਔ ਗਾਤ੍ਰੈ ਤਲਵਾਰ ॥ ਗਡਵੋ ਹੱਥ ਦੂਏ ਜਲ ਸੌਚਵਾਰ ॥\
ਵੇਖ ਸਿੰਘ ਚਿਤ ਮਤਾ ਮਤਾਯਾ ॥ ਅਬ ਖਬਰ ਕਰਨ ਜਾਉ ਜੇ ਪਿਛਲੈ ਦਾਯਾ ॥੩੧॥

With a spear in one hand and sword slung across a shoulder,\
He carried a bowl of water for making morning ablutions.\
Spotting out the Mughal force, he made up his mind,\
That he should not run back to inform the Singhs. (31)

ਸਿੰਘ ਕਹੈਂਗੇ ਡਰ ਮੁੜ ਆਯਾ ॥ ਇਨ ਨੈ ਹੀ ਥੋ ਦੰਗੋ ਉਠਾਯਾ ॥\
ਉਨਕੋ ਖਬਰ ਆਪੇ ਹੁਇ ਆਊਗੁ ॥ ਮਾਰ ਕੂਟ ਜਬ ਜੰਗ ਕੀ ਪਾਊਗੁ ॥੩੨॥

Singhs would regard his going back as an act of cowardice,\
Although they considered him to be the main cause of the present strife.\
He decided that they would themselves get informed,\
When they heard the din and noise raised by the fight. (32)

ਉਨ ਦਯੋ ਲਲਕਾਰੋ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਬੁਲਾਇ ॥ ਤੁਮ ਤੁਰਯੋ ਅਗੈ ਹਮ ਬਾਤ ਸੁਨਾਇ ॥\
ਸੋਊ ਸਾਹਿਬ ਰਾਇ ਲਯੋ ਪਛਾਣ ॥ ਕਹਯੋ ਹਾਕਮਹਿ ਮਤ ਏ ਪਾਏ ਜਾਣ ॥੩੩॥

So he challenged the advancing Mughal force loudly,\
That they should proceed further after dealing with him.\
Sahib Rai (Chaudhry) identifying S. Baghel Singh immediately,\
Signaled the Patti ruler never to allow Baghel Singh to escape. (33)

ਯਹਿ ਹੈ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਮੈਂ ਬਡੀ ਬਿਆਧ ॥ ਇਸ ਇਕ ਮਾਰੇ ਹੁਇ ਜੈਹੈਂ ਆਧ ॥\
ਯਹੀ ਚੋਰ ਹੈ ਘੋੜੀਅਨ ਵਾਰੋ ॥ ਹੈ ਸਿੰਘਾਂ ਮਧ ਬਡੋ ਖੁਆਰੋ ॥੩੪॥

He told that since S. Baghel Singh was the most dreadful among the Singhs,\
His capture would mean half the battle had been won.\
He was the one who had lifted his two horses,\
And he alone was the most wild among the Singhs. (34)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਤੌ ਹਾਕਮ ਹੱਲਾ ਕੀਯੋ ਮਤ ਸਿੰਘ ਪਾਵੈ ਜਾਣ ॥\
ਸਿੰਘ ਭੀ ਅਗਯੋਂ ਕੁਦ ਪਯੋ ਤਹਿਂ ਹੀ ਮਚ ਗਯੋ ਘਾਣ ॥੩੫॥

So the Patti ruler launched an attack,\
So that S. Baghel Singh might not make good his escape.\
As S. Baghel Singh also jumped into the fray,\
There raged a fierce battle (between one and many). (35)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਉਇ ਤੀਰ ਬੰਦੂਕ ਤਕ ਉਸੇ ਚਲਾਂਹਿਂ ॥ ਵਹਿ ਰਲ ਗਯੋ ਉਨ ਹੀ ਮਾਂਹਿ ॥\
ਉਨ ਕੇ ਸ਼ਸਤ੍ਰ ਉਨ ਲਗ ਪਾਹਿਂ ॥ ਰਾਤ ਹਨੇਰੀ ਸਮਝ ਨ ਆਹਿ ॥੩੬॥

As the Mughal soldiers started firing at him,\
He rushed forward and got mingled among them.\
Their own weapons started hitting them,\
As they lost their wits in the darkness of the night. (36)

ਕਿਸੈ ਨੇਜਾ ਕਿਸ ਮਾਰੈ ਤਲਵਾਰ ॥ ਉਪਰ ਘੋੜਯੋਂ ਸਿੰਘ ਤੁਰਕ ਦਏ ਡਾਰ ॥\
ਇਕ ਹੁਤੋ ਭਤੀਜੋ ਉਸ ਫੁਜਦਾਰ ॥ ਰੌਲੈ ਗੌਲੈ ਮੈ ਵਹਿ ਭਯੋ ਮਾਰ ॥੩੭॥

Hitting one with his spear and another with a sword,\
He would tumble down a soldier from his horse's back.\
There was a nephew of the Patti ruler in this contingent,\
Who got killed in this din and noise of the fight. (37)

ਕਯਾ ਜਾਣੋ ਕਿਸ ਤੈ ਲਗ ਗਈ ॥ ਨਾਇ ਬਘੇਲ ਸਿੰਘ ਫਤੈ ਸੁ ਅਈ ॥\
ਤੌ ਲੌ ਆਨ ਪਿਆਦੇ ਪਏ ॥ ਮਾਰ ਗੋਲੀ ਸਿੰਘ ਪੱਟ ਭੰਨ ਦਏ ॥੩੮॥

Nobody knew whose weapon had hit him,\
But the credit for this success went to S. Baghel Singh.\
Then a battery of foot soldiers fell upon him,\
And wounded him fatally on the thighs with bullets. (38)

ਚਲੀ ਬੰਦੂਕ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਸੁਨ ਪਾਈ ॥ ਨਿਕਲ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਦਈ ਭਜਾਇ ਲੜਾਈ ॥\
ਤੁਰਕ ਚਾਲੂ ਕਰ ਦਏ ਹਟਾਇ ॥ ਮੁਯੋ ਦੂਯੋ ਭਾਈ ਪਰ ਦੂਯੋ ਭਰਾਇ ॥੩੯॥

As the Singhs, heard the noise of musket-fire,\
They came out to chase away the Mughal soldiers.\
As this Mughal contingent was compelled to get back,\
Patti ruler's two nephews, sons of his two brothers, got killed[^18]. (39)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਹੁਇ ਚਾਲੂ ਫੁਜਦਾਰ ਮੁੜ ਉਤਰਯੋ ਕੰਬੋ ਕੀ ਜਾਇ ॥\
ਉਨ ਡੇਰ ਲਹੌਰੇ ਰੁਖ ਕੀਯੋ ਫਿਰਯਾਦ ਨਬਾਬਹਿ ਪਾਇ ॥੪੦॥

This fleeing Patti ruler (running from Tara Singh's village),\
Put up his camp at a village known as Kamboki[^19].\
From this camp, he intended to proceed to Lahore,\
To make an appeal for reinforcements from the Nawab. (40)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਖਾਨ ਬਹਾਦਰ ਹੁਤੋ ਨਬਾਬ ॥ ਵਹ ਤੁਰਯੋ ਦਿਖਾਵਨ ਅਪਨੋ ਬਾਬ ॥\
ਸੁਨ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕਈਅਨ ਸੋਚ ਪਈ ॥ ਤਾਰੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਪਹਿ ਆਨ ਇਮ ਕਹੀ ॥੪੧

So the Patti ruler proceeded to show his miserable plight,\
To Khan Bahadur[^20], the then Nawab of Lahore.\
Hearing this development, the Singhs felt concerned,\
And approached Tara Singh to share their concern. (41)

ਅਬ ਤੋ ਫਤੇ ਤੁਮਾਰੀ ਭਈ ॥ ਅਬ ਹੁਇ ਜਾਵੌ ਕਿਤ ਛੁਈ ਮਈ ॥\
ਹਮ ਲਰ ਤੁਰਕ ਜਿਤੈ ਨਹਿ ਜਾਹਿਂ ॥ ਕਿਮ ਹਮ ਮਰ ਕਰ ਜਾਨ ਗਵਾਇਂ ॥੪੨॥

They remarked that since the Singhs had won the first bout,\
They should now go underground for the time being.\
Since the Singhs could never conquer the Mughals in a straight fight,\
Why should they die in vain (in an unequal fight. (42)

ਤੌ ਸਿੰਘ ਜੀ ਉਨ ਅਗਯੋਂ ਉਚਾਰੀ ॥ ਹਮ ਤੌ ਕਰਹਿਂ ਨ ਜਾਨ ਪਿਆਰੀ ॥\
ਹਮ ਪਿਆਰੈ ਕਰਹੈਂ ਗੁਰ ਵਾਕ ॥ ਜਿਮ ਕਰ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਦਯੋ ਹੈ ਭਾਖ ॥੪੩॥

Thereupon S. Tara Singh addressed these Singhs in such a vein,\
That he did not care to hold on to his mortal frame.\
He would rather prefer to follow the Guru's injunction,\
As the great Guru had ordained (in the time of an ordeal). (43)

ਸਿੰਘ ਸੋਊ ਜੁ ਕਰੈ ਨਿਤ ਜੰਗ ॥ ਸਿੰਘ ਸੋਊ ਕਰੈ ਮਰਨੋਂ ਨ ਸੰਗ ॥\
ਅਸੀਂ ਲਰ ਮਰੈਂਗੇ ਬੁੰਗੇ ਮਾਂਹਿ ॥ ਨਹਿਂ ਨਠਿ ਕੈ ਹਮ ਜਾਨ ਬਚਾਹਿਂ ॥੪੪॥

He alone was the Singh who engaged himself in daily battle[^21],\
He alone was the Singh who feared not to die (for a cause).\
So he would fight to the finish at his own place [bunga],\
Nor would he desert his place to save his life. (44)

ਹੁਤੇ ਜੁ ਭੁਜੰਗੀ ਤਾਰੈ ਸਿੰਘ ਪਾਸ ॥ ਉਨੈ ਸਭਨ ਕੋ ਯੌਂ ਦਯੋ ਭਾਖ ॥\
ਜਿਨ ਹੋਣਾ ਹੈ ਇਹਾਂ ਸ਼ਹੀਦ ॥ ਰਹੇ ਸੋਊ ਔਰ ਜਾਹੁ ਵਗੀਦ ॥੪੫॥

The (Tat Khalsa) Singhs [bhujaṅgī][^22] who were present at his place,\
He told all of them in clear cut terms:\
Only those who wished to sacrifice should stay along,\
While others should desert (before the fight started). (45)

ਹੈ ਵੇਲੋ ਜਿਨ ਨਠਨੌ ਆਜ ॥ ਕਲ ਨਹਿ ਕੋਊ ਮਿਲੈਗੋ ਭਾਜ ॥\
ਇਤਨ ਕੁ ਸੁਨਤ ਕਈ ਨਠ ਗਏ ॥ ਕਿਛਕੁ ਸ਼ਹੀਦੀ ਕੋ ਭੀ ਰਹੇ ॥੪੬॥

That was the only day for those who wished to desert,\
Next day, they might not find an opportunity to escape.\
Hearing this, some of his colleagues took to their heels,\
While some of them stayed put to sacrifice their lives. (46)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਹੁਤ ਕੱਚੇ ਸੋ ਨੱਠ ਤੁਰੇ ਰਹੇ ਪੱਕੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਨਾਲ ॥\
ਸੂਰੇ ਪੂਰੇ ਜੋ ਮਹਾਂ ਸਾਬਤ ਸਿੰਘ ਗੁਰ ਲਾਲ ॥੪੭॥

Those, not fully indoctrinated into Khalsa ideology[^23], deserted,\
While those, fully committed to the Khalsa's cause, stayed.\
The latter were those who were fully motivated and committed,\
And to whom Guru's cause was too dear to forsake. (47)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਤੌ ਸਿੰਘ ਜੀ ਨੇ ਜੀਯ ਬਿਚਾਰੀ ॥ ਹੋਨ ਸ਼ਹੀਦੋਂ ਇਹ ਗਏ ਹਾਰੀ ॥\
ਹਮਰੇ ਹੁਤੇ ਸੁ ਜੋਊ ਪਿਆਰੇ ॥ ਕਹਤ ਹੁਤੇ ਹਮ ਚੜ ਹੈਂ ਨਾਰੇ ॥੪੮॥

Then S. Tara Singh contemplated (sadly) in his mind,\
That the deserters had failed in their promise to sacrifice.\
They were those who had been very dear to him,\
Who had promised to make sacrifices along with him. (48)

ਉਨਕੋ ਚਹਿਯ ਖਬਰ ਕਰ ਲਈ ॥ ਅਗੇ ਮਰਜ਼ੀ ਫਿਰ ਉਨ ਕੀ ਰਹੀ ॥\
ਹੁਤੋ ਕੀਯੋ ਹਮ ਉਨ੍ਹਾਂ ਕਰਾਰ ॥ ਜਬ ਖਾਵਤ ਸੁਨਹਿਰੋ ਸਭ ਰਲ ਨਾਰ ॥੪੯॥

He must also inform rest of them (about the impending fight),\
And leave the rest to their own sweet will.\
After all, they had been making promises (to sacrifice),\
When they had been partaking food from the same bowl [sunehria][^24]. (49)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਤਾਰੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਹੁਤ ਰਹਿਤ ਦੁਇ ਬੁਟਰ ਸ਼ਰੀਕ ਭਰਾਇ ॥\
ਬਡ ਸਿਦਕੀ ਬਡ ਸੂਰਮੇ ਵਹਿ ਗੁਰ ਕੇ ਬਚਨ ਕਮਾਇਂ ॥੫੦॥

There were two Buttar brothers, Tara Singh's own collaterals,\
Who used to stay with Tara Singh at his Bunga.\
They were great warriors and highly devout Sikhs,\
Who fully subscribed to and followed Guru's ideology. (50)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਵਸਾਵਾ ਸਿੰਘ ਕੁਇਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਨਾਂਇ ॥ ਬ੍ਰਹਮਣ ਸ਼ਾਹੀ ਝਲੀਯੋ ਥਾਇ ॥\
ਸਮੁੰਦ ਸਿੰਘ ਔ ਝਾਬਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਜੋਊ ॥ ਸੰਗ ਹੁਤੇ ਤਾਰੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਮੁਤਲਾਨੀਏ ਦੋਊ ॥੫੧॥

There were two Singhs named S. Wasawa Singh and Koer Singh,\
Who were Brahmins by caste and residents of village Jhalli[^25].\
There were two others S. Samund Singh and S. Jhabar Singh,\
Who were S. Tara Singh's colleagues from the city of Multan. (51)

ਸੂਰਾ ਸਿੰਘ ਆਯੋ ਖਾਹਲੜਯੋਂ ਤਖਾਣ ॥ ਸੁਣ ਚਿੱਠਾ ਚਿੱਤ ਸ਼ਹੀਦੀ ਠਾਣ ॥\
ਲਖਮੀਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਘਰਯਾਲਯੋਂ ਸੰਧਵੇਟਾ ॥ ਤਿਨ ਆਇ ਕਰੀ ਤਾਰੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਸੋਂ ਭੇਟਾ ॥੫੨॥

S. Sura Singh, a carpenter [Tarkhan][^26], came to join from Khalra,\
After receiving S. Tara Singh's edict and resolving to offer sacrifice.\
S. Lakhmir Singh, son of Sandhu Jats, came from Ghariala,\
And offered himself for sacrifice at Tara Singh's command. (52)

ਦੋਊ ਸਿੰਘ ਭੜਾਨੀਏਂ ਸੰਧੂ ਆਨ ਰਲੇ ॥ ਮਾਲੀ ਸਿੰਘ ਗੁਰਬਖਸ਼ ਸਿੰਘ ਭਲੇ ॥\
ਭੀਮ ਸਿੰਘ ਬਾਦਲ ਸਿੰਘ ਰਤੋ ਕੀ ਆਇ ॥ ਸੁਨਤ ਅਰਦਾਸ ਆਏ ਤੇਊ ਧਾਇ ॥੫੩॥

Two more Singhs of Sandhu caste joined from Bharana,\
Whose good names were S. Maali Singh and S. Gurbax Singh.\
S. Bhim Singh and S. Badal Singh arrived from Rattoke,\
Immediately after receiving an appeal [ardās] (from S. Tara Singh). (53)

ਮੇਘ ਸਿੰਘ ਮਦਰਾ ਦਾ ਗਿੱਲ ॥ ਹਾਤਾ ਸਿੰਘ ਚੂੰਘੋ ਹੁਤ ਜਿਲ ॥\
ਬੁਲਾਕਾ ਸਿੰਘ ਸਾਂਘਣ ਦਾ ਸੇਖੋ ॥ ਜੋਧ ਸਿੰਘ ਬਾਜਵਾ ਨਾਰੋਵਾਲੋਂ ਲੇਖੋ ॥੫੪॥

So Megh Singh, of Gill caste, arrived from Madra,\
As well as S. Hata Singh, of Jill caste, from Chungha.\
S. Bulaka Singh, of Sekhon caste, reached from Sanghana,\
As well as S. Jodh Singh Bajwa, came from Narowal. (54)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਭੋਗੜ ਸਿੰਘ ਔ ਰਸਾਲ ਸਿੰਘ ਪ੍ਰਦੇਸੀ ਪਸ਼ੌਰੀਏ ਜੋਇ ॥\
ਹੁਤ ਤਾਰੈ ਸਿੰਘ ਭੀ ਵਹਿ ਸੰਗੈ ਉਨ ਮਿਥੀ ਸ਼ਹੀਦੀ ਦੋਇ ॥੫੫॥

There were two more Singhs known as S. Bhogar Singh and Rasal Singh,\
Who belonged to a very distant area of Peshawar.\
They, too, had been comrades-in-arms of S. Tara Singh,\
Both of whom had vowed to make a sacrifice. (55)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਦੁਇ ਹੁਤੇ ਕਰਬਾਠਾਂ ਕੇ ਕਰ ਬਾਠ ॥ ਸੋ ਲੈਣ ਸ਼ਹੀਦੀ ਕਰ ਆਏ ਠਾਠ ॥\
ਰਹਯੋ ਹੁਤੋ ਅਮਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਭਸਯੋਵਾਲ ॥ ਆਇ ਰਹਯੋ ਵਹੁ ਭੀ ਤਾਰੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਨਾਲ ॥੫੬॥

There were two other Singhs, of Karbath caste, from Karbath[^27],\
Who arrived fully armed and dressed as martyrs.\
S. Amar Singh Dhillon (brother of late S. Baghel Singh) from Bhusowal,\
Also arrived to join the ranks of S. Tara Singh. (56)

ਸਭੈ ਸਿੰਘ ਰਲ ਬਾਈ ਭਏ ॥ ਕਿਛ ਅਗਲੇ ਕਿਛੁ ਮਗਰੋਂ ਅਏ ॥\
ਕਿਲਾ ਕੋਟ ਥੇ ਨਹਿਂ ਉਨ ਪਾਹਿ ॥ ਉਨ੍ਹ ਸੀਸ ਲਗਾਵਨ ਕੀ ਭਈ ਚਾਹ ॥੫੭॥

All these Singhs (who volunteered to fight) were twenty two in strength,\
Some of whom came earlier while others joined later.\
Neither any fort nor a boundary wall they had for protection,\
Except that they were too keen to make sacrifice in battle. (57)

ਤੁਰਤ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਇਕ ਥੰਮ ਘੜਾਯੋ ॥ ਦੇਖ ਭੂਮ ਭਲੀ ਠੌਰ ਗੜਾਯੋ ॥\
ਕਰ ਝਟਕੇ ਖਲ ਛੇਲੈ ਕਢਵਾਇ ॥ ਲਯੋ ਨਗਾਰੋ ਤੁਰਤ ਮੜ੍ਹਾਇ ॥੫੮॥

So they chiselled out a (wooden) pillar (out of a Tree Trunk),\
And planted it firmly on a plain level ground.\
Then, skinning a tender lamb after slaughtering it [jhatka][^28],\
They prepared a war drum [nagārā][^29] with its dried skin. (58)

ਵਾਂਗ ਟਮਕ ਤਿਨ ਦਰਾ ਲਗਾਯਾ ॥ ਖਾਲਸੋ ਆਕੀਓਂ ਕਹਿ ਵਜਵਾਯਾ ॥\
ਤੁਰਕ ਚੜ੍ਹਯੋ ਕਿਤ ਮੁੜ ਮਤ ਜਾਵੈ ॥ ਬਰ ਜਰੂਰ ਹਮ ਉਪਰ ਆਵੈ ॥੫੯॥

(Soon after) they declared it with the beat of drum [ṭamak][^30],\
That the Khalsa was out to defy the (Mughal rulers),\
Lest the invading Mughal force should return incognito,\
They must launch an attack on (the waiting) Singhs[^31]. (59)

### Notes

[^1]: Bain-Dal: the poet writes ਬਾਂਇ ਡਲ, and later ਬਾਂਇ alone (Kulwant Singh's 'Wan'). BSS: the village of Dal Van in the Amritsar district.
[^2]: Buttar: a Jatt clan; so also the Pannu, Dhillon, Sandhu, Gill, Jill, Sekhon and Bajwa of this episode. BSS: most of the village were Buttar Jatts, though other clans lived there too.
[^3]: ਨੀਲ ਬਸਤ੍ਰ. BSS: blue is the dress the Guru gave in the rahit and that the Akali Panth was given at its founding; the nishan sahib at the Panth's camps is blue for the same reason, the mark and uniform of the Panth.
[^4]: sati: a widow who burns herself on her husband's pyre. BSS: as a sati carries out the word she has given, so Tara Singh kept his.
[^5]: ਤੁਰਕ, Turks: the poet's word for the Muslim rulers and their armies. Kulwant Singh writes Mughals throughout.
[^6]: jagīr: an estate. BSS: the Turks had given it to him, telling him to live at ease there, farm, keep buffaloes and let the saṅgat come and go.
[^7]: bunga: a fortified house of the Singhs. BSS: he had built it on open, bare ground.
[^8]: Naushehra Pannuan, named for its Pannu Jatts, on the road from Harike Pattan to Amritsar (BSS). BSS: Sahib Rai was chaudhari for the Mughals.

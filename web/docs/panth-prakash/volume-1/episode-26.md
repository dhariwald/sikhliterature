# Panth Prakash, Episode 26

## The Story of the March to the South

### (payo Naurang dojak rāhi: Naurang has taken the road to hell)

*Dohra* ਦੋਹਰਾ

ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਤਲਵੰਡੀ ਖੜੇ ਧਯਾਨ ਸੁ ਕੇਤ ਲਗਾਇ ॥\
ਆਇ ਦਖਣੋਂ ਇਕ ਸਿਖ ਕਹੀ ਪਯੋ ਨੌਰੰਗ ਦੋਜਕ ਰਾਹਿ ॥੧॥

The Satguru stood at Talwandi,[^1] his mind fixed on something far away;\
a Sikh came up from the south and said, "Naurang[^2] has taken the road to hell." (1)

*Chaupai* ਚੌਪਈ

ਸੁਨ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਯੌ ਬਚਨ ਉਚਾਰੇ ॥ ਉਸ ਲੜਾਇ ਸੁਤ ਦਯੈ ਮਾਰੇ ॥\
ਅਬ ਦੱਖਣ ਕੀ ਕਰੀਐ ਚੜ੍ਹਾਈ ॥ ਇਮ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਗੁਰ ਉਚਰ ਸੁਨਾਈ ॥੨॥

Hearing it, the Satguru spoke these words:\
"We shall set his sons to fighting and have them killed.[^3]\
Now let us march upon the south."\
So the Guru spoke, and made it known to the Singhs. (2)

*Dohra* ਦੋਹਰਾ

ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਆਖਯੋ ਡੱਲ ਸਿੰਘਾ ਚਲ ਤੂੰ ਹਮਰੈ ਨਾਲ ॥\
ਤੁਮ ਕੋ ਦਿੱਲੀ ਪਤ ਕਰੋਂ ਔ ਪਤਿ ਦਿੱਲੀ ਗਾਲ ॥੩॥

The Satguru said, "Dall Singh,[^4] come with us;\
I shall make you lord of Delhi, and destroy the lord of Delhi." (3)

*Chaupai* ਚੌਪਈ

ਢਿਗ ਡੱਲੈ ਥੋ ਖੜੋ ਲਘੁ ਭ੍ਰਾਤ ॥ ਕਹੀ ਡੱਲੈ ਵਲ ਮੋੜ ਮੁਖ ਬਾਤ ॥\
ਦੇਉਗ ਪਤਸ਼ਾਹੀ ਤੁਧ ਭੀ ਐਸੀ ॥ ਨਿਜ ਪੁੱਤ੍ਰਨ ਕੌ ਦੀਨੀ ਜੈਸੀ ॥੪॥

Beside Dalla stood his younger brother;[^5]\
he turned his face to Dalla and said,\
"He will give you a Patshahi too, just like\
the one he gave his own sons." (4)

ਯਹੀ ਗਲ ਗੁਰ ਪ੍ਰੋਹਤ ਸੁਨੀ ॥ ਬੋਲਯੋ ਗੁਰ ਸੰਗ ਵਹੁ ਸਿਰ ਧੁਨੀ ॥\
ਮੈਂ ਰਹਯੋ ਬਹੁਤ ਬਾਰ ਸਮਝਾਇ ॥ ਛੇੜ ਜਟਨ ਕਛ ਲਯੋ ਕਹਾਇ ॥੫॥

The Guru's family priest[^6] heard those very words;\
shaking his head, he said to the Guru,\
"Many a time I tried to make you see it.\
You stirred up the Jatts[^7] and brought these words upon yourself. (5)

ਯਹ ਜੰਗਲੀ ਜਟ ਗੁਣ ਨਿਤ ਨਵੇਂ ਚਾਹੈਂ ॥ ਭੀ ਪਿਛੋਂ ਨਿੰਦਾ ਸੁਨਵਾਵਹਿਂ ॥\
ਕਹਯੋ ਗੁਰ ਹਮ ਜਟ ਛੇੜੈਂ ਕਾਇ ॥ ਪ੍ਰੋਹਤ ਕਹਯੋ ਪੁਤ ਲਏ ਮੁਏ ਅਖਵਾਇ ॥੬॥

"These Jatts of the Jangal[^8] want some new favour every day,\
and afterwards they make you hear their slander behind your back."\
The Guru said, "Why should we stir up the Jatts?"\
The priest said, "They have made you hear your sons called dead." (6)

ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਕਹੀ ਪੁਤ ਇਸ ਮਰ ਗਏ ॥ ਹਮ ਸਿਖ ਪੂਤ ਸੁ ਠਾਂਢੇ ਭਏ ॥\
ਇਤਨੀ ਕਹਨ ਤੁਰਤ ਡੱਲੇ ਪੁਤ ਮਰਾ ॥ ਤਬ ਡੇਰਾ ਗੁਰ ਕੂਚਹ ਕਰਾ ॥੭॥

The Satguru said, "It is his sons who have died;\
our sons, the Sikhs, stand here on their feet."\
No sooner were the words spoken than Dalla's son was dead,\
and then the Guru struck camp and marched. (7)

*Dohra* ਦੋਹਰਾ

ਨੌ ਮਹੀਨੇ ਤੇ ਨੌ ਦਿਨੈ ਤਲਵੰਡੀ ਰਖੈ ਮੁਕਾਮ ॥\
ਡੇਰਾ ਤੋਰਾ ਰੁਖ ਦਖਣ ਛੱਡ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਬਡ ਥਾਮ ॥੮॥

Nine months and nine days he kept his camp at Talwandi;\
then he struck camp and set his face to the south, leaving the great holdings of the Turks. (8)

*Chaupai* ਚੌਪਈ

ਡੱਲਾ ਗੁਰ ਤੇ ਬੇਮੁਖ ਭਯੋ ॥ ਦੋਇ ਤਿੰਨ ਡੇਰਨ ਤੁਰਤ ਮੁੜ ਪਯੋ ॥\
ਔਰ ਜੰਗਲੀ ਕਿਛ ਸੰਗ ਤੁਰੇ ॥ ਬੇਈਮਾਨ ਹੁਇ ਵਹੁ ਭੀ ਮੁੜੇ ॥੯॥

Dalla turned his face away from the Guru:\
after two or three camps he turned straight back.[^9]\
Some others of the Jangal went along too;\
they broke faith, and they too turned back. (9)

ਹੁਤੋ ਕੌਲ ਸੋਢੀ ਕੋਠੈ ਵਾਰਾ ॥ ਸੁਨਯ ਉਨ੍ਹੈ ਸੁਤ ਲਗਯੋ ਹਮਾਰਾ ॥\
ਉਨ ਮਗਰੋਂ ਦੀਵਾਨ ਦੌੜਾਏ ॥ ਸਣ ਪਲੰਘ ਉਸ ਚੱਕ ਲਿਆਏ ॥੧੦॥

There was Kaul, a Sodhi of Kotha;[^10]\
he heard it said, "Our son has gone along with them,"\
and he sent his diwans running after them;\
they picked the boy up, bed and all, and carried him back. (10)

ਜਬ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਸੋਢੀ ਬੁਲਵਾਯੋ ॥ ਲੋਕਨ ਡੇਰਾ ਖਾਲੀ ਬਤਾਯੋ ॥\
ਇਸ ਤੌਰ ਬਹੁ ਲੋਕ ਨਠ ਆਏ ॥ ਤੌ ਨਠ ਜੰਗਲੀ ਗੁਰੈ ਤਕਾਏ ॥੧੧॥

When the Satguru sent for the Sodhi,\
people told him the Sodhi's camp stood empty.\
In this way many people ran off home,\
and the Guru watched the men of the Jangal run. (11)

ਰਹੇ ਮਜੂਦ ਗੁਰ ਸੰਗ ਘਰ ਚਾਰ ॥ ਮੋਹਨ, ਭਗਤੂ, ਬਹਲੋ, ਰੂਪੇ ਵਾਰ ॥\
ਗਏ ਸਤਿਗੁਰੂ ਸੰਗ ਬਹੁ ਦੂਰ ॥ ਸੋ ਹਟੇ ਖੁਸ਼ੀ ਲੈ ਮੁਖੋਂ ਹਜੂਰ ॥੧੨॥

Four households stayed on with the Guru:\
Mohan's, Bhagtu's, Bahlo's, and those of Rupa.[^11]\
They went a long way with the Satguru,\
and turned back only with his blessing from his own lips. (12)

### Notes

[^1]: Talwandi Sabo in the Malwa, now Damdama Sahib, where the Guru had kept his camp.
[^2]: Naurang is the poet's name for Aurangzeb, the Mughal Patshah, who died in the Deccan on 3 March 1707. The poet's dojak is dozakh, hell. Baba Santa Singh Akali's reading is that he died on reading the Guru's letter, the Zafarnama, before he had finished it.
[^3]: Aurangzeb's sons fought over the throne after his death; Bahadur Shah defeated and killed his brother Azam at Jajau in June 1707.
[^4]: Dalla, the chaudhari of Talwandi Sabo and the Guru's host there. The poet calls him Dall Singh here and Dalla after.
[^5]: Baba Santa Singh Akali names him Malla. The taunt is that the Guru's own sons had been killed.
[^6]: prohat. Baba Santa Singh Akali's reading is that he came of a Brahmin family that had served the Guru's house since Guru Nanak and had become Sikhs; Kulwant Singh calls him the Guru's Brahmin cook.
[^7]: Jatt: the landholding farming caste of the Punjab, to which Dalla and his people belonged.
[^8]: The Jangal: the dry, sandy country of the Malwa around Bathinda and Talwandi Sabo.
[^9]: Baba Santa Singh Akali's reading is that Dalla went only as far as Jhanda Sahib, where he was set on watch with a khanda (the double-edged sword), and at midnight leaned it against the leg of the Guru's bed and went home to Damdama.
[^10]: Kotha Guru, a village near Talwandi Sabo. Sodhi: the Khatri clan of the Gurus from Guru Ram Das on. Baba Santa Singh Akali's reading is that Kaul held an estate there, and that his son, Abhai Ram, was carried off asleep on his bed by night.
[^11]: The poet's Rupe wār, those of Rupa: either the village of Bhai Rupa near Talwandi Sabo or the household of Bhai Rupa. Baba Santa Singh Akali reads the four as the households of Mohan, Bhagtu, Bahlo and Bhai Rupa.

# Bachittar Natak, Chapter 9

## The Battle of Nadaun

>**Questions**
>
>1. Alif Khan's fort: of what, and where?
>2. The Guru's arrows: how many from each hand?

#### ਅਥ ਨਦਉਣ ਕਾ ਜੁੱਧ ਬਰਨਨੰ ॥ *Now the account of the battle of Nadaun*

ਚੌਪਈ ॥ *Chaupai*

ਬਹੁਤ ਕਾਲ ਇਹ ਭਾਂਤਿ ਬਿਤਾਯੋ ॥ ਮੀਆਂ ਖਾਨ ਜੰਮੂ ਕਹ ਆਯੋ ॥\
ਅਲਫ ਖਾਨ ਨਾਦੌਣ ਪਠਾਵਾ ॥ ਭੀਮਚੰਦ ਤਨ ਬੈਰ ਬਢਾਵਾ ॥੧॥

We passed a long time in this way;\
then Mian Khan[^1] came to Jammu\
and sent Alif Khan[^2] to Nadaun,[^3]\
and he stirred up hostility with Bhim Chand.[^4] (1)

ਜੁੱਧ ਕਾਜ ਨ੍ਰਿਪ ਹਮੈ ਬੁਲਾਯੋ ॥ ਆਪਿ ਤਵਨ ਕੀ ਓਰ ਸਿਧਾਯੋ ॥\
ਤਿਨ ਕਠ ਗੜ ਨਵਰਸ ਪਰ ਬਾਂਧਯੋ ॥ ਤੀਰ ਤੁਫੰਗ ਨਰੇਸਨ ਸਾਧਯੋ ॥੨॥

The raja called on us to fight\
and himself set out against him;\
Alif Khan built a fort of wood on the hill of Navras,[^5]\
and the rajas made ready their arrows and muskets.[^6] (2)

ਭੁਜੰਗ ਛੰਦ ॥ *Bhujang Chhand*

ਤਹਾ ਰਾਜ ਸਿੰਘੰ ਬਲੀ ਭੀਮਚੰਦੰ ॥\
ਚੜਿਓ ਰਾਮ ਸਿੰਘੰ ਮਹਾ ਤੇਜ ਵੰਦੰ ॥\
ਸੁਖੰ ਦੇਵ ਗਾਜੀ ਜਸਾਰੋਟ ਰਾਜੰ ॥\
ਚੜੇ ਕ੍ਰੁੱਧ ਕੀਨੇ ਕਰੇ ਸਰਬ ਕਾਜੰ ॥੩॥

There was mighty Bhim Chand, a lion among kings;[^7]\
Ram Singh rode out, a man of great fire;\
Sukhdev the champion, raja of Jasrot,[^8]\
rode out in fury to see the whole work done. (3)

ਪ੍ਰਿਥੀਚੰਦ ਚਢਿਓ ਡਢੇ ਡਢਵਾਰੰ ॥\
ਚਲੇ ਸਿੱਧ ਹੁਐ ਕਾਜ ਰਾਜੰ ਸੁਧਾਰੰ ॥\
ਕਰੀ ਢੂਕ ਢੋਅੰ ਕਿਰਪਾਲਚੰਦੰ ॥\
ਹਟਾਏ ਸਬੈ ਮਾਰਿ ਕੈ ਬੀਰ ਬ੍ਰਿੰਦੰ ॥੪॥

Prithi Chand, the fierce Dadhwal,[^9] rode out,\
and they went straight on to set the rajas' affairs in order;\
as they closed in for the assault, Kirpal Chand[^10] met it,\
killed hosts of heroes and drove them all back. (4)

ਦੁਤੀਯ ਢੋਅ ਢੂਕੈ ਵਹੈ ਮਾਰਿ ਉਤਾਰੀ ॥\
ਖਰੇ ਦਾਂਤ ਪੀਸੈ ਛੁਭੈ ਛੱਤ੍ਰਧਾਰੀ ॥\
ਉਤੈ ਵੈ ਖਰੇ ਬੀਰ ਬੰਬੈ ਬਜਾਵੈਂ ॥\
ਤਰੇ ਭੂਪ ਠਾਂਢੇ ਬਡੋ ਸੋਕੁ ਪਾਵੈਂ ॥੫॥

They closed in for a second assault, and that too was beaten back down the hill;\
the canopied kings[^11] stood there gnashing their teeth in fury;\
up above, the enemy's warriors stood and beat their war drums,\
while the kings stood below in bitter grief. (5)

ਤਬੈ ਭੀਮਚੰਦੰ ਕੀਯੋ ਕੋਪ ਆਪੰ ॥\
ਹਨੂਮਾਨ ਕੇ ਮੰਤ੍ਰ ਕੋ ਮੁਖ ਜਾਪੰ ॥\
ਸਬੈ ਬੀਰ ਬੋਲੇ ਹਮੈ ਭੀ ਬੁਲਾਯੰ ॥\
ਤਬੈ ਢੋਅ ਕੈ ਕੈ ਸੁ ਨੀਕੇ ਸਿਧਾਯੰ ॥੬॥

Then Bhim Chand himself flared with fury\
and chanted the mantra of Hanuman[^12] aloud;\
he called all his heroes, and called us too,\
and then they made their assault and went up in good order. (6)

ਸਬੈ ਕੋਪ ਕੈ ਕੈ ਮਹਾ ਬੀਰ ਢੂਕੇ ॥\
ਚਲੇ ਬਾਰਿਬੇ ਬਾਰ ਕੋ ਜਿਉ ਭਭੂਕੇ ॥\
ਤਹਾ ਬਿਝੁੜਿਆਲੰ ਹਠਿਓ ਬੀਰ ਦਿਆਲੰ ॥\
ਉਠਿਓ ਸੈਨ ਲੈ ਸੰਗਿ ਸਾਰੀ ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾਲੰ ॥੭॥

All the great heroes closed in, blazing with fury,\
and went on like flaming brands carried to burn a hedge;\
there the hero Dayal of Bijharwal[^13] stood stubborn,\
and Kirpal rose up with his whole army behind him. (7)

ਮਧੁਭਾਰ ਛੰਦ ॥ *Madhubhar Chhand*

ਕੁੱਪਿਓ ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾਲ ॥\
ਨੱਚੇ ਮਰਾਲ ॥\
ਬੱਜੇ ਬਜੰਤ ॥\
ਕ੍ਰੂਰੰ ਅਨੰਤ ॥੮॥

Kirpal flared with fury,\
the horses[^14] danced,\
the instruments of war sounded,\
endless and terrible. (8)

ਜੁੱਝੰਤ ਜੁਆਣ ॥\
ਬਾਹੈ ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾਣ ॥\
ਜੀਅ ਧਾਰ ਕ੍ਰੋਧ ॥\
ਛੱਡੇ ਸਰੋਘ ॥੯॥

The young men fought,\
they swung their swords,[^15]\
and with fury in their hearts\
they loosed floods of arrows. (9)

ਲੁੱਝੈ ਨਿਦਾਨ ॥\
ਤੱਜੰਤ ਪ੍ਰਾਣ ॥\
ਗਿਰ ਪਰਤ ਭੂਮਿ ॥\
ਜਣੁ ਮੇਘ ਝੂਮ ॥੧੦॥

They fought to the end\
and gave up their lives;\
they fell to the ground\
like clouds sweeping down. (10)

ਰਸਾਵਲ ਛੰਦ ॥ *Rasaval Chhand*

ਕਿਰਪਾਲ ਕੋਪਿਯੰ ॥ ਹਠੀ ਪਾਵ ਰੋਪਿਯੰ ॥\
ਸਰੋਘੰ ਚਲਾਏ ॥ ਬਡੇ ਬੀਰ ਘਾਏ ॥੧੧॥

Kirpal flared with fury;\
stubborn, he planted his feet;\
he shot floods of arrows\
and struck down great heroes. (11)

ਹਣੇ ਛੱਤ੍ਰਧਾਰੀ ॥ ਲਿਟੇ ਭੂਪ ਭਾਰੀ ॥\
ਮਹਾਂ ਨਾਦ ਬਾਜੇ ॥ ਭਲੇ ਸੂਰ ਗਾਜੇ ॥੧੨॥

Canopied kings were slain,\
great kings lay rolling in the dust;\
mighty sounds rang out,\
and true warriors roared. (12)

ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾਲੰ ਕ੍ਰੁੱਧੰ ॥ ਕੀਯੋ ਜੁੱਧ ਸੁੱਧੰ ॥\
ਮਹਾਂ ਬੀਰ ਗੱਜੇ ॥ ਮਹਾਂ ਸਾਰ ਬੱਜੇ ॥੧੩॥

Kirpal, in fury,\
fought a flawless fight;\
great heroes thundered,\
and great steel rang on steel. (13)

ਕਰਿਯੋ ਜੁੱਧ ਚੰਡੰ ॥ ਸੁਣਿਯੋ ਨਾਵ ਖੰਡੰ ॥\
ਚਲਿਯੋ ਸਸਤ੍ਰ ਬਾਹੀ ॥ ਰਜੌਤੀ ਨਿਬਾਹੀ ॥੧੪॥

He fought so fiercely\
that it was heard in the nine regions of the earth;[^16]\
he went on, swinging his weapons,\
and kept faith with his Rajput honour.[^17] (14)

ਦੋਹਰਾ ॥ *Dohra*

ਕੋਪ ਭਰੇ ਰਾਜਾ ਸਬੈ ਕੀਨੋ ਜੁੱਧ ਉਪਾਇ ॥\
ਸੈਨ ਕਟੋਚਨ ਕੀ ਤਬੈ ਘੇਰ ਲਈ ਅਰਰਾਇ ॥੧੫॥

All the rajas, filled with fury, set about the fight,\
and with a roar they surrounded the army of the Katoch.[^18] (15)

ਭੁਜੰਗ ਛੰਦ ॥ *Bhujang Chhand*

ਚਲੇ ਨਾਂਗਲੂ ਪਾਂਗਲੂ ਵੇਦੜੋਲੰ ॥\
ਜਸਵਾਰੇ ਗੁਲੇਰੇ ਚਲੇ ਬਾਂਧ ਟੋਲੰ ॥\
ਤਹਾਂ ਏਕ ਬਾਜਿਓ ਮਹਾਂ ਬੀਰ ਦਿਆਲੰ ॥\
ਰਖੀ ਲਾਜ ਜੌਨੇ ਸਭੈ ਬਿਝੜਵਾਲੰ ॥੧੬॥

On came the Nangalus, the Pangalus and the Vedrols,[^19]\
the men of Jaswan and of Guler came on in bands;\
there one great hero, Dayal, fought,\
he who saved the honour of all Bijharwal. (16)

ਤਵੰ ਕੀਟ ਤੌ ਲੌ ਤੁਫੰਗੰ ਸੰਭਾਰੋ ॥\
ਹ੍ਰਿਦੈ ਏਕ ਰਾਵੰਤ ਕੇ ਤੱਕਿ ਮਾਰੋ ॥\
ਗਿਰਿਓ ਝੂਮ ਭੂਮੈ ਕਰਿਯੋ ਜੁਧ ਸੁੱਧੰ ॥\
ਤਊ ਮਾਰਿ ਬੋਲਿਯੋ ਮਹਾ ਮਾਨਿ ਕ੍ਰੁੱਧੰ ॥੧੭॥

Then your worm[^20] took up his musket,\
took aim, and shot a chief[^21] through the heart;\
he reeled and fell to the ground, he had fought a true fight,\
and even then he cried out "Kill," proud and full of fury. (17)

ਤਜਿਯੋ ਤੁਪਕੰ ਬਾਨ ਪਾਨੰ ਸੰਭਾਰੇ ॥\
ਚਤੁਰ ਬਾਨਯੰ ਲੈ ਸੁ ਸੱਬਿਯੰ ਪ੍ਰਹਾਰੇ ॥\
ਤ੍ਰਿਯੋ ਬਾਨ ਲੈ ਬਾਮ ਪਾਨੰ ਚਲਾਏ ॥\
ਲਗੇ ਯਾ ਲਗੇ ਨਾ ਕਛੂ ਜਾਨਿ ਪਾਏ ॥੧੮॥

We put down the musket and took arrows in hand;\
we took four arrows and shot them with the right hand,\
took three more and shot them with the left;\
whether they struck or not, we could not tell. (18)

ਸੋ ਤਉ ਲਉ ਦਈਵ ਜੁਧ ਕੀਨੋ ਉਝਾਰੰ ॥\
ਤਿਨੈ ਖੇਦ ਕੈ ਬਾਰਿ ਕੇ ਬੀਚ ਡਾਰੰ ॥\
ਪਰੀ ਮਾਰ ਬੁੰਗੰ ਛੁਟੀ ਬਾਣ ਗੋਲੀ ॥\
ਮਨੋ ਸੂਰ ਬੈਠੇ ਭਲੀ ਖੇਲ ਹੋਲੀ ॥੧੯॥

By then the Lord[^22] had turned the battle;\
the enemy were chased and driven into the water;\
blows rained on the hilltop, arrows and bullets flew,\
as though the warriors had sat down to a fine game of Holi.[^23] (19)

ਗਿਰੇ ਬੀਰ ਭੂਮੰ ਸਰੰ ਸਾਂਗ ਪੇਲੰ ॥\
ਰੰਗੇ ਸ੍ਰੌਣ ਬਸਤ੍ਰੰ ਮਨੋ ਫਾਗ ਖੇਲੰ ॥\
ਲੀਯੋ ਜੀਤ ਬੈਰੀ ਕੀਆ ਆਨ ਡੇਰੰ ॥\
ਤੇਊ ਜਾਇ ਪਾਰੰ ਰਹੇ ਬਾਰਿ ਕੇਰੰ ॥੨੦॥

Heroes fell to the ground, run through by arrows and javelins,[^24]\
their clothes dyed with blood as though they had played at Phag;\
we beat the enemy and came and made our camp,\
and they went across and stayed on the far side of the water. (20)

ਭਈ ਰਾਤ੍ਰਿ ਗੁਬਾਰ ਕੇ ਅਰਧ ਜਾਮੰ ॥\
ਤਬੈ ਛੋਰਿਗੇ ਬਾਰ ਦੇਵੈ ਦਮਾਮੰ ॥\
ਸਬੈ ਰਾਤ੍ਰਿ ਬੀਤੀ ਉਦਿਓ ਦਿਉਸਰਾਣੰ ॥\
ਚਲੇ ਬੀਰ ਚਾਲਾਕ ਖੱਗੰ ਖਿਲਾਣੰ ॥੨੧॥

When an hour and a half of the dark night had passed,\
they slipped away from the river, leaving their drums beating;[^25]\
the whole night passed, the sun, king of the day, rose,\
and the nimble heroes went out brandishing their swords. (21)

ਭਜਿਓ ਅਲਫ ਖਾਨੰ ਨ ਖਾਨਾ ਸੰਭਾਰਿਓ ॥\
ਭਜੇ ਅਉਰ ਬੀਰੰ ਨ ਧੀਰੰ ਬਿਚਾਰਿਓ ॥\
ਨਦੀ ਪੈ ਦਿਨੰ ਅਸਟ ਕੀਨੇ ਮੁਕਾਮੰ ॥\
ਭਲੀ ਭਾਂਤਿ ਦੇਖੇ ਸਬੈ ਰਾਜ ਧਾਮੰ ॥੨੨॥

Alif Khan fled and never stopped to see to his camp;\
the other warriors fled too, with no thought of standing firm;\
we stayed eight days by the river\
and saw all the palaces of the raja well. (22)

ਚੌਪਈ ॥ *Chaupai*

ਇਤ ਹਮ ਹੋਇ ਬਿਦਾ ਘਰ ਆਏ ॥ ਸੁਲਹ ਨਮਿਤ ਵੈ ਉਤਹਿ ਸਿਧਾਏ ॥\
ਸੰਧਿ ਇਨੈ ਉਨ ਕੈ ਸੰਗਿ ਕਈ ॥ ਹੇਤ ਕਥਾ ਪੂਰਨ ਇਤ ਭਈ ॥੨੩॥

Here we took our leave and came home,\
and they went over to the other side to make peace;\
these rajas made a treaty with those men,\
and here the story of it comes to an end.[^26] (23)

ਦੋਹਰਾ ॥ *Dohra*

ਆਲਸੂਨ ਕਹ ਮਾਰਿ ਕੈ ਇਹ ਦਿਸਿ ਕੀਓ ਪਿਯਾਨ ॥\
ਭਾਤਿ ਅਨੇਕਨ ਕੇ ਕਰੇ ਪੁਰ ਅਨੰਦ ਸੁਖ ਆਨ ॥੨੪॥

We sacked Alsun[^27] and set out this way,\
and came to Anandpur and took our ease in every way.[^28] (24)

---
ਇਤਿ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਬਚਿਤ੍ਰ ਨਾਟਕ ਗ੍ਰੰਥੇ ਨਦੌਨ ਜੁੱਧ ਬਰਨਨੰ ਨਾਮ ਨੌਮੋ ਧਿਆਇ ਸਮਾਪਤਮ ਸਤੁ ਸੁਭਮ ਸਤੁ ॥੯॥ ਅਫਜੂ ॥੩੪੪॥

*Here ends the ninth chapter of the holy book Bachittar Natak, called the Account of the Battle of Nadaun. All is true, all is blessed. Chapter 9; verses so far, 344.*

### Notes

[^1]: Steek: Mian Khan was an official of Aurangzeb who came with an army to collect three years' tribute from the hill rajas, and to fight them if they would not pay. Narain Singh: Aurangzeb, busy with the rebels of the Deccan, had received nothing from the Punjab; Dilawar Khan of Lahore told him the hill rajas had paid nothing for three years, and in Sammat 1746 (1689 CE) Aurangzeb sent Mian Khan with an army to collect it.
[^2]: Steek: Alif Khan was a commander of Aurangzeb and Mian Khan's helper; Narain Singh calls him Mian Khan's nephew. Narain Singh: Alif Khan first besieged Kirpal Chand of Kangra, who paid him, made peace, and told him that Bhim Chand was the greatest raja of them all and should be made to pay first, after which no raja would stand against him; Dayal of Bijharwal agreed. Alif Khan marched on Kahlur and sent Bhim Chand word to pay three years' tribute or make ready for war, and Bhim Chand, though hostile to the Guru, put that aside and asked for his help.
[^3]: Nadaun, on the Beas in the Kangra hills. Narain Singh: a gurdwara stands on the bank of the Beas there in memory of the Guru's coming.
[^4]: Bhim Chand, raja of Kahlur. Steek: Kahlur is the country of the state of Bilaspur, on the bank of the Satluj.
[^5]: Navras, a hill near Nadaun (Narain Singh: the mound of Navras).
[^6]: ਤੁਫੰਗ *tufaṅg*, musket (also ਤੁਪਕ, v. 18) · ਤੀਰ *tīr*, ਬਾਨ *bān*, arrow.
[^7]: ਰਾਜ ਸਿੰਘੰ, read here as a lion among kings; Narain Singh reads two warriors, Raj Singh and Ram Singh.
[^8]: Steek: the raja of Jasrot (Jasrota, in the Jammu hills) was Sukhdev.
[^9]: Prithi Chand, raja of Dadhwal (Narain Singh) · ਡਢੇ, fierce.
[^10]: Kirpal Chand, the Katoch raja of Kangra, on Alif Khan's side (Narain Singh).
[^11]: ਛੱਤ੍ਰਧਾਰੀ, the holders of the royal canopy, the ruling rajas.
[^12]: Hanuman, the monkey god, Ram's servant, called on for strength in battle.
[^13]: Dayal (Narain Singh: Dayal Chand), chief of Bijharwal, on Alif Khan's side.
[^14]: The poet's word is ਮਰਾਲ, a swan; here the horses (Narain Singh).
[^15]: ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾਣ, a fighter's sword (ਖੱਗ, v. 21, the same) · ਸਰੋਘ, a flood of arrows.
[^16]: ਨਾਵ ਖੰਡ, the nine regions into which the Puranas divide the earth.
[^17]: ਰਜੌਤੀ, the honour and code of a Rajput.
[^18]: The Katoch, the Kangra line: Kirpal Chand and his men.
[^19]: Steek: the Nangalus are the descendants of Chuhamian, son of Sampar Chand, son of a raja of Kahlur, and Nangalu was their nickname; the Pangalus are the Rajputs of the Pangi country in the state of Chamba; the Vedrols are a branch of the Rajputs tied to the Jaswal capital. The Gulerias are the Rajputs of the state of Guler.
[^20]: ਕੀਟ *kīṭ*, a worm or insect: the Guru names himself so before the Lord he addresses.
[^21]: Raja Dayal of Bijharwal (Macauliffe); Narain Singh: one of the rajas.
[^22]: ਦਈਵ *daīv*, the Divine; Narain Singh reads Akal Purakh · ਬੁੰਗ, the post on the hilltop (Narain Singh: the mound).
[^23]: Holi, the spring festival when people throw coloured powder and water at each other; Phag (ਫਾਗ, v. 20) is the playing of it in the month of Phagan.
[^24]: ਸਾਂਗ *sāṅg*, javelin.
[^25]: ਅਰਧ ਜਾਮ, half a watch of the night · ਦਮਾਮ, war drums. Narain Singh: Alif Khan fled with his army and left a few men behind to keep the fires burning and beat the drums, so that his enemy would think he still held his camp.
[^26]: Steek: the hill rajas made peace with the fleeing Alif Khan and laid the whole responsibility for the war on the Guru, an open proof of their faithlessness and ingratitude. The Guru saw this weakness in them; he had turned to building forts as soon as he came back from Paonta, the fort of Anandgarh was already finished and the others were being built, and he had enrolled two or three thousand Sikh soldiers.
[^27]: Alsun, a village on the road back to Anandpur. Narain Singh: the Guru punished the rebels of Alsun on the way.
[^28]: Steek, quoting the Gurbilas attributed to Bhai Mani Singh (Bhai Randhir Singh's edition, p. 91): the battle at Nadaun was fought in Sammat 1747, on the 22nd day of the month of Chet (March to April 1691 CE); after the victory the Guru came home, and the rajas went to their own homes. Narain Singh: in the last days of Sammat 1747.

# Panth Prakash, Episode 38

## The Episode of the Pathans of Maler: the Battle in Which Khwaja Khidar the Accursed and Two of His Brothers Died

### (mayo akhvāj ju gurū kahyo mardūd...: Khwaja died, the one the Guru had called the Accursed)

>**Questions**
>
>1. The Guru's name for Khwaja Khidar?
>2. How Khwaja Khidar died?
>3. Why Sher Muhammad was spared?

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਬੰਦੈ ਪਠਾਏ ਸਿੰਘ ਥੇ ਤੇ ਫਿਰ ਲੀਏ ਬੁਲਾਇ ॥\
'ਹਮ ਆਵੈਂ ਸਰਹਿੰਦ ਕੋ ਤੁਮ ਆਵੋ ਰੋਪੜ ਦਾਇ' ॥੧॥

Banda now called back to him the Singhs he had sent away before (the Majhail Singhs),\
and wrote to them, "We shall march on Sirhind from our side; you come down toward us by way of Ropar."[^1] (1)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਸੁਨਤ ਸੁ ਚਿੱਠੋ ਖਾਲਸੋ ਚੜਿਓ ॥ ਰੋਪੜ ਦਰੈ ਜਾਇ ਸੋ ਵੜਿਓ ॥\
ਯਹ ਜਬ ਖਬਰ ਬਜੀਰੇ ਭਈ ॥ ਉਧਰ ਫੌਜ ਮਲੇਰੀਅਨ ਠਈ ॥੨॥

As soon as they heard the letter, the Khalsa mounted and rode out,\
and came down and entered the pass at Ropar, just as Banda had told them;\
and when the news of this reached Wazira,[^2] the nawab of Sirhind,\
he made up an army of the men of Maler[^3] and sent it out against them. (2)

ਤਿਨ ਮੇਂ ਚਾਰ ਸਰਦਾਰ ਥੇ ਆਈ ॥ ਨਾਹਰ ਖਾਂ ਕੇ ਥੇ ਜੋ ਭਾਈ ॥\
ਅਖ੍ਵਾਜ ਖਿਦਰ ਔ ਮੁਹੰਮਦ ਸ਼ੇਰ ॥ ਔਰ ਭਾਈ ਦੁਇ ਹੁਤੇ ਮਤੇਰ ॥੩॥

Four leaders came riding at the head of that army of the men of Maler,\
and every one of the four was a brother of Nahar Khan;\
Khwaja Khidar[^4] and Sher Muhammad were two of them, his full brothers,\
and the other two were his stepbrothers, the sons of another mother. (3)

ਤੇ ਕਰ ਦੌੜ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਪਰ ਪੜੇ ॥ ਦੇਖ ਫੌਜ ਸਿੰਘ ਹੋ ਪਏ ਖੜੇ ॥\
ਸਿੰਘਨ ਪਠਾਣਨ ਪੜ ਗਯੋ ਭੇੜ ॥ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਪੈ ਉਨ ਕੀਨੋ ਨੇੜ ॥੪॥

They charged at a run and fell upon the Singhs with all their weight;\
the Singhs, seeing that army coming on, stood up on their feet to face it;\
the Singhs and the Pathans crashed together in a fierce and bloody clash,\
as the Pathans pressed in close upon the Singhs, hand to hand. (4)

ਸਿੰਘ ਭੀ ਅਗਯੋਂ ਟਰੇ ਸੁ ਨਾਂਹਿ ॥ ਸਨਮੁਖ ਹੁਇ ਹੁਇ ਜੁੱਧ ਕਰਾਂਹਿ ॥\
ਦੀਨ ਮਜ੍ਹਬ ਇਕ ਦੂਜੇ ਗੁਰ ਬੈਰ ॥ ਸਿੰਘ ਮਰੇਂ ਕਰ ਆਗੇ ਪੈਰ ॥੫॥

The Singhs did not give way before them, not a single step backward,\
but turned their faces to the enemy and fought them, again and again;\
for one thing this was a war of faith and religion, and for another, the Guru's own feud to settle,\
and the Singhs died with their feet set forward, facing the enemy. (5)

ਕੁੰਡਲੀਆ ਛੰਦ *Kundaliya Chhand*

ਤੋਪ ਜੰਬੂਰੇ ਰਹਕਲੇ ਲਾਗੇ ਚਲਨ ਅਨੇਕ ॥ ਤਬ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਪ੍ਰਿਥਵੀ ਫੜੀ ਅਗਯੋਂ ਟਰਯੋ ਨ ਏਕ ॥\
ਅਗਯੋਂ ਟਰਯੋ ਨੇ ਏਕ ਆਨ ਜਬ ਗਾਢ ਸੁ ਪਰਿਓ ॥ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਫਿਰ ਗਯੋ ਪੈਰ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਨੈ ਆਗੈ ਕਰਿਓ ॥\
ਸਿੰਘ ਲਗੇ ਉਹਾਂ ਅਤਿ ਘਨੇ ਤਉ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਆਯੋ ਕੋਪ ॥ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਹੱਲਾ ਤਬ ਕੀਯੋ ਛਡ ਗਏ ਘੋੜੇ ਤੁਰਕ ਤੋਪ ॥੬॥

Cannon, swivel guns and field guns,[^5] many of them, began to fire upon the Singhs;\
then the Singhs gripped the earth (in their trenches), and not one of them moved back.\
Not one moved back a single step when the fighting pressed hard upon them;\
the feet of the Turks[^6] turned back, and the Singhs set theirs forward.\
Very many Singhs fell there, struck down, and then a fury came upon the Khalsa;\
the Khalsa charged, and the Turks ran, leaving their horses and their cannon behind them. (6)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਖ੍ਵਾਜ ਖਿਦਰ ਸੁਨਿ ਆਇ ਸੁ ਪਯੋ ॥ ਉਨ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਮੁਖ ਆਨ ਫਿਰਯੋ ॥\
ਤਬਹਿ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਬਾਤਿ ਬਿਚਾਰੀ ॥ ਨਠ ਕਰ ਬਚੈ ਕਬ ਫੌਜ ਹਮਾਰੀ ॥੭॥

Khwaja Khidar heard of it, came up, and threw himself into the fight,\
and he drove the Singhs back, forcing their faces round from the front;\
then the Khalsa took counsel together and weighed the matter carefully:\
"When was an army like ours ever saved by running away?" (7)

ਸਿੰਘ ਸੁ ਬੈਠ ਊਹਾਂ ਹੀ ਰਹੇ ॥ ਜਨਕ ਬੈਠ ਕਰ ਡੇਰੋ ਬਹੇ ॥\
ਬਹੁਤ ਜ਼ੋਰ ਤਹਿਂ ਕੀਓ ਪਠਾਣਨ ॥ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਪਕੜਯੋ ਖੂਬ ਮਦਾਨਨ ॥੮॥

So the Singhs sat down right there where they stood, and stayed,\
as if they had sat down at their ease in their own camp;\
the Pathans threw great force against them there, again and again,\
but the Singhs held the field firmly and would not give it up. (8)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਸਿੰਘ ਸੁ ਲੰਮੈ ਪੈ ਰਹੈ ਕਹੈਂ ਪੜੈ ਕਬ ਰਾਤ ॥\
ਤੁਰਕ ਕਹੈਂ ਹੋਤ ਦਿਨਹਿ ਕੈ ਕਰੀਐ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਘਾਤ ॥੯॥

The Singhs lay flat on the ground in their places, saying to one another, "When will the night come down?"\
and the Turks said, "While the day still lasts, let us fall upon the Singhs and slaughter them." (9)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਤੌ ਕਿਛੁ ਢੁਕਯੋ ਦਿਖਯੋ ਦਿਨ ਨੇੜੈ ॥ ਤੁਰਕ ਕਹੈ ਸਿੰਘ ਮਰੈਂ ਨ ਘੇਰੈ ॥\
ਦਾਰੂ ਸਿੱਕੋ ਇਨ ਮੁਕ ਗਯੋ ॥ ਭੁਖ ਪਿਆਸੋ ਇਨ ਬਲ ਗਯੋ ੧੦॥

Then, as they saw the end of the day drawing close upon them,\
the Turks said, "The Singhs will never be killed by a siege;\
but their powder and their lead are all used up now,\
and hunger and thirst have drained away their strength. (10)

ਕਰੋ ਹੱਲਾ ਇਨ ਲਈਏ ਮਾਰ ॥ ਮਲੇਰੀਅਨ ਨੇ ਇਮ ਕਰੀ ਬਿਚਾਰ ॥\
ਮੁਹਰੈ ਭਯੋ ਅਖਵਾਜ ਮ੍ਰਦੂਦ ॥ ਆਯੋ ਉੱਪਰ ਖਾਲਸੇ ਜ਼ੂਦ ॥੧੧॥

"Let us charge them now, and kill them where they lie."\
In this way the men of Maler took counsel together;\
Khwaja the Accursed[^7] went to the front, at the head of them all,\
and came down upon the Khalsa swiftly, without a moment's delay. (11)

ਇਕ ਇਕ ਦੋ ਦੋ ਗੋਲੀ ਥੀ ਰਹੀ ॥ ਸੋਊ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਦਾਗੇ ਦਈ ॥\
ਅਖ੍ਵਾਜ ਸੀਸ ਇਕ ਲਗ ਗਈ ਕਾਰੀ ॥ ਪੈ ਗਯੋ ਤੁਰਤ ਸੁ ਭੂਮ ਮਝਾਰੀ ॥੧੨॥

The Singhs had only one or two bullets each left to them,\
and the Singhs fired off those last few bullets at the enemy;\
one of them struck Khwaja in the head, a deadly shot,\
and at once he fell, crashing down onto the ground. (12)

ਮੁਯੋ ਅਖ੍ਵਾਜ ਜੁ ਗੁਰ ਕਹਯੋ ਮ੍ਰਦੂਦ ॥ ਮਲੇਰੀਯਨ ਮੈ ਥੋ ਬੜੋ ਗਦੂਦ ॥੧੩॥

So Khwaja died, the man the Guru had called the Accursed,\
who had been the hardest knot of gristle[^8] among all the men of Maler. (13)

ਤਿਸਕੋ ਚੁੱਕਨ ਆਏ ਤਿਸ ਭਾਈ ॥ ਉਨ ਦੋਇਨ ਭੀ ਜਾਨ ਗੁਵਾਈ ॥\
ਸ਼ੇਰ ਮੁਹੰਮਦ ਸੁਨ ਕਰ ਆਯਾ ॥ ਹੁਇ ਕਰ ਜ਼ਖ਼ਮੀ ਪਿੱਛੈ ਧਾਯਾ ॥੧੪॥

His brothers came forward to carry his body away from the field,\
and the two of them lost their lives there as well;\
Sher Muhammad heard of it, and came up into the fight,\
but he was wounded, and he turned and fled back to the rear. (14)

ਝੂਲਨਾ ਛੰਦ *Jhulna Chhand*

ਬਚਯੋ ਏਕ ਮੁਹੰਮਦੋ ਸ਼ੇਰ ਓਹੀ ਜਿਸੈ ਹਾਹ ਦਾ ਨਾਅਰਾ ਮਾਰਿਆ ਸਾ ॥\
ਵੇਲੇ ਸ਼ਾਹਿਬਜ਼ਾਦਿਆਂ ਦੇ ਕਤਲ ਕੇ ਸ਼ੀਰ ਖੋਇਰੇ ਮੁਖੋਂ ਉਚਾਰਿਆ ਸਾ ॥\
ਗੁਰਾਂ ਸੁਨਕੈ ਮੁਖੋਂ ਸੀ ਵਾਕ ਕੀਤਾ ਰਹੀ ਜੜ੍ਹ ਯੌਂ ਓਸ ਨੂੰ ਤਾਰਿਆ ਸਾ ॥\
ਸੋਈ ਅੱਜ ਤਾਈਂ ਗੁਰੂ ਵਾਕ ਫੁਰਦਾ ਜੋਊ ਆਖਕੈ ਫੇਰ ਨਹਿਂ ਹਾਰਿਆ ਸਾ ॥੧੫॥

Only one was saved, Sher Muhammad, that same man who had raised his voice in protest,[^9]\
who at the time of the killing of the Sahibzadas[^10] had said with his own lips, "They are children still at the breast."\
The Guru heard of it and spoke a word from his own mouth, "His root shall remain," and in this way he saved him;\
that word of the Guru holds good to this very day, and what he once spoke has never failed. (15)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਤਿਨਕੀ ਲੋਥ ਸੁ ਲੈ ਮੁੜੇ ਨੱਠੇ ਮਲੇਰੇ ਵੱਲ ॥\
ਤੀਨਹੁ ਗੋਰਨ ਮਹਿ ਗਡੇ ਮਨ ਭਯੋ ਬਜੀਰੈ ਤ੍ਰਥੱਲ ॥੧੬॥

They took up the bodies of the fallen and turned back, fleeing toward Maler,\
and buried all three in their graves; and Wazira's heart was thrown into turmoil, shaken to its very depths. (16)

Kulwant Singh is named in full in the notes; Baba Santa Singh Akali is BSS. Cite as Panth Prakash 38.4.*

### Notes

[^1]: Ropar: Rupnagar, on the Satluj where the river comes out of the hills, north of Sirhind; the pass at Ropar is the way down from the hills into the plain.
[^2]: Wazira: the poet's scornful short form for Wazir Khan, the nawab of Sirhind.
[^3]: men of Maler: the poet's Malerīe, Malerīan, the Pathans of Malerkotla, south-west of Sirhind.
[^4]: Khwaja Khidar: the poet writes Akhvāj (Akhvājā) Khidar; the name is usually Khwaja Khizar.
[^5]: cannon, swivel guns and field guns: the poet's top, jambūre and rahkale; the rahkalā was a light gun drawn on a cart.
[^6]: Turks: the poet's word for the Muslim rulers and their armies; here the Pathans of Maler.
[^7]: Accursed: the poet's mardūd (Persian), cast out, accursed. BSS: the Guru gave him the name at Chamkaur, where he hid among the dead to save his life. Kulwant Singh: the coward, who would not come out and face the Guru in single combat.
[^8]: hardest knot of gristle: the poet's gadūd, a gland, a hard lump. BSS: the tough lump on the head of a hornless goat, which a sword bounces off; so, the man hardest to kill. Kulwant Singh: the most formidable and fleshy of the Pathans.
[^9]: raised his voice in protest: the poet's hāh dā nāʿrā, the cry of protest against a wrong; this verse is in Punjabi. BSS: he told Wazir Khan that children at the breast are not killed, and that such a crime is written nowhere in the Quran.
[^10]: Sahibzadas: the two younger sons of Guru Gobind Singh, put to death at Sirhind on Wazir Khan's order.

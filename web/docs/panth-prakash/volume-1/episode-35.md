# Panth Prakash, Episode 35

## The Episode of the Town of Samana

### ('bajīrai kīyo ġazab jo'...: 'The outrage Wazira did'...)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਤਬ ਬੰਦੈ ਨੈ ਦੌੜ ਕਰ ਲਿਯੋ ਸਮਾਣਾ ਮਾਰ ॥\
ਫਤੇ ਆਈ ਫਤੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਕੋ ਸੁ ਬੰਦੇ ਕੀਯੋ ਫੁਜਦਾਰ ॥੨੦॥

Then Banda swept down upon the town of Samana[^1] at full speed, stormed it and sacked it;\
the victory fell to Fateh Singh,[^2] true to his name, who had led the fight, and Banda made him commander.[^3] (1)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਲਯੋ ਮਾਰ ਜਬ ਸ਼ਹਿਰ ਸਮਾਣੋ ॥ ਤਬ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਸਿਰ ਕਹਿਰ ਬਿਹਾਣੋ ॥\
ਬਿਨ ਬੋਦੀ ਜੋ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਪਾਯੋ ॥ ਤੂੰਬੜਿ ਜਿਮ ਤਿਨ ਸੀਸ ਉਡਾਯੋ ॥੨੧॥

When the Singhs had stormed and taken the town of Samana,\
a storm of wrath broke upon the heads of the Turks[^4] who lived there:\
any man the Singhs found with no bodī[^5] on his head, as every Hindu has,\
they sent his head flying from his shoulders like a gourd. (2)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਬਜੀਰੈ ਕਾਰਾ ਜਬ ਕੀਓ ਸਮਾਣੀਏਂ ਭੀ ਥੇ ਨਾਲ ॥\
ਫੌਜਦਾਰ ਉਹ ਬੀ ਹੁਤੈ ਪਾਲਕੀ ਬਾਈ ਵਾਰ ॥੨੨॥

For when Wazira[^6] committed his foul crime against the Guru's young sons, the men of Samana had stood beside him in it;\
they too were commanders and great men, the lords who rode out of Samana in twenty-two palanquins.[^7] (3)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਸ਼ਹਿਰ ਸਮਾਣੋਂ ਜਬ ਲਯੋ ਮਾਰ ॥ ਗਯੋ ਦਲਿੱਦ੍ਰ ਦਲਿੱਦ੍ਰਨ ਵਾਰ ॥\
ਤਬ ਬੰਦੈ ਨੇ ਚਿੱਤ ਬਿਚਾਰੀ ॥ ਬਜੀਰੈ ਲੁਟੀਐ ਫੌਜ ਕਰ ਭਾਰੀ ॥੨੩॥

When the Singhs had stormed and sacked the town of Samana,\
the poor among them were rid of their poverty, so much wealth fell into their hands.[^8]\
Then Banda thought the matter over in his mind and resolved:\
*Let us make the army great, and then fall upon Wazira and plunder him.* (4)

ਉਹਾਂ ਬਜੀਰੇ ਘਲੇ ਹਲਕਾਰੇ ॥ ਬੰਦੈ ਕਹਯੋ ਤਬ ਅਗਮ ਬਿਚਾਰੇ ॥\
ਇਕ ਹੈ ਕਾਣੋ ਇਕ ਹਥ ਏਕ ॥ ਪਕੜ ਮੰਗਾਵੋ ਬਜਾਰੋਂ ਦੇਖ ॥੨੪॥

Wazira sent his spies over to Samana to find out what had happened there;\
but Banda, who could see what was hidden from the eye, said to the Singhs,\
"Two spies have come: one of them is blind in one eye, and the other has only one hand;\
go and look for them in the bazaar, seize them, and bring them here to me." (5)

ਜਹਿਂ ਦੱਸਯੋ ਸੋ ਤਹਾਂ ਸੇ ਖੜੇ ॥ ਲਯਾਏ ਸਿੰਘ ਉਹਾਂ ਤੇ ਫੜੇ ॥\
ਜੂਤੇ ਮਾਰਤੈ ਗੰਜੇ ਕਰੇ ॥ ਨਾਮ ਬਜੀਰੈ ਲੈ ਲੈ ਖਰੇ ॥੨੫॥

The two spies were standing in the very place that Banda had named,\
and the Singhs seized them there and brought them back to him.\
They beat the spies about the head with their shoes until not a hair was left on them,\
and all the while they threw Wazira's name at them, again and again. (6)

ਲਿਖ ਕੈ ਚਿਠੇ ਉਨ ਹਥ ਦਏ ॥ ਕਹਯੋ ਬਜੀਰੇ ਹਮ ਅਬ ਅਏ ॥\
ਯਹ ਸੁਨ ਬਜੀਰੇ ਚਿੰਤਾ ਜਾਨੀ ॥ ਛਡੈ ਨ ਬੰਦਾ ਯੌ ਮਨ ਮਾਨੀ ॥੨੬॥

Then Banda wrote out letters and put them into the spies' hands to carry back,\
telling Wazira, "Now we are coming for you, to settle our account."[^9]\
When Wazira heard this, he was filled with dread and deep worry,\
for his heart told him that Banda would never let him go. (7)

ਸ਼ਹਿਰ ਮਾਂਹਿ ਪੈ ਗਯੋ ਥਰਥੱਲਾ ॥ ਘਰ ਘਰ ਚਲੀ ਬੰਦੇ ਕੀ ਗੱਲਾਂ ॥\
ਆਯੋ ਆਈ ਬਹੁ ਬੰਦੈ ਭਈ ॥ ਚਹਤ ਕਤਲ ਵਹ ਸ਼ਹਿਰੈ ਕਈ ॥੨੭॥

The whole city of Sirhind shook with terror at the news,\
and in every house, from door to door, the people talked of nothing but Banda.\
Everywhere they were saying, "He is coming, he has come,"\
and "He means to slaughter the people of many cities." (8)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਬਜੀਰੈ ਕੀਯੋ ਗਜਬ ਜੋ ਭਯੋ ਸ਼ਹਰ ਕੇ ਨਾਇ ॥\
ਸੋ ਦੁਖ ਸੋ ਮੁਖ ਗੁਰ ਕਹਯੋ ਇਟ ਦਰਿਆਏ ਪਾਇ ॥੨੮॥

"The outrage that Wazira committed has now fallen upon the name of our whole city;\
in his grief at it the Guru said with his own lips, 'Throw the bricks of Sirhind into the river.'[^10] (9)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਸੋਊ ਵਖਤ ਅਬ ਜਾਨੀਯਤ ਆਯਾ ॥ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਬਚਨ ਨ ਖਾਲੀ ਜਾਯਾ ॥\
ਉਸਕੀ ਕਰਨੀ ਹਮ ਸਿਰ ਆਈ ॥ ਐਸੇ ਆਖੈ ਸ਼ਹਰ ਲੁਕਾਈ ॥੨੯॥

"Now, it seems, the hour that the Guru spoke of has come;\
the word of the Satguru never falls empty.\
What that one man did has come down upon all our heads."\
This is what the people of the city were saying to one another. (10)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਕਹੈਂ ਸਿੱਖ ਪਛੁਤਾਇ ਤਬ ਸੁਲਖਣੈ ਮਸੰਦੈ ਬਾਤ ॥\
ਜੋ ਤੂੰ ਹਮ ਕੋ ਆਖਤੋ ਮਰਤ ਗੁਰਨ ਸੁਤ ਸਾਥ ॥੩੦॥

Then the Sikhs of Sirhind, full of regret, turned on Sulakhan the masaṅd[^11] and said to him,\
"If only you had told us then and called us out, we would have died at the side of the Guru's sons. (11)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਤੁੰ ਕਹ ਹਮਤੇ ਦਰਬ ਦਿਲਾਵਤ ॥ ਸਾਥ ਸ੍ਵਰਨ ਔ ਮੋਤੀ ਤੁਲਾਵਤ ॥\
ਸੋਊ ਦਰਬ ਸਭ ਲੁੱਟੀ ਜਾਉਗੁ ॥ ਪ੍ਰਾਣ ਹਮੈ ਲੈ ਸੰਗ ਸਿਧਾਉਗੁ ॥੩੧॥

"Had you only said the word, you could have taken all the wealth you wanted from us;\
we would have weighed the Sahibzadas against gold and pearls to buy back their lives.\
Now all that wealth of ours will be plundered and lost for nothing,\
and it will take our lives along with it when it goes." (12)

Gurmukhi text of record from the edition of the Institute of Sikh Studies, Chandigarh, volume 1, episodes 1 to 81, with the English translation of Kulwant Singh. Punjabi commentary from the Prachin Panth Prakash Steek, part 1, of Singh Sahib Baba Santa Singh Akali, Jathedar of the 96 Krori Budha Dal. Kulwant Singh is named in full in the notes; Baba Santa Singh Akali is BSS. Cite as Panth Prakash 35.4.*

### Notes

[^1]: Samana: a rich Mughal town south-west of where Patiala now stands. The source numbers this episode's verses 20 to 31, running on from Episode 34; the English counts them 1 to 12.
[^2]: Fateh Singh: the leader of the Malwa jatha (Episode 34); fateh means victory, and the line plays on his name. BSS: he fought at the front, cleared the field and drove the Turks off.
[^3]: commander: the poet's fujdār (faujdar). BSS: Banda put the whole army in his charge and made him its general; Kulwant Singh reads him as made governor of Samana.
[^4]: Turks: the poet's word for the Muslim rulers and their people.
[^5]: bodī: the tuft of hair a Hindu leaves uncut on the crown of the head. BSS: Muslims kept no bodī, so a man without one was taken for a Turk. Kulwant Singh reads the line the other way round.
[^6]: Wazira: the poet's scornful short form (also Bajira) for Wazir Khan, the nawab of Sirhind, who had the Guru's two younger sons put to death.
[^7]: palanquins: covered litters carried on men's shoulders, ridden only by the great. BSS: twenty-two palanquins went out of Samana, for twenty-two commanders and nawabs lived there, and all of them had shared in Wazira's crime.
[^8]: BSS: the Singhs who had lacked arms, clothes and horses came away with gold and silver and good horses, enough to go home and repay the loans they had taken at twice and four times over (Episode 33).
[^9]: BSS gives the letter as a challenge: eat and drink while you can, call whoever you want to your aid, the Khalsa is coming to settle the account of the Sahibzadas.
[^10]: the river: the tradition is that the Guru, hearing of his sons' death, said Sirhind would be pulled down and its bricks thrown into the river. Kulwant Singh names the river as the Satluj.
[^11]: masaṅd: one of the Guru's agents, who gathered the Sikhs' offerings in a district. Sulakhan was masaṅd of the Sikhs of Sirhind (Kulwant Singh: of Chanarthal, near Sirhind). BSS: there were about three thousand Sikh households in Sirhind, and they all slept; not one of them spoke up in the court.

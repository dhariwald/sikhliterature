# Panth Prakash, Episode 27

## The First Story of Banda

### (Dādū Duār mahant Jait Rām nāl mel: The Meeting with Mahant Jait Ram at Dadu Dwar)

>Note:\
>Name of banis (compositions) recited by the Singhs\
>Name of the mahant who met the Guru at Dadu Dwar\
>The Mahant's request to the Guru\
>The Guru's response\

*Dohra* ਦੋਹਰਾ

ਜਬ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਦੱਖਣ ਪੁੱਜੇ ਜਹਿਂ ਥੋ ਦਾਦੂ ਦ੍ਵਾਰ ॥\
ਡੇਰਾ ਲਾਯਾ ਦੇਖ ਥਾਮ ਸੰਗ ਪੰਜ ਸਤ ਸਿੰਘ ਹਜ਼ਾਰ ॥੧॥

When the Satguru reached the south, where Dadu Dwar[^1] stood,\
he saw the spot and pitched his camp, with five to seven thousand Singhs about him. (1)

*Chaupai* ਚੌਪਈ

ਬਜੈਂ ਨਗਾਰੇ ਝੂਲਹਿਂ ਨਿਸ਼ਾਨ ॥ ਪੜ੍ਹੈਂ ਬਾਣੀਆਂ ਕਿਸ ਕਰੈਂ ਨ ਕਾਣ ॥\
ਚੰਡੀ ਚਰਿਤ੍ਰ ਔ ਚੰਡੀ ਕੀ ਵਾਰ ॥ ਅਕਾਲ ਉਸਤਤ ਕੋ ਕਰੈਂ ਉਚਾਰ ॥੨॥

The war drums[^2] beat and the banners streamed;\
they recited the sacred verse and bowed to no one:\
Chandi Charitra and Chandi ki Var,\
and they chanted the Akal Ustat aloud.[^3] (2)

ਜਿਮ ਜੇਠ ਤਪਤ ਮਧ ਬਰਖਾ ਹੋਇ ॥ ਬੋਲੈ ਦਾਦਰ ਸੁਖ ਪਾਵੈ ਸੋਇ ॥\
ਘੋਰ ਘਟਾ ਸੁਨ ਮੋਰ ਪੁਕਾਰੈਂ ॥ ਇਸ ਛਬ ਸੋਂ ਸਿਖ ਬਾਣੀ ਉਚਾਰੈਂ ॥੩॥

As when rain falls in the burning heat of Jeth,[^4]\
and the frog calls out and is at ease,\
and the peacocks cry when they hear the dark clouds thunder:\
with such beauty the Sikhs recited the sacred verse. (3)

*Dohra* ਦੋਹਰਾ

ਕਿਤੈ ਗ੍ਰੰਥ ਕਿਤ ਪੁਸਤਕਹਿਂ ਕਿਤੈਂ ਪੜ੍ਹੈਂ ਮੁਖ ਵਾਕ ॥\
ਕਈ ਪੜ੍ਹੈਂ ਰਬਾਬੀ ਰਬਾਬ ਲੈ ਲਗ ਰਹਯੋ ਇੰਦ੍ਰਾਸਨ ਠਾਠ ॥੪॥

Some read from the Granth, some from books, and some recited the words the Guru spoke;\
many rabābīs sang, rabāb in hand:[^5] the splendour of Indra's court[^6] was set out there. (4)

*Savaiya* ਸ੍ਵੈਯਾ

ਕਿਤ ਝਾਂਝ ਮ੍ਰਿੰਦਗ ਉਪੰਗ ਬਜੈ ਕਿਤ ਦੁਇ ਕਰ ਤਾਲ ਰਬਾਬ ਬਜੈ ॥\
ਜਲ ਬੀਨ ਸੁ ਬੀਨ ਪ੍ਰਵੀਨ ਸਜੈ ਕਿਤ ਤਾਰ ਸਤਾਰ ਤੰਬੂਰ ਸਜੈ ॥\
ਜਛ ਕਿੰਨਰ ਨਾਰਦ ਹਾਹਿ ਹੂ ਹੂ ਧੁਨ ਤੋਮਰ ਉਲੂਕ ਸੁਨ ਸਾਜ ਸਜੈ ॥\
ਇਮ ਠਾਠ ਠਟਯੋ ਸੁ ਹਜ਼ੂਰ ਗੁਰੂ ਲਖ ਹੋਤ ਇੰਦ੍ਰਾਸਨ ਲਾਜ ਲਜੇ ॥੫॥

Here cymbals, the mridang and the upaṅg played; there hands kept time and the rabāb sounded;[^7]\
the skilled set out their water bowls and vinas; here the strings of sitar and tambura rang;\
such music rose as the Yakshas and Kinnars make, and Narad, Haha and Huhu, Tumbar and Uluk;[^8]\
such splendour was set out in the Guru's presence that Indra's court, seeing it, was put to shame.[^9] (5)

*Chaupai* ਚੌਪਈ

ਕਈ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਕੈ ਆ ਚਰਨੀਂ ਪੜੈਂ ॥ ਲੈ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਹੁਕਮ ਕਈ ਡੇਰੋ ਕਰੈਂ ॥\
ਧੁੂਪ ਧੁਖਾਇ ਕਈ ਗਿਰਦੈ ਫਿਰਾਹਿ ॥ ਜਿਵੈਂ ਧ੍ਰਵ ਗਿਰਦ ਤਾਰੈਂ ਘੁਮਾਹਿਂ ॥੬॥

Many came and fell at the Satguru's feet;\
many took the Satguru's command and pitched their camps;\
many lit incense and went circling round him,\
as the stars wheel around the Pole Star.[^10] (6)

ਕੋਊ ਚਰਨਨ ਛੁਹ ਮੂਧਾ ਪਰੈ ॥ ਜਨ ਡੰਡੌਤ ਦੇਵ ਇੰਦ੍ਰੈ ਕਰੈ ॥\
ਤੰਬੂ ਚਾਨਣੀ ਕਈ ਫੜ ਖੜੈਂ ॥ ਮੂਲ ਬਿਰਖ ਜਿਮ ਸੌ ਰਹਿਂ ਅੜੈ ॥੭॥

One touched his feet and lay face down on the ground,\
as the gods lie full length before Indra;\
many stood holding the tents and canopies,\
firm as a tree held fast by its root. (7)

ਜਨਕ ਰਹੈਂ ਥੰਭਾ ਸੇ ਗਾਡ ॥ ਵਾਲ ਧੂਪ ਮੈਂ ਜਾਹਿ ਨ ਛਾਡ ॥੮॥

They stood as if planted there like pillars,\
and would not leave in wind or sun.[^11] (8)

*Dohra* ਦੋਹਰਾ

ਸੂਰਜਮੁਖੀ ਸਮ ਸੂਰਜੈ ਬਿਜਨ ਸੁ ਬਿੱਜ ਸਮਾਨ ॥\
ਬਾਦਲ ਜਿਮ ਛਤ੍ਰ ਘੁਮੇ ਬਡ ਤੰਬੂ ਘਟਨ ਸਮਾਨ ॥੯॥

The sun-standards[^12] shone like the sun, the fans flashed like lightning;\
the royal canopy wheeled like a cloud, and the great tents were like massed clouds. (9)

ਇੰਦ੍ਰਹਿ ਜਿਮ ਗੁਰ ਬਹੈਂ ਹਥ ਇੰਦ੍ਰ ਧਨੁ ਜੈਸ ਕਮਾਨ ॥\
ਚੌਰ ਮੁਠੋ ਐਸੇ ਫਿਰੈ ਜਾਨਕ ਮੋਰ ਨਚਾਨ ॥੧੦॥

The Guru sat like Indra; the bow[^13] in his hand was like Indra's bow, the rainbow;\
the chaur[^14] swept round in its grip as though a peacock were dancing. (10)

*Chaupai* ਚੌਪਈ

ਤਹਾਂ ਸ਼ਹਿਰ ਮੈਂ ਪਰ ਗਈ ਧਾਕ ॥ ਦੇਖਣ ਲੋਕ ਲੱਗ ਗਈ ਲਾਕ ॥\
ਦਰਸ਼ਨ ਤੇ ਉਨ ਮਨ ਪਤਿਆਯਾ ॥ ਲਈ ਚੜ੍ਹਾਵੈ ਸ਼ਹਰ ਚਲ ਆਯਾ ॥੧੧॥

Awe of him fell upon the town there;\
people came in a long line to see him.\
At the sight of him their hearts believed,\
and the whole town came bringing offerings. (11)

*Dohra* ਦੋਹਰਾ

ਬਡੋ ਅਚੰਭਾ ਤਿਹ ਲਖਾ ਅਚੰਭਤ ਭਯੋ ਸੰਸਾਰ ॥\
ਆਏ ਦਰਸ਼ਨ ਸਤਿਗੁਰੂ ਛਡ ਛਡ ਦਾਦੂ ਦ੍ਵਾਰ ॥੧੨॥

They saw a great wonder, and all the world was filled with wonder;\
one after another they left Dadu Dwar and came to see the Satguru. (12)

*Chaupai* ਚੌਪਈ

ਦਾਦੂ ਦ੍ਵਾਰੇ ਖਬਰੋ ਪਈ ॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਆਯੋ ਦਸਮੋਂ ਜਈ ॥\
ਤਿਸ ਕਉ ਦੱਯੈ ਧਾਮ ਖੁਲਾਈ ॥ ਮਹੰਤ ਆਯੋ ਸੰਗ ਚੇਲੇ ਲਾਈ ॥੧੩॥

The news reached Dadu Dwar:\
"The tenth Nanak has come;\
let us open our house to him."\
The mahant[^15] came, bringing his disciples with him. (13)

ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਆਦਰ ਤਿਸ ਬਹੁ ਕੀਯੋ ॥ ਸੰਤ ਸਾਧ ਮਿਰਜਾਦਾ ਥੀਯੋ ॥\
ਦਰਸਨ ਪਰਸਨ ਭਏ ਪ੍ਰਸੰਨ ॥ ਕਹਯੋ ਤੂੰ ਨਾਨਕ ਗੁਰ ਭਯੋ ਧੰਨ ॥੧੪॥

The Satguru showed him great honour,\
as is the custom among saints and holy men.\
At the sight and touch of him they rejoiced;\
the mahant said, "You are Nanak, the Guru. Blessed are you." (14)

ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਕਹਯੋ ਸੰਤ ਸਭ ਏਕ ॥ ਨੀਰ ਛੀਰ ਮਿਲ ਤਜੈ ਬਿਬੇਕ ॥\
ਜੈਤ ਰਾਮ ਥੋ ਨਾਮ ਮਹੰਤ ॥ ਭਲੋ ਪੁਰਸ਼ ਉਸ ਆਹਿ ਮਹੰਤ ॥੧੫॥

The Satguru said, "All saints are one,\
as water mixed with milk can no longer be told apart."\
Jait Ram was the mahant's name;\
a good man he was, that mahant. (15)

*Dohra* ਦੋਹਰਾ

ਹਾਥ ਜੋੜ ਠਾਂਢੋ ਭਯੋ ਤੌ ਉਨ ਕਹੀ ਅਰਦਾਸ ॥\
ਧੰਨ ਹਮਾਰੇ ਭਾਗ ਹੈਂ ਤੁਮ ਆਏ ਹਮਰੇ ਪਾਸ ॥੧੬॥

He stood with his hands joined and made this humble request:\
"Blessed is our fortune, that you have come to us. (16)

*Chaupai* ਚੌਪਈ

ਹਮ ਤੇ ਭੋਜ ਸੁ ਇਕ ਡੰਗ ਲੀਜੈ ॥ ਸਾਧ ਸੰਗਤ ਕੋ ਧਰਮ ਰਖ ਲੀਜੈ ॥\
ਤੌ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਹਸ ਐਸ ਉਚਾਰਾ ॥ ਤੁਮ ਤੇ ਭੋਜਨ ਹਮ ਸਰੈ ਨ ਸਾਰਾ ॥੧੭॥

"Take one meal from us,\
and let us keep the duty of the holy company."[^16]\
Then the Satguru laughed and said,\
"You could not supply the food for all of us. (17)

ਹਮ ਸੰਗ ਹੈਂ ਬਹੁ ਮਾਸਅਹਾਰੀ ॥ ਉਨਕੀ ਖੁਧਯਾ ਮਿਟੈ ਕਿਮ ਸਾਰੀ ॥\
ਉਨ ਮੁਖ ਤੇ ਤਬ ਐਸ ਉਚਾਰੀ ॥ ਦਾਦੂ ਸਤ ਹਮ ਖ੍ਵਾਹਿਂ ਜੁਵਾਰੀ ॥੧੮॥

"Many meat-eaters are with us;[^17]\
how could all their hunger be stilled?"\
Then these words came from the mahant's mouth:\
"By Dadu's truth, we shall feed them millet."[^18] (18)

### Notes

[^1]: The seat of the Dadupanthis at Naraina, near Jaipur in Rajasthan, where Dadu Dayal (1544 to 1603), the saint whose followers they are, died. The poet writes Dādū Dwār; the Guru passed it in 1707 on his way south.
[^2]: The poet's nagāre. The banners are nishān.
[^3]: Compositions of Guru Gobind Singh in the Dasam Granth: Chandi Charitra and Chandi ki Var tell of the goddess Chandi's wars with the demons; Akal Ustat is the praise of Akal Purakh.
[^4]: Jeth: the hottest month, May to June.
[^5]: The rabāb: the stringed instrument of Sikh kīrtan since Bhai Mardana; the rabābīs are its players.
[^6]: Indra: king of the gods in the Puranas, whose court is famed for its music and dancing. The poet's word is Indrāsan, Indra's throne.
[^7]: jhāñjh cymbals; mridang, a two-headed drum; upaṅg, a reed instrument; the rabāb with the beat kept on both hands. In the next line jal bīn, tuned water bowls struck like a xylophone; bīn, the vina; sitār and tambūrā, stringed instruments.
[^8]: The musicians of the gods in the Puranas: Yakshas and Kinnars, Kuber's attendants; Narad the sage; Haha, Huhu and Tumbar (Tumburu), singers of Indra's court; Uluk, the owl from whom Narad is said to have learned music.
[^9]: The poet's lakh also means a hundred thousand: Baba Santa Singh Akali reads it so, that a hundred thousand courts of Indra would be put to shame.
[^10]: Dhruv, the Pole Star, round which the heavens turn.
[^11]: Baba Santa Singh Akali's reading is that storms were blowing and the Singhs held the tent poles against the wind.
[^12]: sūrajmukhī: sun-faced standards carried with the banners, set with jewels and bearing the Khalsa's emblem (Baba Santa Singh Akali).
[^13]: The poet's kamān.
[^14]: chaur: the whisk waved over the Guru, here of peacock feathers (Baba Santa Singh Akali).
[^15]: mahant: the head of a religious house.
[^16]: Baba Santa Singh Akali: it is the duty of a holy man's seat to feed the holy man who comes to it.
[^17]: Baba Santa Singh Akali's reading is that the meat-eaters are the Guru's hawks and hounds, not his Sikhs; the next episode opens with the hawks eating millet (Panth Prakash 28.1). Kulwant Singh reads them as the Singhs.
[^18]: The poet's juvārī: jowar, or sorghum, the great millet, a coarse grain.

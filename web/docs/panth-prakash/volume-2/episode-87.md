# Panth Prakash, Episode 87

## The Story of the Randhawas of Chumunda and Kairon Nangal

### (randhāvan siṅghan dayyai mravāi: They had the Randhawa Singhs killed.)

>**Questions**
>
>**Vocabulary:** Kalals (ਕਲਾਲ), "hanne hanne patshah" (ਹੰਨੈ ਹੰਨੈ ਹੁਇ ਬਹਿਂ ਪਤਸ਼ਾਹਿ)
>
>**Summarize (2–3 sentences each):**
>
>1. How the Randhawa Singhs lived and escaped the first raid.
>2. The plot of the Sultanis and Kalals, and their motivation
ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਸੁਨੋ ਸਾਖੀ ਔਰ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕੀ ਜਿਨ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਸਿਰ ਸਿਰ ਲਾਇ ॥\
ਸਿਦਕ ਰਖਯੌ ਗੁਰ ਬਚਨ ਪਰ ਭਯੋ ਕੇਸਨ ਸਾਸਨ ਨਿਬਾਹਿ ॥੧॥

Hear the story of other Singhs, who laid their own heads on the heads of the Turks;[^1]\
they kept their steadfast faith in the Guru's word, and held to it with their uncut hair to their last breath. (1)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਨਾਮ ਠਾਮ ਜਾਤ ਗੋਤ ਬਤਾਊਂ ॥ ਸਿਖੀ ਸਾਂਝ ਹਿਤ ਟਹਲ ਉਠਾਊਂ ॥\
ਸੁਨੀ ਜਿਸੈ ਬਿਧ ਬਡਿਅਨ ਪਾਸ ॥ ਕਹੂੰ ਤਿਮੈ ਪੰਥ ਰਖ ਹੇਤ ਦਾਸ ॥੨॥

I shall tell their names and their places, their caste and their clan;\
for the sake of the bond of sikhī[^2] I take this service on myself.\
Just as I heard it from my elders,\
so I, the Panth's servant, shall tell it, for love of the Panth. (2)

ਹੁਤਿ ਰੰਧਾਵੈ ਚੁਮੁੰਡੇ ਬਾਈ ਬਾਸ ॥ ਮਧ ਇਕ ਕੈਰੋਂ ਨੰਗਲ ਭਾਸ ॥\
ਉਨ ਮਧ ਤੇ ਕੁਛ ਗੁਰ ਪੰਥ ਆਏ ॥ ਖੰਡੈ ਪਹੁਲ ਲਈ ਸ਼ਸਤ੍ਰ ਉਠਾਏ ॥੩॥

The Randhawas[^3] lived in twenty-two villages around Chumunda,[^4]\
and among them was one called Kairon Nangal.[^5]\
From among them some came into the Guru's Panth;\
they took the khanda's pāhul[^6] and took up arms. (3)

ਜਿਮ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਆਗਯਾ ਦਈ ॥ ਵਹੀ ਭਾਂਤਿ ਤਿਨ ਕਰਨੀ ਠਈ ॥\
ਰਲੇ ਪੰਥ ਗੁਰ ਕਰੇਂ ਦੰਗਾਹੀ ॥ ਲਹੌਰੇ ਦਿੱਲੀ ਰਾਹੁ ਲੁਟ ਖਾਹੀਂ ॥੪॥

As the Sri Satguru had commanded,\
in just that way they set about their deeds.\
They joined the Guru's Panth and took to fighting,\
and lived by plundering on the road from Lahore to Delhi. (4)

ਜੋਊ ਚਕਯੋ ਹੁਤ ਖਾਲਸੇ ਤੌਰ ॥ ਇਨ ਭੀ ਤਿਮਹਿਂ ਮਚਾਯੋ ਰੌਰ ॥\
ਸੋਊ ਖਬਰ ਯਹਿ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਪਾਈ ॥ ਆਏ ਮਾਰਨ ਉਨ ਕਰ ਧਾਈ ॥੫॥

The way of fighting the Khalsa had taken up,\
they took up too, and raised an uproar.\
The Turks came to hear the news of it,\
and fell on them in a swift attack to kill them. (5)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਸਿੰਘਨ ਪੈ ਗੜ ਨਹਿਂ ਹੁਤੋ ਜਿਤ ਕੀ ਓਟ ਲੜਾਹਿਂ ॥\
ਉਠ ਨਠ ਰੋਹੀ ਮਧ ਛਿਪਹਿਂ ਕਿ ਖੂਹਨ ਖਡਨ ਮਾਂਹਿ ॥੬॥

The Singhs had no fort behind whose walls they could fight;\
they would get up and run and hide in the waste scrubland, or down in wells and ravines. (6)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਸੁਨੀ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਜਬ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਅਵਾਈ ॥ ਛਡ ਕਰ ਘਰ ਗਏ ਬਣਹਿ ਲੁਕਾਈ ॥\
ਹੁਤੇ ਉਨੈਂ ਕੇ ਟੱਬਰ ਜੇਊ ॥ ਰਲ ਮਿਲ ਲੋਕਨ ਬਚ ਰਹੇ ਤੇਊ ॥੭॥

When the Singhs heard word that the Turks were coming,\
they left their homes and went to hide in the forest.\
Their families, the ones they left behind,\
mixed in among the other people and so were spared. (7)

ਦਿਨੈ ਸਿੰਘ ਹੁਤ ਥੇ ਤਿਹ ਨਾਂਹਿ ॥ ਰਾਤ ਪਰੀ ਆਵਤ ਘਰ ਮਾਂਹਿ ॥\
ਇਸੀ ਬਾਰ ਤਉ ਇਮ ਬਚ ਰਹੇ ॥ ਹਥ ਨ ਆਏ ਵੈ ਖਾਲੀ ਗਏ ॥੮॥

By day the Singhs were never there;\
when night fell, they came back into their homes.\
So this time they escaped in this way;\
they did not fall into the Turks' hands, and the Turks went back empty-handed. (8)

ਜਬ ਖਾਲੀ ਤੁਰਕ ਇਮ ਗਏ ਆਈ ਨਮੋਸ਼ੀ ਤਾਂਹਿ ॥\
ਚਿੱਤੈ ਜਿਮ ਝੂਰਤ ਫਿਰਹਿਂ ਮਾਰਨ ਸਿੰਘ ਫਿਰਾਹਿਂ ॥੯॥

When the Turks went back empty-handed like this, shame came over them;\
like a leopard[^7] (that has missed its kill) they went about brooding, bent on killing the Singhs. (9)

ਤੁਰਕ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਫਿਰ ਚਾਹੈਂ ਮਾਰਨ ॥ ਸਿੰਘ ਭੀ ਚਾਹੈਂ ਵਖਤ ਗੁਜਾਰਨ ॥\
ਕਹੈਂ ਸਿੰਘ ਕਬ ਹਮ ਬਖਤ ਆਵਗੁ ॥ ਹਮ ਤੈ ਲੁਕ ਤੁਰਕ ਜਿੰਦ ਬਚਾਵਗੁ ॥੧੦॥

The Turks still wanted to kill the Singhs,\
and the Singhs wanted only to live through the hard times.\
The Singhs would say, "When will our fortune come,\
when the Turks will hide from us to save their lives? (10)

ਹੰਨੈ ਹੰਨੈ ਹੁਇ ਬਹਿਂ ਪਤਸ਼ਾਹਿ ॥ ਲਹਿਂ ਆਪ ਅਪਨੋ ਮੁਲਖ ਉਗਰਾਹਿ ॥\
ਹਮ ਗੁਰਬਚਨ ਝੂਠ ਤੌ ਨਾਂਹੀ ॥ ਹਮ ਸਹੀ ਜਾਂਇਂ ਦੁਖ ਕਟੈਂ ਤਹਾਂਹੀ ॥੧੧॥

"Every saddle shall seat a Patshah,[^8]\
and each shall gather the revenue of his own land himself.\
The Guru's word to us is never false;\
we go on bearing it all, and in that hour our sorrows will be cut away." (11)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਹੁਤੇ ਵਤਨ ਮਧ ਸਾਕ ਮਿਤਿ ਸਿੰਘ ਬਸੈਂ ਨਿਜ ਦੇਸ ॥\
ਤਿਹੀਂ ਗੁਜਾਰਾ ਸਿੰਘ ਕਰਹਿਂ ਲੁਕ ਛਪ ਰਖ ਗੁਰ ਭੇਸ ॥੧੨॥

They had kin and friends in their homeland, for the Singhs lived in their own country;\
with them the Singhs got by, hiding and keeping out of sight, but never giving up the Guru's dress. (12)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਅਬ ਫਿਰ ਸੁਨੋ ਉਨ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਬਾਤ ॥ ਕਰਾਇ ਦਯੋ ਜਿਮ ਦੁਸ਼ਟਨ ਘਾਤ ॥\
ਹੁਤੋ ਸਜਾਦੋ ਸੁਲਤਾਨੀਓਂ ਗ੍ਰਾਮ ॥ ਉਸ ਕਹਿਂ ਹਿੰਦੂ ਕਰੇ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਕਾਮ ॥੧੩॥

Now hear again the story of those Singhs,\
and how the wicked had them slaughtered.\
There was Sajada,[^9] a village of the Sultanis;[^10]\
they called themselves Hindus,[^11] but they did the Turks' work. (13)

ਉਨ ਸਾਥ ਰਲਾਏ ਮੁਸਲੇ ਕਲਾਲ ॥ ਤੇਊ ਚਾਕਰ ਹੁਤਿ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਨਾਲ ॥\
ਇਮ ਮਿਲ ਦੋਇਨ ਮਤੇ ਮਤਾਏ ॥ ਰੰਧਾਵਨ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਦੱਯੈ ਮ੍ਰਵਾਏ ॥੧੪॥

They drew the Musle[^12] Kalals[^13] in with them,\
for those men were servants of the Turks.\
Meeting together in this way, the two laid their plans\
to have the Randhawa Singhs killed. (14)

ਏ ਆਏ ਅਬ ਹਮਰੇ ਦਾਇ ॥ ਪਹਿਲੋਂ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਤੇ ਭੂਮ ਲਿਖਵਾਇ ॥\
........................... ਗਰਭ ਛੇਦ ਇਨ ਦਯੈ ਕ੍ਰਵਾਇ ॥੧੫॥

"These men have fallen to our chance at last:\
first we shall have the Turks write their land over to us,\
(the third line is missing)[^14]\
and have the wombs (of their women) cut open.[^15] (15)

ਇਮ ਹਮ ਪੈ ਬਹੁ ਤੁਰਕ ਖੁਸ਼ ਹੋਹਿਂ ॥ ਸਿਰੋਪਾਉ ਇਨਾਮ ਚਹਿਂ ਪਾਇਂ ਸੋਇ ॥\
ਗੁਪਤ ਪਾਪ ਦੋਇਨ ਗਿਨ ਲਯੋ ॥ ਜਾਇ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਪੈ ਤਿਮ ਹੀ ਕਹਯੋ ॥੧੬॥

"In this way the Turks will be well pleased with us,\
and we shall win the robes of honour and the rewards we want."\
The two of them settled on this secret sin,\
then went to the Turks and told them just that. (16)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਜੋ ਉਨ ਆਖੀ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਕੋ ਉਨ ਤਿਮ ਹੀ ਮੰਨ ਲੀਨ ॥\
ਗ੍ਰਾਮ ਇਨਾਮ ਔ ਗਰਭ ਛੇਦ ਚੌਧਰ ਲਿਖੀ ਜ਼ਮੀਨ ॥੧੭॥

Whatever they put to the Turks, the Turks agreed to, just as it was said;\
a village as reward, leave to cut open the wombs, the chaudhari's office and the land were all written over to them. (17)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਤੁਰਕ ਚਹਿਤ ਹੁਤ ਥੇ ਯਹੀ ਬਾਤ ॥ ਜਿਮ ਕਿਮ ਹੁਇ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕੋ ਘਾਤ ॥\
ਜਿਮ ਜਿਮ ਉਨਕੋ ਸਿੰਘ ਦਿਸ ਆਵਤ ॥ ਜੋ ਚਾਹਤ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਤੇ ਮਨਵਾਵਤ ॥੧੮॥

This was the very thing the Turks had wanted:\
that by any means at all the Singhs should be destroyed.\
Each time those men caught sight of Singhs,\
they got the Turks to grant them whatever they wished. (18)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਸਿੰਘਨ ਖਬਰ ਯਹਿ ਨਹਿਂ ਭਈ ਉਇ ਗ੍ਵਾਂਢੀ ਸਿਲ ਕਰਵਾਇਂ ॥\
ਉਸੀ ਗਿਰਦ ਛਪ ਦਿਨ ਰਹੈਂ ਰਾਤੀਂ ਆਵੈਂ ਗ੍ਰਾਂਇ ॥੧੯॥

The Singhs had no word of this, that their own neighbours were informing on them;\
they stayed hidden by day round about the village, and came into it at night. (19)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਜਿਸ ਨਿਸ ਦਿਵਸ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕੋ ਹੋਇ ॥ ਸਿੰਘ ਹੁਇ ਇਕੱਤ੍ਰ ਨਿਸ ਤਹਿਂ ਸੋਇ ॥\
ਤਹਿਂ ਦੁਸਟਨ ਸਿਲ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕਰਾਈ ॥ ਇਕ ਸੁਲਤਾਨੀ ਪਿੰਡ ਹੀ ਮਾਹੀ ॥੨੦॥

On whatever night or day was set for the Singhs,\
the Singhs would gather and sleep there that night.\
There the wicked gave the Singhs away;\
one of them was a Sultani in the village itself. (20)

ਸੋ ਸੇਲੂ ਉਨ ਰਖਯੋ ਬਨਾਇ ॥ ਸਭੀ ਖਬਰ ਦਏ ਜਟਨ ਪਹੁੰਚਾਇ ॥\
ਦੇਂ ਜਟ ਆਗੈ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਘਲਾਇ ॥ ਇਮ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਲਈ ਮੌਤ ਤਕਾਇ ॥੨੧॥

The Turks had made this man their informer,[^16]\
and he passed every piece of news to the Jatts;\
the Jatts sent it on ahead to the Turks,\
and in this way they marked out death for the Singhs. (21)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਕਹਿਨੈ ਕੋ ਵੁਹ ਹੁਤ ਹਿੰਦੂ ਕੰਮ ਸੁ ਤੁਰਕ ਕਰਾਹਿ ॥\
ਹਿੰਦੂਅਨ ਹਿੰਦੂ ਫੜ ਦਿਵਹਿ ਮ੍ਰਵਾਵਹਿ ਸੀਸ ਕਟਵਾਇ ॥੨੨॥

They were Hindus in name, but the work they did was the Turks';\
Hindus handed Hindus over to be seized, and had them killed and their heads cut off. (22)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਸਿੰਘਨ ਮੌਤ ਹੁਤ ਨੇੜੇ ਆਈ ॥ ਸਿੰਘ ਵੜੈਂ ਕਿਤ ਨਿਸ ਘਰ ਧਾਈ ॥\
ਦੁਸ਼ਟ ਸੇਲੂ ਦਈ ਸਿਲ ਪਹੁੰਚਾਇ ॥ ਆਇ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਲਯੋ ਘੇਰੋ ਪਾਇ ॥੨੩॥

Death had drawn near the Singhs;\
one night they hurried into a house.\
The wicked informer got word through,\
and the Turks came and threw a ring around it. (23)

ਚਾਰ ਤਰਫ਼ ਤਿਨ ਫੌਜ ਖੜਵਾਈ ॥ ਤੌ ਲੌ ਝਬ ਦੁਸ਼ਟ ਜਟ ਭਏ ਆਈ ॥\
ਘੇਰਾ ਕੀਨੋ ਬਹੁ ਤਕੜਾਇ ॥ ਅੰਦਰੋ ਜਾਣ ਨ ਕੁਤੋ ਪਾਇਂ ॥੨੪॥

They stationed their army on all four sides,\
and by then the wicked Jatts had come running too.\
They drew the ring so tight\
that not even a dog could get out from inside. (24)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਜਿਮ ਦੁਸ਼ਟ ਸੁਲਤਾਨੀ ਜਟ ਕਹੈ ਤਿਮ ਹੀ ਤੁਰਕ ਕਮਾਹਿਂ ॥\
ਰਲ ਮਿਲ ਦੋਊਅਨ ਸਿੰਘ ਕਟੇ ਲਭਯੋ ਸੁ ਛਡਯੋ ਨਾਂਹਿ ॥੨੫॥

Just as the wicked Sultani Jatts told them, so the Turks did;\
together the two of them cut down the Singhs, and spared no one they found. (25)

ਚੌਦਾਂ ਲਭੇ ਉਨ ਬਡਨ ਕੇ ਸੋ ਪੁਰਸ਼ ਦੀਨੇ ਕਾਟ ॥\
ਉਨ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕੀ ਕੁਲ ਸਮਝ ਔਰ ਦੀਏ ਕਟ ਕਾਟ ॥੨੬॥

They found fourteen of the elders, and those men they cut down;\
taking others for the lineage of those Singhs, they hacked them down as well. (26)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਜੋ ਕੋਊ ਉਨਕੀ ਦ੍ਰਿਸ਼ਟੈ ਆਯਾ ॥ ਸੋ ਤੋ ਤੇਗਨ ਮਧ ਕਟਾਯਾ ॥\
ਅਕੈ ਕਿਤੈ ਉਨ ਹੋਊਗੁ ਗਯੋ ॥ ਇਮ ਕਰ ਟੰਢੁਮੂਲ ਉਨ ਹੋਊਗੋ ਰਹਯੋ ॥੨੭॥

Whoever came into their sight\
was cut down by their teghs;[^17]\
or else, if one of them had gone off somewhere,\
in that way some stump of their root[^18] may have been left. (27)

ਐਸੀ ਭਾਂਤਿ ਦੁਖ ਸਹਯੋ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਮਝੈਲਨ ॥ ਮੁੜ ਜਾਨੈ ਰਾਜ ਪੰਥ ਪਾਯੋ ਹੋ ਗੁਸੈਲਨ ॥\
ਉਨ ਸਿਰ ਤੇ ਪਰੈ ਪਤਿਸ਼ਾਹੀ ਤਕਾਈ ॥ ਤੌ ਹੰਨੇ ਹੰਨੇ ਉਨ ਲਭੀ ਪਤਿਸ਼ਾਹੀ ॥੨੮॥

In this way the Majhail[^19] Singhs bore their suffering;\
and know that afterwards the Panth of those fierce men[^20] won its rule.\
They set their eyes on Patshahi beyond the loss of their heads,\
and so, saddle by saddle, they found their Patshahi. (28)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਜੋ ਕੋਊ ਪੁਛੈ ਉਨ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕਿਛ ਰਹਯੋ ਹੁਤੋ ਟੰਢਮੂਲ ॥\
ਰਾਜ ਲਭਯੋ ਕੈ ਭੂਮ ਲਭੀ ਸੋਊ ਬਤਾਊਂ ਸੂਲ ॥੨੯॥

If anyone should ask whether any stump of those Singhs' root was left,\
and whether they won rule or won their land, I shall give the trace[^21] of that too. (29)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਬਚਯੋ ਏਕ ਸਾਹਬ ਸਿੰਘ ਨਾਮ ॥ ਕਿਤ ਗਯੋ ਹੁਤੋ ਥੋ ਔਰ ਗ੍ਰਾਮ ॥\
ਸ੍ਰੀ ਅੰਮ੍ਰਿਤਸਰ ਕੀ ਲਭੀ ਉਨ ਕਾਰ ॥ ਭਏ ਅਰਦਾਸੀਏ ਸਚੇ ਦਰਬਾਰ ॥੩੦॥

One survived, Sahib Singh by name;\
he had gone off to some other village.\
He found service at Sri Amritsar,\
and became the ardāsīā[^22] at the True Darbar.[^23] (30)

ਉਨ ਇਜੈ ਬਿਜੈ ਕੀ ਪਦਵੀ ਪਾਈ ॥ ਸ਼ਾਹ ਪਤਸ਼ਾਹਿ ਉਨ ਚਰਨ ਛੁਹਾਈ ॥\
ਉਨ ਪੈ ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾ ਇਮ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਗੁਰ ਭਈ ॥ ਉਨ ਰਾਜ ਭੂਮੋਂ ਅਧਿਕਾਈ ਲਈ ॥੩੧॥

He won a station of honour and victory;[^24]\
Shahs and Patshahs touched his feet.\
Such was the grace of the Sri Guru upon him\
that he gained more than rule and land. (31)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਉਨ ਪੁਤ ਪੋਤ੍ਰੇ ਬਹੁ ਭਏ ਲੀ ਭੂਮ ਬੀ ਅਪਨ ਛੁਡਵਾਇ ॥\
ਬਦਲੇ ਲਏ ਦੁਸ਼ਮਨੋਂ ਤੇ ਖਾਲਸੇ ਕੇ ਬਲ ਪਾਇ ॥੩੨॥

Many sons and grandsons were born to him, and they won back their own land too;\
with the strength of the Khalsa behind them, they took their revenge on their enemies. (32)

### Notes

[^1]: Turks: the poet's word for the Muslim rulers and their armies · laid their heads on the heads of the Turks: ਸਿਰ ਸਿਰ ਲਾਇ, so that their blood lay on the Turks; Kulwant Singh: they held the Turks guilty of their deaths. BSS reads it as setting their heads against the Turks' heads.
[^2]: sikhī: the way of the Guru's Sikhs.
[^3]: Randhawa: a Jatt clan of the Majha.
[^4]: Chumunda: as the poet writes it; BSS: Chamunda, a town of the Majha on the road from Amritsar to Lahore. BSS takes ਬਾਈ ਬਾਸ as that road (Sher Shah Suri's highway); Kulwant Singh, as here, the twenty-two villages.
[^5]: Kairon Nangal: a village of the Randhawas; the poet's title calls its people the Kairon Nanglias.
[^6]: khanda: the double-edged sword · pāhul: the amrit of initiation into the Khalsa, stirred with the khanda (khande kī pāhul).
[^7]: ਚਿੱਤੈ, the chītā. BSS: a hunting cat smaller than the tiger, which broods and finds no peace when its spring has missed, until it has struck again.
[^8]: ਹੰਨੈ ਹੰਨੈ, from hannā, a saddle: every horseman of the Khalsa shall be a Patshah. BSS: the Guru's own word for his Khalsa.
[^9]: ਸਜਾਦੋ; BSS: Sajada; Kulwant Singh: Shahzada.
[^10]: Sultani: followers of Sakhi Sarvar (BSS).
[^11]: BSS: Jatts and others commonly called themselves Hindus. In verse 22 the poet counts the Singhs among the Hindus who were handed over.
[^12]: Musle: ਮੁਸਲੇ, a scornful word for Muslims.
[^13]: Kalal: the distillers' caste; BSS: there are Hindu Kalals and Muslim Kalals.
[^14]: The source prints the third line as a row of dots; Kulwant Singh: the couplet is incomplete in the handwritten manuscripts.
[^15]: ਗਰਭ ਛੇਦ. BSS: they meant to have even the children in the womb killed, so that no heir to the land would ever be born to them.
[^16]: ਸੇਲੂ, selū. BSS: the Turks kept him to bring them word of the Singhs.
[^17]: tegh: ਤੇਗ, the curved sword.
[^18]: ਟੰਢੁਮੂਲ, ṭaṅḍh mūl, stump and root: a last survivor of the line. BSS: some rare one may have been left; but Vahiguru keeps the root of all, and no one can kill the one his grace rests on.
[^19]: Majhail: the Singhs of the Majha, the country between the Beas and the Ravi.
[^20]: The line ਮੁੜ ਜਾਨੈ ਰਾਜ ਪੰਥ ਪਾਯੋ ਹੋ ਗੁਸੈਲਨ is obscure; ਗੁਸੈਲਨ, the fierce or wrathful ones, is read as the Singhs. Kulwant Singh: in the hope that their defiance would win the Panth its rule.
[^21]: ਸੂਲ; BSS: the trace, the tidings of it; Kulwant Singh: a hint.
[^22]: ardāsīā: the one who says the ardās, the Sikh prayer of supplication, on behalf of the saṅgat.
[^23]: The True Darbar: Sri Harimandar Sahib (BSS). BSS: when Sahib Singh came to Amritsar for darshan and told the Khalsa of the martyrdom, the Khalsa honoured him with the seva of the Harimandar.
[^24]: ਇਜੈ ਬਿਜੈ, read as honour (izzat) and victory; BSS: the station of victory in the Guru's house.

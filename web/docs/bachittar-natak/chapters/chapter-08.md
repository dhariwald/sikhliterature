# Bachittar Natak, Chapter 8

## The Battle of Bhangani

>**Questions**
>
>1. Who killed Najabat Khan?
>2. Hari Chand's 3 arrows struck what?
>3. Where did the Guru go after the victory?

#### ਅਥ ਰਾਜ ਸਾਜ ਕਥਨੰ ॥ *Now the account of taking up rule*

ਚੌਪਈ ॥ *Chaupai*

ਰਾਜ ਸਾਜ ਹਮ ਪਰ ਜਬ ਆਯੋ ॥ ਜਥਾ ਸਕਤ ਤਬ ਧਰਮ ਚਲਾਯੋ ॥\
ਭਾਂਤਿ ਭਾਂਤਿ ਬਨ ਖੇਲ ਸਿਕਾਰਾ ॥ ਮਾਰੇ ਰੀਛ ਰੋਝ ਝੰਖਾਰਾ ॥੧॥

When the charge of rule came upon us,\
we set dharam[^1] going as far as our strength allowed;\
we hunted game of every kind in the forests\
and killed bears, nilgai[^2] and swamp deer. (1)

ਦੇਸ ਚਾਲ ਹਮ ਤੇ ਪੁਨਿ ਭਈ ॥ ਸਹਰ ਪਾਂਵਟਾ ਕੀ ਸੁਧਿ ਲਈ ॥\
ਕਾਲਿੰਦ੍ਰੀ ਤਟਿ ਕਰੇ ਬਿਲਾਸਾ ॥ ਅਨਿਕ ਭਾਂਤ ਕੇ ਪੇਖ ਤਮਾਸਾ ॥੨॥

Then the time came for us to leave the country,[^3]\
and we made for the town of Paonta;[^4]\
on the bank of the Jamuna[^5] we took our pleasure\
and watched sights and wonders of every kind. (2)

ਤਹ ਕੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਘਨੇ ਚੁਨਿ ਮਾਰੇ ॥ ਰੋਝ ਰੀਛ ਬਹੁ ਭਾਂਤਿ ਬਿਦਾਰੇ ॥\
ਫਤੇਸਾਹ ਕੋਪਾ ਤਬਿ ਰਾਜਾ ॥ ਲੋਹ ਪਰਾ ਹਮ ਸੋ ਬਿਨੁ ਕਾਜਾ ॥੩॥

There we picked out and killed many lions,\
and tore down nilgai and bears in great numbers;\
then Raja Fateh Shah[^6] flared with fury\
and came at us with iron[^7] for no cause at all. (3)

ਭੁਜੰਗ ਪ੍ਰਯਾਤ ਛੰਦ ॥ *Bhujang Prayat Chhand*

ਤਹਾਂ ਸਾਹ ਸ੍ਰੀਸਾਹ ਸੰਗ੍ਰਾਮ ਕੋਪੇ ॥\
ਪੰਚੋ ਬੀਰ ਬੰਕੇ ਪ੍ਰਿਥੀ ਪਾਇ ਰੋਪੇ ॥\
ਹਠੀ ਜੀਤਮੱਲੰ ਸੁ ਗਾਜੀ ਗੁਲਾਬੰ ॥\
ਰਣੰ ਦੇਖੀਐ ਰੰਗ ਰੂਪੰ ਸਹਾਬੰ ॥੪॥

Then Sri Shah, Shah Sangram, King of Battle,[^8] blazed with fury,\
and the five bold heroes[^9] planted their feet in the earth:\
stubborn Jit Mall, and Gulab the champion;\
seeing the battle, their faces flushed crimson. (4)

ਹਠਿਯੋ ਮਾਹਰੀਚੰਦਯੰ ਗੰਗਰਾਮੰ ॥\
ਜਿਨੈ ਕਿਤਯੰ ਜਿਤੀਯੰ ਫੌਜ ਤਾਮੰ ॥\
ਕੁਪੇ ਲਾਲਚੰਦੰ ਕੀਏ ਲਾਲ ਰੂਪੰ ॥\
ਜਿਨੈ ਗੱਜੀਯੰ ਗਰਬ ਸਿੰਘੰ ਅਨੂਪੰ ॥੫॥

Stubborn Mahri Chand stood fast with Ganga Ram,\
men who had beaten army after army and hacked them into meat;[^10]\
Lal Chand flared up and went red with fury,[^11]\
he who had crushed the pride of peerless lions. (5)

ਕੁਪਿਯੋ ਮਾਹਰੂ ਕਾਹਰੂ ਰੂਪ ਧਾਰੇ ॥\
ਜਿਨੈ ਖਾਨ ਖਾਵੀਨਿਯੰ ਖੇਤ ਮਾਰੇ ॥\
ਕੁਪਿਓ ਦੇਵਤੇਸੰ ਦਯਾਰਾਮ ਜੁੱਧੰ ॥\
ਕੀਯੋ ਦ੍ਰੋਣ ਕੀ ਜਿਉ ਮਹਾ ਜੁੱਧ ਸੁੱਧੰ ॥੬॥

Maharu[^12] blazed up and took on a terrible form,\
he who had struck down great Khans[^13] on the field;\
godlike Daya Ram[^14] flared up in the fighting\
and fought a great and flawless battle, like Drona.[^15] (6)

ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾਲ ਕੋਪਿਯੰ ਕੁਤਕੋ ਸੰਭਾਰੀ ॥\
ਹਠੀ ਖਾਨਹੱਯਾਤ ਕੇ ਸੀਸ ਝਾਰੀ ॥\
ਉੱਠੀ ਛਿੱਛ ਇੱਛੰ ਕਢਾ ਮੇਝ ਜੋਰੰ ॥\
ਮਨੋ ਮਾਖਨੰ ਮੱਟਕੀ ਕਾਨ੍ਹ ਫੋਰੰ ॥੭॥

Kirpal[^16] flared with fury and took up his club,[^17]\
and brought it down on the head of stubborn Hayat Khan;\
the spray burst up as the force of it drove out the marrow,\
as though Kanh[^18] had smashed a pot of butter. (7)

ਤਹਾ ਨੰਦਚੰਦੰ ਕੀਯੋ ਕੋਪੁ ਭਾਰੋ ॥\
ਲਗਾਈ ਬਰੱਛੀ ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾਣੰ ਸੰਭਾਰੋ ॥\
ਤੁਟੀ ਤੇਗ ਤ੍ਰਿੱਖੀ ਕਢੇ ਜਮਦੱਢੰ ॥\
ਹਠੀ ਰਾਖਯੰ ਲੱਜ ਬੰਸੰ ਸੱਨਢੰ ॥੮॥

There Nand Chand[^19] blazed with a great fury;\
he drove in his spear, then gripped his sword;[^20]\
his keen tegh[^21] snapped, and he drew his dagger;\
so that stubborn man kept the honour of the Sodhi line.[^22] (8)

ਤਹਾਂ ਮਾਤਲੇਯੰ ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾਲੰ ਕ੍ਰੁੱਧੰ ॥\
ਛਕਿਓ ਛੋਭ ਛੱਤ੍ਰੀ ਕਰਯੋ ਜੁੱਧ ਸੁੱਧੰ ॥\
ਸਹੇ ਦੇਹ ਆਪੰ ਮਹਾਂ ਬੀਰ ਬਾਣੰ ॥\
ਕਰੋ ਖਾਨ ਬਾਨੀਨ ਖਾਲੀ ਪਲਾਣੰ ॥੯॥

Then the maternal uncle, Kirpal,[^23] blazed with anger;\
filled with fury, that Kshatriya[^24] fought a flawless fight;\
the great hero took the arrows into his own body\
and emptied the saddles of the archer Khans.[^25] (9)

ਹਠਿਯੋ ਸਾਹਬੰਚੰਦ ਖੇਤੰ ਖਤ੍ਰਿਯਾਣੰ ॥\
ਹਨੇ ਖਾਨ ਖੂਨੀ ਖੁਰਾਸਾਨ ਭਾਨੰ ॥\
ਤਹਾਂ ਬੀਰ ਬੰਕੇ ਭਲੀ ਭਾਂਤਿ ਮਾਰੇ ॥\
ਬਚੇ ਪ੍ਰਾਨ ਲੈ ਕੇ ਸਿਪਾਹੀ ਸਿਧਾਰੇ ॥੧੦॥

Stubborn Sahib Chand stood in the field as a Kshatriya should\
and slew bloodthirsty Khans, the suns of Khurasan;[^26]\
he cut down bold warriors there, and cut them well,\
and the soldiers who were left fled, carrying off their lives. (10)

ਤਹਾਂ ਸਾਹ ਸੰਗ੍ਰਾਮ ਕੀਨੇ ਅਖਾਰੇ ॥\
ਘਨੇ ਖੇਤ ਮੋ ਖਾਨ ਖੂਨੀ ਲਤਾਰੇ ॥\
ਨ੍ਰਿਪੰ ਗੋਪਲਾਯੰ ਖਰੋ ਖੇਤ ਗਾਜੈ ॥\
ਮ੍ਰਿਗਾ ਝੁੰਡ ਮੱਧਿਯੰ ਮਨੋ ਸਿੰਘ ਰਾਜੈ ॥੧੧॥

There Shah Sangram made the field his wrestling ring\
and trampled many bloodthirsty Khans in the battle;\
Raja Gopal[^27] stood in the field and roared,\
as a lion holds sway in the middle of a herd of deer. (11)

ਤਹਾਂ ਏਕ ਬੀਰੰ ਹਰੀ ਚੰਦ ਕੋਪ੍ਯੋ ॥\
ਭਲੀ ਭਾਂਤਿ ਸੋ ਖੇਤ ਮੋ ਪਾਵ ਰੋਪ੍ਯੋ ॥\
ਮਹਾ ਕ੍ਰੋਧ ਕੈ ਤੀਰ ਤੀਖੇ ਪ੍ਰਹਾਰੇ ॥\
ਲਗੈ ਜੌਨ ਕੇ ਤਾਹਿ ਪਾਰੈ ਪਧਾਰੇ ॥੧੨॥

Then one hero, Hari Chand,[^28] flared with fury\
and planted his feet firmly in the field;\
in great anger he shot his sharp arrows,[^29]\
and whoever they struck, they passed clean through. (12)

ਰਸਾਵਲ ਛੰਦ ॥ *Rasaval Chhand*

ਹਰੀਚੰਦ ਕ੍ਰੁੱਧੰ ॥ ਹਨੇ ਸੂਰ ਸੁੱਧੰ ॥\
ਭਲੇ ਬਾਣ ਬਾਹੇ ॥ ਬਡੇ ਸੈਨ ਗਾਹੇ ॥੧੩॥

Hari Chand, in fury,\
cut down true warriors;\
he shot his arrows well\
and trampled through great armies. (13)

ਰਸੰ ਰੁੱਦ੍ਰ ਰਾਚੇ ॥ ਮਹਾ ਲੋਹ ਮਾਚੇ ॥\
ਹਨੇ ਸਸਤ੍ਰ ਧਾਰੀ ॥ ਲਿਟੇ ਭੂਪ ਭਾਰੀ ॥੧੪॥

Steeped in wrath,[^30]\
he raised a great clash of iron;\
he slew men who bore arms,\
and great rajas lay sprawled on the ground. (14)

ਤਬੈ ਜੀਤਮੱਲੰ ॥ ਹਰੀਚੰਦ ਭੱਲੰ ॥\
ਹ੍ਰਿਦੈ ਐਂਚ ਮਾਰਿਓ ॥ ਸੁ ਖੇਤੰ ਉਤਾਰਿਓ ॥੧੫॥

Then Jit Mall\
drew back his spear\
and drove it into Hari Chand's heart,\
and brought him down on the field. (15)

ਲਗੇ ਬੀਰ ਬਾਣੰ ॥ ਰਿਸਿਯੋ ਤੇਜਿ ਮਾਣੰ ॥\
ਸਮੁਹ ਬਾਜ ਡਾਰੇ ॥ ਸੁਵਰਗੰ ਸਿਧਾਰੇ ॥੧੬॥

As arrows struck the heroes,\
they blazed red as fire;[^31]\
they left their horses on the field before them\
and went on to heaven. (16)

ਭੁਯੰਗ ਪ੍ਰਯਾਤ ਛੰਦ ॥ *Bhuyang Prayat Chhand*

ਖੁਲੈ ਖਾਨ ਖੂਨੀ ਖੁਰਾਸਾਨ ਖੱਗੰ ॥\
ਪਰੀ ਸਸਤ੍ਰ ਧਾਰੰ ਉਠੀ ਝਾਲ ਅੱਗੰ ॥\
ਭਈ ਤੀਰ ਭੀਰੰ ਕਮਾਣੰ ਕੱੜਕੇ ॥\
ਗਿਰੇ ਬਾਜ ਤਾਜੀ ਲਗੇ ਧੀਰ ਧੱਕੇ ॥੧੭॥

The bloodthirsty Khans bared their swords of Khurasan;\
edge clashed on edge, and the sparks flew up like fire;\
arrows thronged the air, the bows cracked and twanged,\
and Arab horses went down under the blows of steady men. (17)

ਬਜੀ ਭੇਰਿ ਭੁੰਕਾਰ ਧੁੱਕੇ ਨਗਾਰੇ ॥\
ਦੁਹੂੰ ਓਰ ਤੇ ਬੀਰ ਬੰਕੇ ਬਕਾਰੇ ॥\
ਕਰੇ ਬਾਹੁ ਆਘਾਤ ਸਸਤ੍ਰੰ ਪ੍ਰਹਾਰੰ ॥\
ਡਕੀ ਡਾਕਣੀ ਚਾਂਵਡੀ ਚੀਤਕਾਰੰ ॥੧੮॥

The war horns blared and the war drums thundered,[^32]\
and from both sides bold heroes roared their challenges;\
they swung their arms and struck with their weapons;\
the dakinis drank their fill and the Chamundis shrieked.[^33] (18)

ਦੋਹਰਾ ॥ *Dohra*

ਕਹਾ ਲਗੇ ਬਰਨਨ ਕਰੋਂ ਮਚਿਯੋ ਜੁਧੁ ਅਪਾਰ ॥\
ਜੇ ਲੁੱਝੇ ਜੁੱਝੇ ਸਬੈ ਭੱਜੇ ਸੂਰ ਹਜਾਰ ॥੧੯॥

How far can I go on describing it? A boundless battle raged;\
all who fought fell fighting, and heroes by the thousand fled. (19)

ਭੁਜੰਗ ਪ੍ਰਯਾਤ ਛੰਦ ॥ *Bhujang Prayat Chhand*

ਭਜਿਯੋ ਸਾਹ ਪਹਾੜਤਾਜੀ ਤ੍ਰਿਪਾਯੰ ॥\
ਚਲਿਯੋ ਬੀਰੀਯਾ ਤੀਰੀਯਾ ਨ ਚਲਾਯੰ ॥\
ਜਸੋ ਡੱਢਵਾਲੰ ਮਧੁੱਕਰ ਸੁ ਸਾਹੰ ॥\
ਭਜੇ ਸੰਗ ਲੈ ਕੇ ਸੁ ਸਾਰੀ ਸਿਪਾਹੰ ॥੨੦॥

The Shah of the hills[^34] fled, setting his horse to leap;\
off went the little hero, without firing one little arrow;[^35]\
the Jaswal, and Madhukar Shah of Dadhwal,[^36]\
fled too, taking all their soldiers with them. (20)

ਚਕ੍ਰਿਤ ਚੋਪਿਯੋ ਚੰਦ ਗਾਜੀ ਚੰਦੇਲੰ ॥\
ਹਠੀ ਹਰੀਚੰਦੰ ਗਹੇ ਹਾਥ ਸੇਲੰ ॥\
ਕਰਿਓ ਸੁਆਮਿ ਧਰਮੰ ਮਹਾ ਰੋਸ ਰੁੱਝਿਯੰ ॥\
ਗਿਰਿਓ ਟੂਕ ਟੂਕ ਹ੍ਯੈ ਇਸੋ ਸੂਰ ਜੁੱਝਿਯੰ ॥੨੧॥

Astonished, the Chandel, Ghazi Chand,[^37] flared up eagerly;\
stubborn Hari Chand took a lance[^38] in his hand;\
he kept his dharam to his master, caught up in great fury,\
and fell cut to pieces: so did that hero fight to his death. (21)

ਤਹਾ ਖਾਨ ਨੈਜਾਬਤੋ ਆਨ ਕੈ ਕੈ ॥\
ਹਨਿਓ ਸਾਹ ਸੰਗ੍ਰਾਮ ਕੌ ਸਸਤ੍ਰ ਲੈ ਕੈ ॥\
ਕਿਤੈ ਖਾਨ ਬਾਨੀਨ ਹੂੰ ਅਸਤ੍ਰ ਝਾਰੇ ॥\
ਸਹੀ ਸਾਹ ਸੰਗ੍ਰਾਮ ਸੁਰਗੰ ਸਿਧਾਰੇ ॥੨੨॥

Then Najabat Khan[^39] came up\
and struck at Shah Sangram with his weapons;\
many archer Khans rained missiles[^40] on him,\
and so, in truth, Shah Sangram went to heaven. (22)

ਦੋਹਰਾ ॥ *Dohra*

ਮਾਰਿ ਨਜਾਬਤ ਖਾਨ ਕੋ ਸੰਗੋ ਜੁਝੈ ਜੁਝਾਰ ॥\
ਹਾ ਹਾ ਇਹ ਲੋਕੈ ਭਇਓ ਸੁਰਗ ਲੋਕ ਜੈਕਾਰ ॥੨੩॥

Sango, the warrior, killed Najabat Khan and fell fighting;\
this world filled with lament, and heaven rang with cries of victory. (23)

ਭੁਜੰਗ ਛੰਦ ॥ *Bhujang Chhand*

ਲਖੇ ਸਾਹ ਸੰਗ੍ਰਾਮ ਜੁੱਝੇ ਜੁਝਾਰੰ ॥\
ਤਵੰ ਕੀਟ ਬਾਣੰ ਕਮਾਣੰ ਸੰਭਾਰੰ ॥\
ਹਨਿਯੋ ਏਕ ਖਾਨੰ ਖਿਆਲੰ ਖਤੰਗੰ ॥\
ਡਸਿਯੋ ਸਤ੍ਰੁ ਕੋ ਜਾਨੁ ਸ੍ਯਾਮੰ ਭੁਜੰਗੰ ॥੨੪॥

When we saw Shah Sangram fall fighting,\
your worm[^41] took up bow and arrows,\
took aim and struck down a Khan with an arrow,\
and it bit the enemy like a black cobra. (24)

ਗਿਰਿਯੋ ਭੂਮ ਸੋ ਬਾਣ ਦੂਜੋ ਸੰਭਾਰਯੋ ॥\
ਮੁਖੰ ਭੀਖਨੰ ਖਾਨ ਕੇ ਤਾਨ ਮਾਰਯੋ ॥\
ਭਜਿਯੋ ਖਾਨ ਖੂਨੀ ਰਹਿਯੋ ਖੇਤ ਤਾਜੀ ॥\
ਤਜੇ ਪ੍ਰਾਣ ਤੀਜੇ ਲਗੇ ਬਾਣ ਬਾਜੀ ॥੨੫॥

He fell to the ground; we took up a second arrow,\
drew it tight and shot it into Bhikhan Khan's face;[^42]\
the bloodthirsty Khan fled, leaving his horse on the field,\
and at the third arrow the horse gave up its life. (25)

ਛੁਟੀ ਮੂਰਛਨਾ ਹਰੀਚੰਦੰ ਸੰਭਾਰੇ ॥\
ਗਹੇ ਬਾਣ ਕਾਮਾਨ ਭੇ ਐਂਚ ਮਾਰੇ ॥\
ਲਗੇ ਅੰਗ ਜਾ ਕੇ ਰਹੇ ਨਾ ਸੰਭਾਰੰ ॥\
ਤਨੰ ਤਿਆਗ ਤੇ ਦੇਵਲੋਕੰ ਪਧਾਰੰ ॥੨੬॥

Hari Chand came out of his swoon and gathered himself;\
he took up his bow and arrows, drew and shot;\
any man they struck in the body could not stand,\
but left the body and went on to the world of the gods. (26)

ਦੁਯੰ ਬਾਨ ਖੈਂਚੇ ਇਕੰ ਬਾਰ ਮਾਰੇ ॥\
ਬਲੀ ਬੀਰ ਬਾਜੀਨ ਤਾਜੀ ਬਿਦਾਰੇ ॥\
ਜਿਸੈ ਬਾਨ ਲਾਗੈ ਰਹੈ ਨ ਸੰਭਾਰੰ ॥\
ਤਨੰ ਬੇਧਿ ਕੈ ਤਾਹਿ ਪਾਰੰ ਸਿਧਾਰੰ ॥੨੭॥

He drew two arrows and shot them at once,\
and tore through strong heroes, chargers and Arab horses;\
any man his arrow struck could not keep his feet;\
it pierced the body and passed clean through. (27)

ਸਭੈ ਸ੍ਯਾਮਿ ਧਰਮੰ ਸੁ ਬੀਰੰ ਸੰਭਾਰੇ ॥\
ਡਕੀ ਡਾਕਣੀ ਭੂਤ ਪ੍ਰੇਤੰ ਬਕਾਰੇ ॥\
ਹਸੇ ਬੀਰ ਬੈਤਾਲ ਔ ਸੁੱਧ ਸਿੱਧੰ ॥\
ਚਵੀ ਚਾਵਡੀਯੰ ਉਡੀ ਗ੍ਰਿੱਧ ਬ੍ਰਿੱਧੰ ॥੨੮॥

All the heroes kept their dharam to their master;\
the dakinis drank their fill, ghosts and phantoms howled;\
the spirits and the vetalas laughed, and the pure adepts too;[^43]\
the Chamundis chattered, and huge vultures flew. (28)

ਹਰੀਚੰਦ ਕੋਪੇ ਕਮਾਣੰ ਸੰਭਾਰੰ ॥\
ਪ੍ਰਥਮ ਬਾਜੀਯੰ ਤਾਣ ਬਾਣੰ ਪ੍ਰਹਾਰੰ ॥\
ਦੁਤੀਯ ਤਾਕ ਕੈ ਤੀਰ ਮੋ ਕੌ ਚਲਾਯੰ ॥\
ਰਖਿਓ ਦਈਵ ਮੈ ਕਾਨ ਛ੍ਯੈ ਕੈ ਸਿਧਾਯੰ ॥੨੯॥

Hari Chand flared with fury and took up his bow;\
he drew his first arrow and struck our horse;\
then he took aim and fired a second at me:\
the Lord[^44] kept me safe, and it only grazed my ear as it went by. (29)

ਤ੍ਰਿਤੀਯ ਬਾਣ ਮਾਰਿਯੋ ਸੁ ਪੇਟੀ ਮਝਾਰੰ ॥\
ਬਿਧਿਅੰ ਚਿਲਕਤੰ ਦੁਆਲ ਪਾਰੰ ਪਧਾਰੰ ॥\
ਚੁਭੀ ਚਿੰਚ ਚਰਮੰ ਕਛੂ ਘਾਇ ਨ ਆਯੰ ॥\
ਕਲੰ ਕੇਵਲੰ ਜਾਨ ਦਾਸੰ ਬਚਾਯੰ ॥੩੦॥

He shot his third arrow into our belt;[^45]\
it pierced the bright cloth and went on through the strap;\
its point pricked our skin, but no wound came of it:\
Kal[^46] alone, knowing us for his servant, saved us. (30)

ਰਸਾਵਲ ਛੰਦ ॥ *Rasaval Chhand*

ਜਬੈ ਬਾਣ ਲਾਗ੍ਯੋ ॥ ਤਬੈ ਰੋਸ ਜਾਗ੍ਯੋ ॥\
ਕਰੰ ਲੈ ਕਮਾਣੰ ॥ ਹਨੰ ਬਾਣ ਤਾਣੰ ॥੩੧॥

When the arrow struck,\
our anger woke;\
we took our bow in hand\
and drew and shot. (31)

ਸਬੈ ਬੀਰ ਧਾਏ ॥ ਸਰੋਘੰ ਚਲਾਏ ॥\
ਤਬੈ ਤਾਕਿ ਬਾਣੰ ॥ ਹਨ੍ਯੋ ਏਕ ਜੁਆਣੰ ॥੩੨॥

All the heroes charged\
and fired a storm of arrows;\
then we took aim\
and struck down a young warrior. (32)

ਹਰੀਚੰਦ ਮਾਰੇ ॥ ਸੁ ਜੋਧਾ ਲਤਾਰੇ ॥\
ਸੁ ਕਾਰੋੜਰਾਯੰ ॥ ਵਹੈ ਕਾਲ ਘਾਯੰ ॥੩੩॥

Hari Chand was slain,[^47]\
his fighters were trampled,\
and as for Karor Rai,[^48]\
Kal struck him down too. (33)

ਰਣੰ ਤਿਆਗਿ ਭਾਗੇ ॥ ਸਬੈ ਤ੍ਰਾਸ ਪਾਗੇ ॥\
ਭਈ ਜੀਤ ਮੇਰੀ ॥ ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾ ਕਾਲ ਕੇਰੀ ॥੩੪॥

They left the field and fled,\
every one of them gripped by fear;\
the victory was mine,\
by the grace of Kal. (34)

ਰਣੰ ਜੀਤਿ ਆਏ ॥ ਜਯੰ ਗੀਤ ਗਾਏ ॥\
ਧਨੰ ਧਾਰ ਬਰਖੇ ॥ ਸਬੈ ਸੂਰ ਹਰਖੇ ॥੩੫॥

We came home, having won the battle,\
and sang the songs of triumph;\
we rained down wealth in torrents,\
and all the heroes rejoiced. (35)

ਦੋਹਰਾ ॥ *Dohra*

ਜੁਧ ਜੀਤ ਆਏ ਜਬੈ ਟਿਕੈ ਨ ਤਿਨ ਪੁਰ ਪਾਂਵ ॥\
ਕਾਹਲੂਰ ਮੈਂ ਬਾਂਧਿਯੋ ਆਨ ਆਨੰਦਪੁਰ ਗਾਂਵ ॥੩੬॥

When we came home, victors in the battle, our feet would not rest in that town;\
we came to Kahlur[^49] and founded the village of Anandpur.[^50] (36)

ਜੇ ਜੇ ਨਰ ਤਹ ਨ ਭਿਰੇ ਦੀਨੇ ਨਗਰ ਨਿਕਾਰ ॥\
ਜੇ ਤਿਹ ਠਉਰ ਭਲੇ ਭਿਰੇ ਤਿਨੈ ਕਰੀ ਪ੍ਰਤਿਪਾਰ ॥੩੭॥

We drove out of the town every man who had not fought there,\
and took into our care all who had fought well in that place. (37)

ਚੌਪਈ ॥ *Chaupai*

ਬਹੁਤ ਦਿਵਸ ਇਹ ਭਾਂਤਿ ਬਿਤਾਏ ॥ ਸੰਤ ਉਬਾਰ ਦੁਸਟ ਸਭ ਘਾਏ ॥\
ਟਾਂਗ ਟਾਂਗ ਕਰਿ ਹਨੇ ਨਿਦਾਨਾ ॥ ਕੂਕਰ ਜਿਮਿ ਤਿਨ ਤਜੇ ਪਰਾਨਾ ॥੩੮॥

Many days passed in this way;\
we saved the saints and slew all the wicked;\
we strung up those fools[^51] and killed them one by one,\
and they gave up their lives like dogs. (38)

---
ਇਤਿ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਬਚਿਤ੍ਰ ਨਾਟਕ ਗ੍ਰੰਥੇ ਭੰਗਾਣੀ ਜੁੱਧ ਬਰਨਨੰ ਨਾਮ ਅਸਟਮੋ ਧਿਆਇ ਸਮਾਪਤਮ ਸਤੁ ਸੁਭਮ ਸਤੁ ॥੮॥ ਅਫਜੂ ॥੩੨੦॥

*Here ends the eighth chapter of the holy book Bachittar Natak, called the Account of the Battle of Bhangani. All is true, all is blessed. Chapter 8; verses so far, 320.*

### Notes

[^1]: dharam, ਧਰਮ: what is right, faith and duty in one word; ਸੁਆਮਿ ਧਰਮ (vv. 21, 28) is the dharam a fighting man owes his master.
[^2]: ਰੋਝ *rojh*, the nilgai or blue bull, the largest antelope of India · ਝੰਖਾਰਾ *jhaṅkhārā*, the barasingha or swamp deer.
[^3]: Steek: Sri Dasmesh Ji took his seat on the Guru's throne in Sammat 1733 (1676 CE), and the move from Anandpur came in Sammat 1743, in September and October 1686 CE.
[^4]: Paonta, now Paonta Sahib, on the Jamuna in the hill state of Sirmaur (Nahan).
[^5]: The poet's word is ਕਾਲਿੰਦ੍ਰੀ, Kalindri, the Jamuna as the daughter of Mount Kalinda.
[^6]: Fateh Shah, raja of Srinagar in Garhwal. Steek: he led the hill rajas in this battle.
[^7]: Steek: the hill rajas fell on the Guru near the village of Bhangani, where the ground is partly level and partly hill, and at the sound of their war bugles the villagers scattered. A Sikh ran in, out of breath, with news of the hill army while the Guru was at work on the Krishna Avatar of the Dasam Granth; at once everyone in the fort armed and came out, shouting their war cries.
[^8]: Sri Shah and Shah Sangram are both Sango Shah. Steek: Shah Sangram, King of Battle, was the son of Sadhu and Bibi Viro; he fought so bravely in battle that the Guru gave him that title.
[^9]: Steek: the five sons of Bibi Viro, the Guru's father's sister: Shah Sri Shah (Sango Shah), Jit Mall, Gulab Chand, Mahri Chand and Ganga Ram. They drew their swords and stood in the path of the armies, and fell on the enemy with such blows that he could come no further. Jit Mall and Gulab Chand brought riders down by cutting their horses' legs, then hacked them apart as carrots and radishes are cut up for pickle; the ground ran red with blood, and flocks of carrion birds wheeled overhead.
[^10]: ਤਾਮ *tām*, food. Steek: they made the army like roasted mince; Rattan Singh Jaggi: food for the beasts of prey.
[^11]: Steek: red-faced with fury, Lal Chand raised the war cry, calling on his comrades to roar like lions, thunder like clouds and fall on the enemy; he himself roared as Hanuman roared in the war of Ram and Ravan, and as Bhim Sen roared his challenge in the Mahabharata.
[^12]: ਮਾਹਰੂ as printed. The Steek reads Mehar Chand; Rattan Singh Jaggi and Narain Singh read Mahri Chand.
[^13]: Khan, the Pathan title. Steek: some Muslims of the village of Damla, who had left Aurangzeb's service, had been taken into the Guru's army at the request of Pir Buddhu Shah of Sadhaura; these Pathans broke faith with the salt they had eaten and fought for the hill rajas against the Guru.
[^14]: Steek: Daya Ram, a Brahmin, son of Mall, met the enemy at Bhangani with great courage, and the Guru gives him the title of a god (devtā). When the rajas' soldiers struck at him, their blades flashed like lightning and their own hands shook; when the Brahmin struck, the young men went down senseless. He caught the bridle of Raja Fateh Shah's horse and asked whether he was Fateh Shah, and told him to look how many had died: the Guru Maharaj was full of blessings and nothing would happen to him, but nothing would be left of Fateh Shah. Fateh Shah came on, and dread of the field took him as he saw army upon army coming; the Brahmin told him that the Guru Maharaj had himself invited him, that he was a believer in the Guru's house and no enemy, and had joined this war only for a marriage tie, at the bidding of his kinsman by marriage. As Fateh Shah looked at the Brahmin, he seemed a tall soldier with a long bow and arrow; the raja took fright, put his horse to the gallop and fled.
[^15]: Drona, the teacher of arms to the Pandavas and the Kauravas in the Mahabharata.
[^16]: Steek: Kirpal Das, the Udasi sadhu (in its line-by-line meaning, a Vairagi sadhu). Hayat Khan, a proud Pathan, was roaring and swinging his sword fast, his cap fallen and his shaven head shining. He struck at Kirpal Das, who caught the blow on his club and struck back so hard that the Khan's sword broke in three; at the third blow Hayat Khan lay flat and never rose. Then Khem Chand, a Rajput and a proud player of gatka, came roaring *Jai Deva, Jai Deva* and mocked a sadhu for fighting Rajputs; Kirpal Das called them snakes reared on the scraps of Aurangzeb, whose heads only a sadhu's club could crush, struck him on the head, and with a second blow sent him spinning onto Hayat Khan's body. Seeing Kirpal Das's daring, his companions took heart and beat Rajputs and Pathans with their clubs; but because they fought only with clubs, many sadhus were killed by the arrows of rajas shooting from far off.
[^17]: ਕੁਤਕਾ *kutkā*, a short, heavy club. Steek: the club he used for grinding bhang, which he took up and laid on his shoulder.
[^18]: Kanh, Krishna, who as a boy broke the cowherd women's pots of butter.
[^19]: Nand Chand, the Guru's diwan. Steek: because Kirpal Das had killed Hayat Khan, Najabat Khan fell on him, and Nand Chand came forward to stop the blow; the Turks surrounded him. Spear in one hand and sword in the other, he turned his horse with his feet and challenged Najabat Khan, and they fought hard. Najabat struck at Nand Chand with all his strength; the Guru saved Nand Chand from the blow, and Najabat, striking so hard, pitched forward. Nand Chand's blade went into his side, and when he drew the dagger out, it brought Najabat Khan's entrails with it.
[^20]: ਬਰੱਛੀ *barachhī*, spear (ਭੱਲ, v. 15, the same) · ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾਣ, a fighter's sword (ਖੱਗ, v. 17, the same).
[^21]: tegh, ਤੇਗ, the curved sword · ਜਮਦੱਢ *jamdhaṛh*, a thrusting dagger.
[^22]: The poet's ਸਨੱਢ, read by the Steek and the other commentators as Sodhi, the Guru's own Khatri clan.
[^23]: Kirpal Chand, the Guru's mother's brother. Steek: when the Turks surrounded Nand Chand, Mama Kirpal came to his aid and drove them off.
[^24]: Kshatriya, ਛੱਤ੍ਰੀ, the order of warriors.
[^25]: ਬਾਨੀਨ: the Steek reads the Khans who were shooting arrows; Narain Singh and Rattan Singh Jaggi read the bold, dashing Khans.
[^26]: Khurasan, the old name for eastern Iran and the country towards Afghanistan. Steek: Khans who were called the suns of Khurasan.
[^27]: Gopal, raja of Guler. Steek: Nand Chand struck him a heavy blow in the face.
[^28]: Hari Chand, raja of Handur (Nalagarh), on the side of the hill rajas. Steek: the blow to Gopal stung Hari Chand, and he took up his bow. While Hari Chand was still fighting, a Sikh brought up a wooden cannon; at the Guru's order it was loaded with powder and stone shot and fired, the stones wounded many hill soldiers, and its roar shook the hill.
[^29]: arrow: ਤੀਰ *tīr*, ਬਾਣ *bāṇ*, and ਖਤੰਗ *khataṅg* (Persian khadang) at v. 24 · bow: ਕਮਾਣ *kamāṇ*.
[^30]: ਰਸੰ ਰੁੱਦ੍ਰ, raudra ras, the mood of wrath, one of the nine moods (rasa) of Indian poetics.
[^31]: ਰਿਸਿਯੋ ਤੇਜਿ ਮਾਣੰ is hard to read. Steek: the blood of a man struck by an arrow shone red as the sun; Narain Singh: he went red as fire with fury.
[^32]: ਭੇਰਿ *bheri*, the war horn · ਨਗਾਰੇ *nagāre*, the great war drums.
[^33]: dakinis (ਡਾਕਣੀ), female spirits that feed on the slain · Chamundis (ਚਾਂਵਡੀ), the female spirits in the train of the goddess Chamunda (Rattan Singh Jaggi); the Steek reads kites and yoginis, Narain Singh witches.
[^34]: Fateh Shah (Steek, Narain Singh).
[^35]: The poet's diminutives, ਬੀਰੀਯਾ and ਤੀਰੀਯਾ, are scornful. The Steek, Narain Singh and Rattan Singh Jaggi read the line as none of the Guru's warriors shooting an arrow at the fleeing raja.
[^36]: The Jaswal is Kesari Chand, raja of Jaswan; Madhukar Shah, raja of Dadhwal (Steek).
[^37]: The Steek names him Ghazi Chand of Chanderi.
[^38]: ਸੇਲ *sel*, a lance.
[^39]: Najabat Khan (also written Nijabat), of Kunjpura, leader of the Khans who broke faith with the Guru's salt, and commander of a hundred horsemen; Sain Buddhu Shah of Sadhaura had placed him in the Guru's service, and now he fought on the enemy's side. Steek: Sango Shah and Najabat Khan struck at each other at the same moment, and both fell.
[^40]: ਅਸਤ੍ਰ *astra*, a weapon that is thrown or shot, as against ਸਸਤ੍ਰ, a weapon held in the hand.
[^41]: ਕੀਟ *kīṭ*, a worm or insect: the Guru names himself so before Kal.
[^42]: Steek: Bhikhan Khan was also one of Najabat Khan's companions, commander of a hundred horsemen. In this battle a thousand hillmen died and thousands were wounded; of four hundred Pathans who had broken faith with the Guru's salt, more than three hundred died, offered up to the war; and between three and four hundred Singhs fell as martyrs.
[^43]: spirits, ਬੀਰ *bīr* · vetalas, ਬੈਤਾਲ, the spirits that haunt cremation grounds and enter corpses · the adepts, ਸਿੱਧ, Shiva's attendants (Narain Singh, Rattan Singh Jaggi).
[^44]: ਦਈਵ *daīv*, the Divine. The Steek reads Akal Purakh.
[^45]: ਪੇਟੀ, the belt (Steek: the waistband) · ਚਿਲਕਤ, read here as the bright cloth; Rattan Singh Jaggi: a silk garment · ਦੁਆਲ, the strap of the belt.
[^46]: Kal (ਕਲ, ਕਾਲ), the Lord as all-conquering Time, the name the Bachittar Natak gives the Supreme Being; the Steek and Narain Singh read Akal Purakh.
[^47]: Steek: the young warrior of v. 32 was Hari Chand. With his death the rout began: all the hill rajas and their allies slipped away, and women whose husbands and sons had not come home went searching for them. The queen of Raja Hari Chand of Handur came, found her husband's body, had her slave women build a pyre, and became sati with the raja, and so went to heaven.
[^48]: Karor Rai: the chief of Kot Lehar (Steek, Narain Singh).
[^49]: Kahlur, the hill state later called Bilaspur, on the Satluj. Steek: after the victory at Bhangani the Sikhs wanted to take Fateh Shah's state and hold the road from Anandpur to Paonta, but the Guru's aim was not to take any land; it was to give courage and daring to the downtrodden. After the battle he cared for the enemy's wounded as well as his own wounded Sikhs, and for this reason Bhim Chand and many other rajas came to Anandpur in Sammat 1746 (1689 CE), asked his pardon and made peace.
[^50]: Steek: Anandpur had first been founded by Guru Tegh Bahadur. In Sammat 1745 (1688 CE) the Guru came back to it and settled it again, for in his three or four years away the town had emptied. The raja of Nahan was devoted to the Guru, but for fear of his own brotherhood he fought on neither side, so the Guru left without meeting him. At the full moon of Sammat 1745 he reached the fair at Kapal Mochan and taught the Sikh way there; at Raipur the rani served him with body and mind and took up his Sikhi, and memorials were built wherever his feet had rested; at Dhakauli the Pathans gave him a great welcome, and he had a stepwell dug there. The hill rajas were brought so low, by weddings and by war, that they spent months in weeping. The Steek asks what kind of people they are who call the Bachittar Natak the work of poets.
[^51]: ਨਿਦਾਨਾ, read as fools by the Steek and Rattan Singh Jaggi; Surinder Singh Kohli reads it as in the end. Steek: he hanged those fools and evildoers from trees.

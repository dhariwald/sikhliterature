# Panth Prakash, Episode 31

## Banda's Faith

### (...jau māṅgai tis soū divāvai: ...whatever a man asked, he had it given him)

*Dohra* ਦੋਹਰਾ

ਤੌ ਚਿਤ ਬੰਦੈ ਇਮ ਅਈ ਅਬ ਲੀਜੈ ਬਚਨ ਪ੍ਰਤੋਇ ॥\
ਸਦ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਦਿਵਾਨ ਲਗਾਇ ਕੈ ਬਿੱਚੈ ਆਪ ਖਲੋਇ ॥੧॥

Then it came into Banda's mind to put the Guru's word to the test.\
He called the Singhs, gathered them in assembly, and stood among them himself. (1)

*Chaupai* ਚੌਪਈ

ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਬਚਨ ਅਬ ਲੱਯੈ ਪਰਤਾਈ ॥ ਸਿੱਖ ਪੰਜ ਤਿਨ ਲਏ ਬੁਲਾਈ ॥\
ਹਾਥ ਜੋੜ ਵਿਚ ਆਪ ਖਲੋਯੋ ॥ ਸ੍ਰਬਤ੍ਰ ਖਾਲਸੋ ਇਕ ਮਨ ਹੋਯੋ ॥੨॥

Now, to prove the Satguru's word,\
he called five Sikhs forward,\
and stood in their midst with his palms joined,\
and the whole Khalsa was of one mind. (2)

ਨਹੀਂ ਖਰਚ ਅਬ ਹਮਰੇ ਪਾਸ ॥ ਆਵੈ ਖਰਚ ਯੌ ਕਰੀ ਅਰਦਾਸ ॥\
ਆਏ ਲੁਬਾਣੇ ਲਗ ਗਈ ਲਾਰ ॥ ਦਯੋ ਦਸਵੰਧ ਉਨ ਕਈ ਹਜ਼ਾਰ ॥੩॥

"We have no money left for our needs;\
let money come to us": so they made the ardās.[^1]\
Labanas[^2] came, a long line of them,\
and gave their dasvaṅdh,[^3] many thousands. (3)

ਸੋਊ ਬੰਦੈ ਆਇ ਆਗੈ ਧਰਯੋ ॥ ਕਰਾਇ ਅਰਦਾਸ ਬੰਦੇ ਹਥ ਫਰਯੋ ॥\
ਬੰਦੇ ਬਧੀ ਖਾਲਸੇ ਪ੍ਰੀਤ ॥ ਨਿਸਚੈ ਭਯੋ ਬੰਦੈ ਮਧ ਚੀਤ ॥੪॥

They came and laid it all before Banda;\
he had the ardās said and took it in his hand.\
Banda's love for the Khalsa grew,\
and faith took hold within his heart. (4)

ਬੰਦਾ ਖਾਲਸੇ ਦੀਵਾਨ ਲਗਾਵੈ ॥ ਅਕਾਲ ਉਸਤਤਿ ਵਾਰ ਚੰਡੀ ਪੜ੍ਹਾਵੈ ॥\
'ਅਕਾਲ ਅਕਾਲ' ਕਾ ਜਾਪ ਜਪਾਵੈ ॥ ਵਾਹਿਗੁਰੂ ਵਾਹਿਗੁਰੂ ਸਦਾ ਮੁਖ ਗਾਵੈ ॥੫॥

Banda would gather the Khalsa in assembly\
and have Akal Ustat and Chandi di Var[^4] recited;\
he had them chant "Akal,[^5] Akal,"\
and sang with his own lips, always, "Vahiguru,[^6] Vahiguru." (5)

*Dohra* ਦੋਹਰਾ

ਬੰਦਾ ਪੰਜਾਬਹਿ ਕੋ ਚੜ੍ਹਯੋ ਲਯੋ ਖਾਲਸੋ ਸਾਥ ॥\
ਲੈਨ ਬੈਰ ਗੁਰ ਸੁਤਨ ਸੁਨ ਆ ਟੇਕੈਂ ਸਿਖ ਮਾਥ ॥੬॥

Banda marched for Punjab, and took the Khalsa with him;\
Sikhs who heard he went to avenge the Guru's sons[^7] came and bowed their heads. (6)

*Chaupai* ਚੌਪਈ

ਜਹਿਂ ਬੰਦਾ ਆਨ ਪਲਕ ਖਲੋਵੈ ॥ ਰਿਧਿ ਆਨ ਉਹਾਂ ਸਿੱਖਨ ਢੋਵੈ ॥\
ਜੋਊ ਬੀਰ ਬੰਦੈ ਥੇ ਪਾਸ ॥ ਆਵੈਂ ਸਿੱਖਨ ਸੁਨ ਅਰਦਾਸ ॥੭॥

Wherever Banda stopped, if only for a moment,\
the Sikhs came there, hauling in plenty;\
and the familiar spirits[^8] that Banda kept\
came when they heard the Sikhs' ardās. (7)

ਯੌ ਕਰ ਬੰਦੇ ਭਈ ਪ੍ਰਤੀਤ ॥ ਤਬ ਬੰਦਾ ਭਯੋ ਨਿਸਚਲ ਚੀਤ ॥\
ਪੁਤ ਮੰਗੈ ਤਿਸ ਦ੍ਵਾਏ ਪੂਤ ॥ ਦੁੱਧ ਮੰਗਤ ਦੇ ਦੁੱਧ ਬਹੂਤ ॥੮॥

In this way Banda came to believe,\
and from then on his mind stood unshaken.\
Whoever asked for a son, he had a son granted;\
whoever asked for milk,[^9] he gave milk in plenty. (8)

ਜੇਕਰ ਕੋਈ ਦੁਖੀਆ ਆਵੈ ॥ ਕਰ ਅਰਦਾਸ ਤਿਸ ਦੂਖ ਮਿਟਾਵੈ ॥\
ਵਾਹਿਗੁਰੂ ਕਾ ਜਾਪ ਜਪਾਵੈ ॥ ਜੋ ਮਾਂਗੈ ਤਿਸ ਸੋਊ ਦਿਵਾਵੈ ॥੯॥

If anyone in distress came to him,\
he made the ardās and wiped his pain away;\
he had him chant the name Vahiguru,\
and whatever a man asked, he had it given him. (9)

ਐਸੀ ਜਗ ਮੈਂ ਪਰ ਗਈ ਧਾਂਕ ॥ ਆਇ ਮਿਲੈਂ ਰਾਣਾ ਔ ਰਾਂਕ ॥\
ਦੂਰਹਿ ਤੇ ਜੋ ਨਿੰਦਤ ਆਵੈ ॥ ਹੁਇ ਨੇੜੈ ਵਹੁ ਚਰਨੀ ਪਾਵੈ ॥੧੦॥

Such awe of him spread through the world\
that rana and pauper came to meet him;\
and whoever came from far off slandering him\
fell at his feet when he drew near. (10)

ਜਹਿਂ ਬੰਦਾ ਆਇ ਡੇਰਾ ਕਰੇ ॥ ਕਾਢ ਮੋਹਰ ਸੋ ਤਹਿਂ ਬਹੁ ਧਰੇ ॥\
ਦੀਵੈ ਪਾਵੈ ਤੇਲੀ ਤੇਲ ॥ ਇਕ ਮੋਹਰ ਤਿਹ ਦੇਵੈ ਮੇਲ ॥੧੧॥

Wherever Banda came and pitched his camp,\
he took out gold coins[^10] and heaped them there in plenty.\
If a Teli[^11] poured oil into the lamp,\
Banda would put a gold coin in his hand. (11)

ਠੂਠੀ ਭਾਂਡਾ ਲਯਾਵੈ ਘੁਮਯਾਰ ॥ ਦੇਵੈ ਮੋਹਰ ਕਢ ਖੀਸਯੋਂ ਡਾਰ ॥\
ਲਕੜੀ ਚੂਹੜੋ ਲਯਾਵੈ ਜੋਈ ॥ ਮੋਹਰ ਖੀਸਯੋਂ ਦੇਵੈ ਓਈ ॥੧੨॥

If a Ghumiar[^12] brought a cup or a pot,\
he took a gold coin from his pocket and dropped it in;\
and any Chuhra[^13] who brought in firewood,\
to him too he gave a gold coin from his pocket. (12)

*Dohra* ਦੋਹਰਾ

ਖਾਲੀ ਕਿਸੇ ਸੁ ਨਹਿ ਛਡੇ ਮੋਹਰੁ ਸੁ ਦਏ ਫੜਾਇ ॥\
ਜੋ ਆਵੈ ਔ ਸਜੂਦ ਹੁਇ ਤਿਹ ਸੰਗ ਲਏ ਚੜ੍ਹਾਇ ॥੧੩॥

He let no one go away empty-handed, but pressed a gold coin into his hand;\
and whoever came armed and ready,[^14] he took along with him on the march. (13)

Punjabi commentary from the Prachin Panth Prakash Steek, part 1, of Singh Sahib Baba Santa Singh Akali, Jathedar of the 96 Krori Budha Dal. Both commentators are named in full in the notes; the project docs abbreviate them KS and BSS. Cite as Panth Prakash 31.4.*

### Notes

[^1]: ardās: the Sikh prayer of petition, made standing before the Guru.
[^2]: Labana: a caste of Sikh traders who carried goods by bullock train; Makhan Shah, who found Guru Tegh Bahadur at Bakala, was a Labana.
[^3]: dasvaṅdh: the tenth of his earnings that a Sikh gives to the Guru. Baba Santa Singh Akali says the Labana Sikhs still give it in thousands of rupees, a custom kept in their families.
[^4]: Akal Ustat (Praise of the Timeless) and Chandi di Var (the Ballad of Chandi): compositions of Guru Gobind Singh. Baba Santa Singh Akali says their reading with the daily prayers is still the custom of the Budha Dal.
[^5]: Akal: the Timeless One, a name of God. Baba Santa Singh Akali takes the chant as the Khalsa's cry of Sat Sri Akal, raised when a Singh comes in sight of the Panth's banners.
[^6]: Vahiguru: the Sikh name of God, the Guru's mantra.
[^7]: the Guru's sons: the Sahibzadas of Guru Gobind Singh, the elder two killed in battle at Chamkaur and the younger two put to death at Sirhind.
[^8]: familiar spirits: the poet's bīr, the spirit servants Banda had mastered as a yogi (Episodes 28 and 29).
[^9]: sons and milk: the old blessing of a full house. Baba Santa Singh Akali takes the milk as the gift of buffaloes and cows.
[^10]: gold coins: the poet's mohar, the gold coin of the Mughal mint.
[^11]: Teli: the oil-pressers' caste. Baba Santa Singh Akali says it was the custom of the time for Telis to come and fill a ruler's lamps, and to be rewarded for it.
[^12]: Ghumiar: the potters' caste.
[^13]: Chuhra: the sweepers' caste, counted lowest of all.
[^14]: armed and ready: the poet's sajūd. Baba Santa Singh Akali reads it as a man come fully armed and mounted, a saint and a soldier.

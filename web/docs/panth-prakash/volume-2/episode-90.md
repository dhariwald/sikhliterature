# Panth Prakash, Episode 90

## The Episode of Kapur Singh's Nawabi

### (jai jai kār khālse grih bhayo: Cries of victory rang in the homes of the Khalsa.)

>**Questions**
>
>**Define:**  Subeg Singh (ਸੁਬੇਗ ਸਿੰਘ), tankhāh (ਤਨਖਾਹ), Darbara Singh (ਦਰਬਾਰਾ ਸਿੰਘ), Nawab Kapur Singh (ਨਵਾਬ ਕਪੂਰ ਸਿੰਘ), Akal Bunga (ਅਕਾਲ ਬੁੰਗਾ), the five jathas (ਪੰਜ ਡੇਰੇ, ਪੰਜ ਨਿਸ਼ਾਨ), Ranghreta (ਰੰਘਰੇਟਾ), Jassa Singh Ahluwalia (ਜੱਸਾ ਸਿੰਘ ਆਹਲੂਵਾਲ)
>
>**Summarize (2–3 sentences each):**
>
>1. Subeg Singh's mission, and why Darbara Singh refused the nawabship.
>2. How Kapur Singh became Nawab and ordered the Panth into five jathas.

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਮਾਰਯੋ ਜਬੈ ਬਿਵਾਸਤੇ ਚਿਤ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਵਧਯੋ ਹੁਲਾਸ ॥\
ਤੁਰਕਨ ਕੋ ਸੰਸੋ ਪਰਯੋ ਭਯੋ ਉਨ੍ਹੈ ਬਹੁ ਨਾਸ਼ ॥੧॥

When the Khalsa had struck down the men of the holy war,[^1] joy swelled in their hearts;\
dread fell upon the Turks,[^2] for great was the ruin that had come on them. (1)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਲਰਨ ਬਿਵਾਸਤੇ ਜੋ ਜੋ ਅਯੋ ॥ ਨਠਯੋ ਸੁ ਬਚਯੋ ਲੜਯੋ ਮਾਰ ਲਯੋ ॥\
ਤੁਰਕ ਤੋ ਪਿਛਲਨ ਆਨ ਦਬਾਏ ॥ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਢੁਕਨ ਨ ਪਾਈ ਮਾਏ ॥੨॥

Of all who had come to fight in that holy war,\
the one who fled was saved, and the one who stood and fought was killed.\
The Turks who fell, their kin came and buried;\
but no mother could come near the Singhs who fell.[^3] (2)

ਹੈ ਹੈ ਕਾਰ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਘਰ ਕਯੋ ॥ ਜੈ ਜੈ ਕਾਰ ਖਾਲਸੇ ਗ੍ਰਿਹ ਭਯੋ ॥\
ਐਸ ਭਈ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਕੀ ਕਲਾ ॥ ਥੋੜੇ ਖਾਲਸੇ ਲਸ਼ਕਰ ਬਹੁ ਗਲਾ ॥੩॥

Wailing and lament rose in the homes of the Turks,\
and cries of victory rang in the homes of the Khalsa.\
Such was the power of the Satguru at work:\
the Khalsa were few, and they wore a great army away to nothing. (3)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਅਸਲਮ ਖਾਨ ਨਿਬਾਬ ਕੇ ਮਨੈ ਭਏ ਦੁਇ ਭਾਇ ॥\
ਮੁਏ ਤਲਬੀ ਸੋ ਖੁਸ਼ ਭਯੋ ਮੁਏ ਦੀਨ ਸੋ ਪਛੁਤਾਇ ॥੪॥

Two feelings rose in the mind of Aslam Khan, the nawab:[^4]\
he was glad that the revenue men had died, and grieved for the men of the faith who had died.[^5] (4)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਹੁਤ ਨਬਾਬ ਮਨਸੂਬੇ ਬਾਜ ॥ ਉਨ ਸਮਝਯੋ ਭਲ ਅਪਨੋ ਕਾਜ ॥\
ਟਕੇ ਪੁਜਤ ਥੇ ਹਮ ਤੇ ਨਾਂਹਿ ॥ ਮੁਝ ਕੋ ਫੜ ਲੈ ਦਿੱਲੀ ਜਾਂਹਿ ॥੫॥

The nawab was a schemer, a man of plots,\
and he saw that the thing had turned out well for him.\
*The money could never have reached Delhi from me;\
they would have seized me and carried me off to Delhi.* (5)

ਭਲੀ ਭਈ ਜੋ ਉਇ ਮਰ ਗਏ ॥ ਭਲੇ ਭਾਗ ਸੋ ਸਿੰਘ ਆ ਪਏ ॥\
ਹਮਹਿ ਕਹਨ ਕੋ ਬਨਯੋ ਵਲਾਉ ॥ ਗ਼ਨੀਮ ਬਲੀ ਪਰਯੋ ਹਮ ਪਰ ਆਇ ॥੬॥

*It is well that they are dead;\
it was my good fortune that the Singhs fell on them.\
Now I have a pretext to say it:\
a mighty enemy came down upon us.* (6)

ਬੰਦੈ ਮਰਨੇ ਨ ਗ਼ਨੀਮ ਮਰ ਗਯੋ ॥ ਉਸ ਤੇ ਚੌਗੁਨ ਸੋ ਅਬ ਭਯੋ ॥\
ਇਮ ਲਿਖਿਕੈ ਉਨਿ ਦਿੱਲੀ ਘਲ ਦਯੋ ॥ ਸਿਰ ਸਦਕੇ ਸ਼ਾਹ ਲਸ਼ਕਰ ਭਯੋ ॥੭॥

"When Banda died, the enemy did not die;\
now it has grown four times greater than he was."\
So he wrote, and sent the letter to Delhi:\
"The Shah's army has given itself as a sacrifice for your head."[^6] (7)

ਬਿਵਾਸਤੇ ਕੀ ਗਲ ਉਨਿ ਨਹਿਂ ਕਹੀ ॥ ਨਿਜ ਢਬ ਕੀ ਉਨ ਬਾਤ ਲਿਖ ਦਈ ॥\
ਮਨਸੂਬੇ ਸੋ ਉਨ ਖੇਲਯੋ ਖੇਲ ॥ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਸਿਉਂ ਕਰਨ ਲਾਗਯੋ ਮੇਲ ॥੮॥

Of the holy war he said nothing at all;[^7]\
he wrote the story in his own way.\
He played his game by scheming,\
and set about making peace with the Singhs. (8)

ਅਸਲਮ ਖਾਂ ਬਡ ਸੂਬੇਦਾਰ ॥ ਉਨ ਲਿਖਯੋ ਪਤਿਸ਼ਾਹੈ ਪੇਚੇ ਨਾਰ ॥\
ਯਹਿ ਗ਼ਨੀਮ ਹੈ ਬੁਰੀ ਬਲਾਇ ॥ ਕਹੋ ਪ੍ਰਚਾਈਏ ਇਸ ਟੁਕ ਪਾਇ ॥੯॥

Aslam Khan, the great subedar,\
wrote to the Patshah with a cunning twist:\
"This enemy is an evil plague;\
say the word, and we will win them over by tossing them a crust. (9)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਗੁੜ ਦਿਤੈ ਦੁਸ਼ਮਨ ਮਰੈ ਕਿਮ ਮਹੁਰੌ ਖ਼ਰੀਦੀਏ ਧਾਇ ॥\
ਕਹੈਂ ਸਿਆਨੇ ਗਲ ਇਮੈ ਟੁੱਕ ਪਾਏ ਕੁੱਤਾ ਨ ਕਟਾਇ ॥੧੦॥

"If an enemy dies when you give him gur,[^8] why run out to buy poison?\
The wise put it this way: a dog thrown a crust does not bite. (10)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਕਹੋ ਤੋ ਉਸ ਸੋਂ ਛਲ ਕੋਊ ਕੀਜੈ ॥ ਫਿਰ ਮਾਰ ਲੇਹਿਂ ਜਬ ਆਵਨ ਪਤੀਜੈ ॥\
ਕਹੋ ਉਨੈਂ ਸਿਰ ਪਾਵ ਘਲਾਵਾਂ ॥ ਲੇਟ ਪੇਟ ਸੋਂ ਉਨ੍ਹੈਂ ਪਤਯਾਵਾਂ ॥੧੧॥

"Say the word, and some trick shall be played on them,\
and then we will kill them when they come to trust us;\
or say the word, and I shall send them a robe of honour\
and win their trust with smooth and winding talk." (11)

ਉਤ ਇਮ ਲਿਖ ਇਤ ਔਰੇ ਬਿਧ ਕਰੀ ॥ ਸਬੇਗ ਸਿੰਘ ਸਦਯੋ ਉਸ ਹੀ ਘਰੀ ॥\
ਕਹਯੋ ਜਾਹਿ ਤੂੰ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਵੱਲ ॥ ਹਮਰੀ ਉਨਕੀ ਕਰਾ ਦਿਹੁ ਗੱਲ ॥੧੨॥

So he wrote to Delhi, and here he took another course:\
that very hour he sent for Subeg Singh,[^9]\
and said, "Go to the Khalsa,\
and open talks between them and me. (12)

ਭਲੀ ਕਰੀ ਉਨ ਯਹ ਫੌਜ ਮਾਰੀ ॥ ਹਮਰੇ ਸਿਰ ਤੇ ਜਨ ਜੂੰ ਟਾਰੀ ॥\
ਐਸ ਭਾਂਤ ਜਾ ਉਨ ਸਮਝਈਓ ॥ ਉਇ ਹਮੇ ਬ੍ਰਾਦਰ ਹਮ ਉਸ ਭਈਓ ॥੧੩॥

"They did well to kill that army;\
it was as if they had picked a louse off my head.[^10]\
Go and make them understand it in this way:\
they are brothers to me, and I am a brother to them. (13)

ਤੁਮ ਹਮ ਸੁਖਦ ਹਮ ਤੁਮ ਸੁਖਦਾਇ ॥ ਰਲ ਮਿਲ ਲਈਐ ਮੁਲਕ ਬਸਾਇ ॥\
ਜਗੀਰ ਲਿਖਾਇ ਲਿਹੁ ਗੁਰ ਚੱਕ ਨਾਲ ॥ ਅਬ ਤੁਮ ਕਰੋ ਨ ਮੁਲਕ ਉਜਾਰ ॥੧੪॥

"'You bring me peace, and I bring you peace;\
let us join together and see the country settled.\
Have an estate[^11] written in the name of Amritsar,[^12]\
and lay the country waste no more.' (14)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਸਭੀ ਤੁਮਾਰੋ ਦੇਸ਼ ਹੈ ਸਭ ਤੁਮਰੇ ਅਜ਼ੀਜ਼ ਭਿਰਾਇ ॥\
ਉਨ ਹੀ ਕੋ ਦੁਖ ਕਿਮ ਦਯੌ ਕੱਯੈ ਉਨ ਸੁਖਦਾਇ ॥੧੫॥

"'The whole land is yours, and all its people are your own dear brothers;\
why give pain to them? Give them peace.'" (15)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਸਬੇਗ ਸਿੰਘ ਉਸ ਅਗਯੋਂ ਭਾਖੀ ॥ ਕਿਛੁ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਮੈਂ ਢਿਗ ਦੇ ਰਾਖੀ ॥\
ਕਿਛੁ ਤੋਫੋ ਤੁਫਾਯਤ ਔ ਮਗਰੋਂ ਦੇਹੁ ॥ ਤਊ ਬਣੈ ਤੁਮ ਸਯੋਂ ਉਨ ਨੇਹੁ ॥੧੬॥

Subeg Singh answered him in turn:\
"Give me something to set before the Khalsa;\
then send more gifts and presents after it;\
only so will they come to feel any warmth for you." (16)

ਵਹੀ ਬਾਤ ਨਿਬਾਬੈ ਮੰਨ ਲਈ ॥ ਮਤਲਬ ਅਪਨੈ ਤੁਰਤ ਆਨ ਦਈ ॥\
ਸਿਰੇ ਪਾਉ ਥੋ ਉਸ ਨੋ ਆਯੋ ॥ ਸੋਊ ਸੂਬੇਦਾਰੀ ਕੋ ਖ਼ਿਲਤ ਘਲਾਯੋ ॥੧੭॥

The nawab agreed to that very thing,\
and for his own ends he brought it out at once.\
A robe of honour had come to him,[^13]\
and that robe of the governorship he sent. (17)

#### ਪੁਸ਼ਾਕ ਨਿਬਾਬੀ ਕਪੂਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਸਿਰ ਧਰੀ
*The Robe of the Nawabship Is Placed on Kapur Singh's Head*

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਦੈ ਖਿਲਤ ਏਤੀ ਗਲ ਕਹੀ ॥ ਯਹ ਪੁਸ਼ਾਕ ਨ ਜਾਨਯੋ ਯਹਿ ਨਿਬਾਬੀ ਭਈ ॥\
ਇਸੀ ਸਾਥ ਹੋਗੁ ਲਿਖੀ ਜਗੀਰ ॥ ਇਸੀ ਸਾਥ ਹੋਗੁ ਮੁਲਕ ਤਤਬੀਰ ॥੧੮॥

As he gave the robe, he said this much:\
"Do not take this for a mere garment; this is a nawabship.\
With it shall come an estate, set down in writing;\
with it shall come the right to order the country. (18)

ਇਹ ਹੈ ਨਿਬਾਬੀ ਅਧ ਸੂਬੇਦਾਰੀ ॥ ਉਸੈ ਮਿਲੈਗੀ ਰੱਯਤ ਸਾਰੀ ॥\
ਸ਼ਾਹੀ ਪ੍ਰਵਾਨੋ ਉਸ ਨਾਇ ਆਵਗੁ ॥ ਉਹੀ ਵੰਡ ਸਭ ਲੋਗਨ ਖੁਲਾਵਗੁ ॥੧੯॥

"This nawabship is half the governorship;\
all the subjects of the land shall come to him;\
the Shah's written orders[^14] shall come in his name,\
and he shall be the one to give out grants to all the people. (19)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਸੂਰ ਸਤੀ ਦਾਤਾ ਹਠੀ ਤਪੀ ਜਪੀ ਜੋ ਕੋਇ ॥\
ਦੇਵਯੋ ਉਸ ਬਿਚਾਰ ਕੈ ਜੋ ਇਸ ਲਾਇਕ ਹੋਇ ॥੨੦॥

"Whoever is a warrior, a man of truth, a giver, a man who holds firm, an ascetic, a man of prayer,\
weigh it well, and give it to the one who is worthy of it." (20)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਦਈ ਨਗਦੀ ਖਰਚੇ ਤਾਈਂ ॥ ਸੁਬੇਗ ਸਿੰਘ ਤੁਰਯੋ ਜੁਕਤ ਬਣਾਈ ॥\
ਜਹਿਂ ਡੇਰੋ ਥੋ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਲਾਯੋ ॥ ਬਜਹਿਂ ਨਗਾਰੇ ਨਿਸ਼ਾਨ ਝੁਲਾਯੋ ॥੨੧॥

He gave him cash for the expenses,\
and Subeg Singh set out with his plan made ready,\
to where the Khalsa had pitched its camp,\
where the war drums beat and the banner swayed. (21)

ਜਾ ਪਹੁੰਚਯੋ ਵਹਿ ਸਿੰਘ ਸੁਜਾਨ ॥ ਆਗੈ ਲਗਯੋ ਹੁਤ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਦੀਵਾਨ ॥\
ਪੜ੍ਹੈਂ ਸ਼ਬਦ ਔ ਬਜੈ ਰਬਾਬ ॥ ਸੁੱਖੈ ਘੋਟੈਂ ਭੁੰਨੈਂ ਮਾਜ ਪ੍ਰਸ਼ਾਦਿ ॥੨੨॥

That wise Singh came up to them,\
and found the Khalsa seated in a dīvān.[^15]\
They were singing shabads, the rabāb[^16] was playing,\
others were grinding bhang and roasting meat.[^17] (22)

ਕੋ ਘੋੜੈ ਕੋ ਮਾਲਸ਼ ਕਰੈ ॥ ਕੋ ਸ਼ਸਤ੍ਰ ਮਾਂਜ ਉੱਜਲ ਕਰ ਧਰੈ ॥\
ਕੋਊ ਤੀਰ ਕੋ ਬੰਦੂਕ ਚਲਾਵਹਿ ॥ ਕੋਊ ਉਂਗਲ ਪਰ ਚੱਕ੍ਰ ਘੁਮਾਵਹਿ ॥੨੩॥

One was rubbing down a horse,\
one was scouring his weapons and laying them by, bright and shining;\
some were shooting arrows, some firing muskets,\
and some were spinning the chakra on a finger.[^18] (23)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਕੋਊ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਚਾਪੀ ਕਰੈ ਕੋਊ ਚੌਰ ਝਲਾਇ ॥\
ਕੋ ਪਾਨੀ ਲਯਾਵਹਿ ਦੂਰ ਤੇ ਬੈਠ ਇਸ਼ਨਾਨ ਕਰਾਹਿ ॥੨੪॥

Some were pressing the tired limbs of the Singhs, some waving the chaur over them;[^19]\
some fetched water from far off, and sat the Singhs down and bathed them. (24)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਕਈ ਸਿੰਘ ਖੜ ਦਾਤਨ ਕਰਾਹਿਂ ॥ ਕਈ ਸਿੰਘ ਖੜ ਕੱਛ ਬੰਨ੍ਹਾਂਹਿਂ ॥\
ਕਿਸੈ ਸ਼ਰੀਕੋ ਕਰੈ ਕੋਊ ਨਾਂਹਿ ॥ ਦੌੜ ਦੌੜ ਸਿੰਘ ਟਹਲ ਕਮਾਹਿਂ ॥੨੫॥

Many Singhs stood by while others cleaned their teeth with twigs;[^20]\
many stood by and helped others tie on their kachhehras.[^21]\
No one held any rivalry against another;\
the Singhs ran and ran to serve one another. (25)

ਦੇਖ ਸੁਬੇਗ ਸਿੰਘ ਛਡ ਦਯੋ ਘੋੜੈ ॥ ਕੁਨਸੈ ਕਰ ਕਰ ਹਥ ਦੁਇ ਜੋੜੈ ॥\
ਵਾਹਿਗੁਰੂ ਜੀ ਕੀ ਫਤੇ ਬੁਲਾਈ ॥ ਅਕਾਲ ਅਕਾਲ ਕਹਿ ਊਚੈ ਸੁਨਾਈ ॥੨੬॥

Seeing them, Subeg Singh left his horse,\
and came on, bowing low again and again, with both hands joined.\
He called out "Vahiguru ji ki Fateh,"[^22]\
and cried "Akal! Akal!" aloud for all to hear. (26)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਅਗਯੋਂ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਅਵਾਜ਼ ਸੁਨ ਧਰਯੋ ਸੀਸ ਪਰ ਹਾਥ ॥\
ਤੱਤ ਖਾਲਸੋ ਰੀਤਿ ਜੋ ਸਿੰਘ ਕਰਹਿਂ ਉਸ ਭਾਂਤ ॥੨੭॥

Hearing his voice, the Khalsa raised their hands to their heads in answer;\
that is the way of the Tatt Khalsa,[^23] and the Singhs did it in that way. (27)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਜਿਮ ਸੁਬੇਗ ਸਿੰਘ ਢਿਗ ਝੁਕ ਆਵੈ ॥ ਕਰੈ ਕੁੰਨਸ ਔ ਸੀਸ ਝੁਕਾਵੈ ॥\
ਦੇਖ ਖਾਲਸਾ ਹੋਇ ਪ੍ਰਸੰਨ ॥ ਕਹੈ ਖਾਲਸਾ ਅੱਗਯੋਂ ਧੰਨ ॥੨੮॥

As Subeg Singh came nearer, bent low,\
bowing to the ground and bowing his head,\
the Khalsa watched him and were well pleased,\
and the Khalsa answered, "Blessed are you." (28)

ਢੁਕ ਢਿਗ ਸਿੰਘ ਸੁ ਰਹਯੋ ਖਲੋਇ ॥ ਲਾਇ ਤਨਖਾਹ ਅਬ ਬਖ਼ਸ਼ੀਏ ਮੋਹਿ ॥\
ਹੈ ਖਾਲਸੋ ਸਭ ਬਖ਼ਸ਼ਨ ਜੋਗੁ ॥ ਹਮ ਤਨਖਾਹੀ ਘਰਬਾਰੀ ਲੋਗ ॥੨੯॥

Coming close, the Singh stood still before them:\
"Set me a tankhāh[^24] now, and forgive me.\
The Khalsa has the power to forgive all;\
I am a householder, a man who has earned a tankhāh." (29)

ਹੁਇ ਪ੍ਰਸੰਨ ਬਚ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਕੀਯੋ ॥ ਲਾਇ ਤਨਖਾਹਿ ਬਖ਼ਸ਼ਨ ਕਹਿ ਦੀਯੋ ॥\
ਖਾਲਸੈ ਹੁਕਮ ਕੀਯ ਪੰਜ ਭੁਜੰਗਨ ॥ ਤਨਖਾਹ ਮਨਾਇ ਬਖ਼ਸ਼ਯੋ ਭਲ ਰੰਗਨ ॥੩੦॥

Well pleased, the Khalsa gave its word:\
it told them to set him a tankhāh and forgive him.\
The Khalsa gave the command to five bhujaṅgīs;[^25]\
they had him accept the tankhāh, and forgave him with warm goodwill. (30)

ਸੁਬੇਗ ਸਿੰਘ ਤਊ ਪੰਥ ਬਹਾਯੋ ॥ ਕਰ ਆਦਰ ਉਸ ਪ੍ਰਸ਼ਨ ਪੁਛਾਯੋ ॥\
ਸੁਬੇਗ ਸਿੰਘ ਤਬ ਸਭ ਗਲ ਕਹੀ ॥ ਲਯਾਯੋ ਹੁਤੋ ਧਰ ਮੁਹਰੈ ਦਈ ॥੩੧॥

Then the Panth seated Subeg Singh among them,\
and with honour asked him why he had come.\
Subeg Singh told them the whole matter,\
and set before them the gold coins he had brought. (31)

ਕੋਊ ਸਿੰਘ ਕਹੈ ਲੁੱਟ ਮਚਾਯੋ ॥ ਕੋਊ ਕਹੈ ਇਸ ਦੂਰ ਹਟਾਯੋ ॥\
ਸੁਬੇਗ ਸਿੰਘ ਤਿਨ ਅਗਯੋਂ ਉਚਾਰੀ ॥ ਯਹ ਕਯਾ ਕਰੋ ਤੁਮ ਗਲ ਅਵਿਚਾਰੀ ॥੩੨॥

Some Singh said, "Let it be plundered!"\
Another said, "Push it far away from us."\
Subeg Singh answered them:\
"What is this? Why do you talk without thinking? (32)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਸੁਬੇਗ ਸਿੰਘ ਨੇ ਤਬ ਕਹਯੋ ਖਾਲਸੇ ਸੋਂ ਹੱਥ ਜੋੜ ॥\
ਗਰੀਬਨ ਕੀ ਰਛਯਾ ਕਰੋ ਲੇਹੁ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਤੇ ਤੋੜ ॥੩੩॥

Then Subeg Singh spoke to the Khalsa with joined hands:\
"Protect the poor, and come to terms with the Turks."[^26] (33)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਸੋਉ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਨੇ ਮੰਨ ਲਈ ॥ ਪਤਿਸ਼ਾਹੀ ਕੈ ਸਿਰ ਕੋੜ ਟਿਕਵਈ ॥\
ਤੁਰਕਨ ਭੇਜਯੋ ਤੁਸੀ ਕਿਮ ਮੋੜੋ ॥ ਖਾਹੋ ਵਰਤੋ ਔਰ ਲੈਣ ਲੋੜੋ ॥੩੪॥

The Khalsa accepted that counsel;\
it laid a leprosy on the head of the Patshahi.[^27]\
"The Turks have sent it; why should you send it back?\
Eat it, share it out, and look to take still more." (34)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਦਰਬ ਜੁ ਆਈ ਥੀ ਤਊ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਲਈ ਵਰਤਾਇ ॥\
ਕਹਯੋ ਦਰਬਾਰੇ ਸਿੰਘ ਕੋ ਤੂੰ ਨਿਬਾਬੀ ਲੈ ਸਿਰੋਪਾਉ ॥੩੫॥

The wealth that had come the Khalsa shared out among them,\
and they said to Darbara Singh,[^28] "You take the nawabship and the robe of honour." (35)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਦਰਬਾਰੈ ਸਿੰਘ ਅਗਯੋਂ ਕਹੀ ॥ ਅਸੀਂ ਨਿਬਾਬੀ ਕਦ ਚਹੈਂ ਲਈ ॥\
ਹਮ ਕੋ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਬਚਨ ਪਤਿਸ਼ਾਹੀ ॥ ਹਮ ਕੋ ਜਾਪਤ ਢਿਗ ਸੋਊ ਆਹੀ ॥੩੬॥

Darbara Singh answered them:\
"When would I ever want to take a nawabship?\
The Satguru's word has promised us Patshahi,\
and to me it seems that it is near. (36)

ਹਮ ਰਾਖਤ ਪਤਿਸ਼ਾਹੀ ਦਾਵਾ ॥ ਜਾਂ ਇਤਕੋ ਜਾਂ ਅਗਲੋ ਪਾਵਾ ॥\
ਜੋ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਸਿੱਖਨ ਕਹੀ ਬਾਤ ॥ ਹੋਗੁ ਸਾਈ ਨਹਿਂ ਖਾਲੀ ਜਾਤ ॥੩੭॥

"We hold a claim to Patshahi;\
we shall have it, either here or in the world to come.\
Whatever the Satguru has told his Sikhs\
shall surely be; it does not go empty. (37)

ਧ੍ਰੂ ਵਿਧਰਤ ਔ ਧਵਲ ਡੁਲਾਇ ॥ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਬਚਨ ਨ ਖਾਲੀ ਜਾਇ ॥\
ਪਤਿਸ਼ਾਹੀ ਛਡ ਕਿਮ ਲਹੈਂ ਨਿਬਾਬੀ ॥ ਪਰਾਧੀਨ ਜਿਹ ਮਾਂਹਿ ਖਰਾਬੀ ॥੩੮॥

"The Pole Star may shift and the bull that holds up the earth may shake,[^29]\
but the Satguru's word does not go empty.\
Why should we let go of Patshahi to take a nawabship,\
which means serving another, and is rotten through and through? (38)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਹਮ ਪਤਿਸ਼ਾਹੀ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਦਈ ਹੰਨੈ ਹੰਨੈ ਲਾਇ ॥\
ਜਹਿਂ ਜਹਿਂ ਬਹੈਂ ਜਮੀਨ ਮਲ ਤਹਿਂ ਤਹਿਂ ਤਖਤ ਬਨਾਇਂ ॥੩੯॥

"The Satguru has given us Patshahi, every saddle a throne;[^30]\
wherever we sit down and take hold of the land, there we shall make our throne." (39)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਇਸੀ ਭਾਂਤ ਬਹੁ ਸਿੱਖਨ ਕਹੀ ॥ ਹਮ ਕੋ ਲੋੜ ਨਿਬਾਬੀ ਨਹੀਂ ॥\
ਉਨ ਮਾਂਗੀ ਕਦ ਦਈ ਪਤਿਸ਼ਾਹੀ ॥ ਪੰਥ ਛਡ ਬਹਯੋ ਕਬ ਉਨ ਕੇ ਪਾਹੀ ॥੪੦॥

Many Sikhs spoke in this same way:\
"We have no need of a nawabship.\
When was Patshahi ever given for the asking?\
When would we leave the Panth to sit at their side? (40)

ਪੰਥ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਕੋ ਐਸੋ ਮੇਲ ॥ ਬਰੂਦ ਅਗਨ ਕੋ ਜੈਸੋ ਖੇਲ ॥\
ਕਿਸੈ ਸਿੰਘ ਕੋ ਯਹ ਬੀ ਦੇਹੋ ॥ ਟਹਲ ਕਰਤ ਕਿਸ ਕੋ ਲਖ ਲੇਹੋ ॥੪੧॥

"Between the Panth and the Turks there is the kind of friendship\
that gunpowder plays at with fire."\
Subeg Singh said, "Give this, too, to some Singh;\
look for someone busy at seva, and choose him."[^31] (41)

ਕਪੂਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਥੋ ਟਹਲ ਕਮਾਵਤ ॥ ਦੌੜ ਦੌੜ ਥੋ ਪਖਾ ਝੁਲਾਵਤ ॥\
ਉਨ ਝੱਲੀ ਤੇਗ਼ ਥੀ ਸਨੁਮੁਖ ਕੱਲੈ ॥ ਐਸ ਸਜੀ ਜਨ ਸਸਿ ਮ੍ਰਿਗ ਛੱਲੈ ॥੪੨॥

Kapur Singh[^32] was at his seva,\
running here and there, waving a fan over the saṅgat.\
He had taken a tegh-stroke[^33] full in the face, alone, facing the enemy,\
and the scar sat on him like the mark of the deer on the moon.[^34] (42)

ਜ਼ਖਮ ਕਚੋ ਥੋ ਲਗਯੋ ਤਤਕਾਲ ॥ ਕਹੈਂ ਦੇਖ ਉਸ ਸਿੰਘ ਨਿਹਾਲ ॥\
ਉਹੀ ਬਚਨ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਕੋ ਫੁਰਯੋ ॥ ਆਇ ਸਮਾਂ ਸਭ ਸਿੰਘ ਕੋ ਜੁਰਯੋ ॥੪੩॥

The wound was still raw, newly struck;\
the Singhs looked at him and called him blessed.\
That very word came into the mind of the Khalsa,\
and for every Singh the moment came together. (43)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਤਹਾਂ ਸਿੰਘ ਇਕ ਆਪਨੋ ਪੜ੍ਹਤ ਬਾਣੀ ਗੁਰ ਲਾਲ ॥\
ਟਹਲ ਮਹਲ ਤਾਕਉ ਮਿਲੈ ਜਾ ਕਉ ਸਾਧ ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾਲ ॥੪੪॥

Just then a Singh, a beloved son of the Guru, was reading bāṇī to himself:\
"He alone is given seva and the Mansion of the Lord, on whom the Holy One is merciful."[^35] (44)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਸਿੰਘ ਕਪੂਰ ਝਲੈ ਪੱਖੋ ਥੋਈ ॥ ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾ ਨਜ਼ਰ ਪੰਥ ਉਸ ਵਲ ਹੋਈ ॥\
ਅਵਾਜ਼ ਅਕਾਲੋਂ ਪੰਥਹਿ ਸੁਨ ਪਾਈ ॥ ਗੁਰੂ ਗ੍ਰੰਥ ਬਚ ਮੰਨ ਲਿਹੁ ਭਾਈ ॥੪੫॥

Kapur Singh was still waving the fan,\
and the Panth turned its eyes of grace toward him.\
The Panth heard a voice from Akal:[^36]\
"Accept the word of the Guru Granth, brothers. (45)

ਟਹਲ ਕਰਤ ਇਸ ਮਹਲ ਪੁਚਾਵੋ ॥ ਕਪੂਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਕੋ ਸਿਰੋਪਾਉ ਪਹਿਰਾਵੋ ॥\
ਕਹਯੋ ਪੰਥ ਚਕੋ ਸਿੰਘ ਭੁਜੰਗੀ ॥ ਕਰ ਕਰ ਕੁਨਸ ਉਸ ਆਖੀ ਚੰਗੀ ॥੪੬॥

"He is doing seva: raise him to the high place;\
put the robe of honour on Kapur Singh."\
The Panth said, "Pick it up, young Singh!"\
And bowing low again and again, he gave a fitting answer: (46)

ਪੰਜ ਭੁਜੰਗੀਅਨ ਚਰਨੀ ਛੁਹਾਇ ॥ ਧਰੋ ਸੀਸ ਮੋਹਿ ਪਵਿਤ੍ਰ ਕਰਾਇ ॥\
ਪੰਜ ਭੁਜੰਗੀਅਨ ਚਰਨ ਬਲ ਪਾਈ ॥ ਸਿੰਘ ਸਸੋ ਹੋਇ ਪ੍ਰਬਤ ਭਏ ਰਾਈ ॥੪੭॥

"Touch it to the feet of five bhujaṅgīs,\
and when it is made holy, place it on my head.\
By the strength of the feet of five bhujaṅgīs\
a hare becomes a lion, and a mustard seed a mountain. (47)

ਬਾਜਨ ਕੋ ਚਿਰੀਆਂ ਤੋਰ ਖਾਹਿਂ ॥ ਪਾਵੈ ਖਾਲਸੋ ਬਲ ਜਿਹ ਮਾਂਹਿ ॥\
ਹੋਇ ਪ੍ਰਸੰਨ ਬਿਧ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਕਰੀ ॥ ਪੁਸ਼ਾਕ ਨਿਬਾਬੀ ਕਪੂਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਸਿਰ ਧਰੀ ॥੪੮॥

"Sparrows tear hawks apart and eat them,\
when the Khalsa puts its strength into them."\
Well pleased, the Khalsa did as he asked,\
and placed the robe of the nawabship on Kapur Singh's head. (48)

#### 'ਕਪੂਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਨਿਬਾਬੀ ਭਈ'
*Kapur Singh Becomes Nawab*[^37]

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਕਪੂਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਨਬਾਬੀ ਭਈ ਤੌ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਵਧਯੋ ਪ੍ਰਤਾਪ ॥\
ਤੁਰਕ ਖੁਸ਼ਾਮਦ ਬਹੁ ਕਰੈਂ ਟਕੇ ਦਏਂ ਘਲ ਆਪ ॥੧॥

When Kapur Singh became Nawab, the glory of the Singhs grew;\
the Turks flattered them greatly, and sent the money of their own accord. (49)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਪੰਥ ਓਲੈ ਸੁਖ ਅਸਲਮ ਖਾਂ ਪਾਯੋ ॥ ਟਕੇ ਬਚੇ ਮਧ ਲਹੌਰ ਬਹਾਯੋ ॥\
ਪੰਥਹਿ ਕੀ ਵਹਿ ਘੂਰ ਘਲਾਵੈ ॥ ਬਲਾਇ ਬੁਰੀ ਵਹਿ ਪੰਥ ਬਤਾਵੈ ॥੨॥

Under the shelter of the Panth Aslam Khan found ease;\
the money he saved he kept in Lahore.\
He sent word to Delhi of the Panth's menace,\
and described the Panth as an evil plague.[^38] (50)

ਬਾਰਾਂ ਪਿੰਡ ਦਏ ਜਗੀਰ ਲਿਖਾਈ ॥ ਗੁਰੂ ਚੱਕ ਸਿਉਂ ਦਏ ਲਗਾਈ ॥\
ਕਹੈ ਸਿੰਘ ਮੈਂ ਰਖੇ ਪਰਚਾਇ ॥ ਜੇਊ ਹੁਤੇ ਥੇ ਬੁਰੀ ਬਲਾਇ ॥੩॥

He had twelve villages written over as an estate,\
and joined them to Amritsar.\
He said, "I have kept the Singhs won over,\
who were an evil plague before. (51)

ਟਕੇ ਮਹੀਨਾ ਅੱਗੇ ਲੇਹਿਂ ॥ ਤੌ ਮੁਲਖਈਏ ਦੁੱਖ ਨ ਦੇਹਿਂ ॥\
ਹਮਰੀ ਉਨਕੀ ਐਸੀ ਪ੍ਰੀਤ ॥ ਬਰੂਦ ਅਗ਼ਨ ਕੀ ਜੈਸੀ ਰੀਤਿ ॥੪॥

"They take their money a month in advance,\
and so they do not trouble the people of the country.\
Between them and me there is a love\
like the love of gunpowder and fire. (52)

ਜੇਕਰ ਆਵੈਂ ਮੇਰੇ ਦਾਇ ॥ ਬੰਦੈ ਜਿਮ ਦੇਊਂ ਅਲਕ ਮਿਟਾਇ ॥\
ਦਈ ਨਿਬਾਬੀ ਕਰਨਿ ਧਿਜਾਇ ॥ ਉਸ ਫੜ ਲਯਾਵੋਂ ਤੁਮਰੇ ਪਾਇ ॥੫॥

"If ever they fall into my trap,\
I shall wipe out every trace of them, as I did Banda.\
I gave the nawabship to win their trust;\
I shall seize him and bring him to your feet." (53)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਸ਼ਾਹਿ ਕਹੀ ਜਿਮ ਨੇਕ ਹੁਇ ਤਿਮ ਹੀ ਲਓ ਬਣਾਇ ॥\
ਹਮ ਮੁਖਤਯਾਰੀ ਤੁਝ ਦਈ ਤੁਝ ਤੇ ਕੌਣ ਦਨਾਇ ॥੬॥

The Shah said, "Whatever way is good, settle it that way;\
I have given you full authority; who is wiser than you?" (54)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਫੇਰ ਬਾਤ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਘਰ ਆਈ ॥ ਸੁਨੋਂ ਸੰਤ ਔ ਗੁਰਮੁਖ ਭਾਈ ॥\
ਪੰਥ ਡੇਰਾ ਆਇ ਗੁਰ ਚੱਕ ਪਾਯੋ ॥ ਨਿਤ ਨਿਤ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਹੁਵੈ ਸਵਾਯੋ ॥੭॥

Now the story came back to the house of the Singhs;\
listen, you saints and brothers who follow the Guru.\
The Panth came and made its camp at Amritsar,\
and day by day the Khalsa grew greater and greater. (55)

ਨਿਤਪ੍ਰਤਿ ਆਵਹਿਂ ਟਕੇ ਲਹੌਰ ॥ ਨਿਬਾਬ ਵਰਤਾਵਹਿ ਪੰਥਹਿ ਕੋਰ ॥\
ਅਕਾਲ ਬੁੰਗੈ ਬਹਿ ਲਗੇ ਦਿਵਾਨ ॥ ਕਰੈਂ ਪ੍ਰਾਤਿ ਸਰ ਅੰਮ੍ਰਿਤ ਸ਼ਨਾਨ ॥੮॥

Every day the money came from Lahore,\
and the Nawab shared it out, each his portion, to the Panth.\
They sat in dīvān at the Akal Bunga,[^39]\
and at dawn they bathed in the sarovar of Amritsar.[^40] (56)

ਕਪੂਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਪਰ ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾ ਪੰਥ ਕਰੀ ॥ ਭਲੀ ਬੁੱਧਿ ਆਇ ਤਿਸ ਮੈਂ ਪਰੀ ॥\
ਬਹੁਤ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕੋ ਆਦਰ ਧਰੈ ॥ ਬਿਨਾਂ ਪੁਛੇ ਪੰਥ ਗੱਲ ਨ ਕਰੈ ॥੯॥

The Panth had shown its grace to Kapur Singh,\
and good understanding came and settled in him.\
He held the Singhs in great honour,\
and did nothing without asking the Panth. (57)

ਟਹਲ ਅਗੈ ਤੇ ਕਰੈ ਸਵਾਈ ॥ ਬਹੁਤ ਗਰੀਬੀ ਉਸ ਮਨ ਆਈ ॥\
ਜਿਮ ਜਿਮ ਪੰਥਹਿ ਟਹਿਲ ਕਮਾਵੈ ॥ ਤਿਮ ਤਿਮ ਸਿੰਘ ਜੀ ਗੁਰਮਤਿ ਆਵੈ ॥੧੦॥

He did still more seva than before,\
and great humility came into his heart.\
The more he served the Panth,\
the more the Guru's teaching came into Singh ji. (58)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਟਕੋ ਜੁ ਆਵੈ ਇਤ ਉਤੈ ਸੋ ਧਰੈ ਦਿਵਾਨਹਿ ਪਾਸ ॥\
ਦਿਵਾਨ ਬ੍ਰਤਾਵੈ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਕਰ ਕਰ ਨਿਤ ਅਰਦਾਸ ॥੧੧॥

Whatever money came in from here and there, he laid it before the diwan;[^41]\
the diwan shared it out to the Khalsa, saying the ardās[^42] every day. (59)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਹਰੀ ਸਿੰਘ ਇਕ ਲਾਂਗਰੀ ਥੋਊ ॥ ਹਾਥ ਪਦਮ ਨਹਿਂ ਮੁਕੈ ਰਸੋਊ ॥\
ਦਾਣੈ ਦਾਰ ਕਰਯੋ ਜੱਸਾ ਸਿੰਘ ਬਾਲ ॥ ਆਹਲੂਵਾਲ ਜੁ ਹੁਤੋ ਕਲਾਲ ॥੧੨॥

Hari Singh was a cook of the laṅgar;[^43]\
his hand bore the lotus mark, and his kitchen never ran short.[^44]\
The boy Jassa Singh was made keeper of the grain,[^45]\
he who came from Ahluwal and was a Kalal by caste.[^46] (60)

ਇਕ ਥਾਂ ਤੇ ਸਭ ਲੇਹੁ ਪੁਸ਼ਾਕ ॥ ਇਕਹਿ ਥਾਹਿ ਸਭ ਦੇਵਹਿ ਰਾਖ ॥\
ਇੱਕ ਥਾਂਇ ਸਭ ਰਖਹਿਂ ਕਮਾਈ ॥ ਰਖੈ ਨਹੀਂ ਕੋਊ ਤਿਸੈ ਛਪਾਈ ॥੧੩॥

All took their clothing from one place,\
and all gave it back to be kept in one place;\
all kept their earnings in one place,\
and no one kept any of it hidden away. (61)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਤੋਪ ਰਹਕਲੈ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਪੈ ਸੋ ਤੋ ਚਕੇ ਨ ਜਾਂਹਿ ॥\
ਜੰਬੂਰੈਂ ਜੰਜਾਇਲ ਸੋ ਸਿੰਘ ਚਲੈਂ ਉਠਾਇ ॥੧੪॥

The Singhs had cannon and field guns, but those could not be lifted;[^47]\
the swivel guns and the long muskets the Singhs carried with them on the march.[^48] (62)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਕਪੂਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕਹਿ ਦੀਓ ॥ ਚੱਕਨ ਵਾਰਨ ਘਿਉ ਸੇਰ ਕੀਓ ॥\
ਗੁਰਬਖ਼ਸ ਸਿੰਘ ਹੁੰਦਿਲ ਹੁਤ ਝੀਤੈ ਗ੍ਰਾਮ ॥ ਸੌਂਪੈ ਜੰਬੂਰੇ ਤਿਸਕੇ ਨਾਮ ॥੧੫॥

Kapur Singh gave the Singhs his order:\
for those who carried them, a ser of ghee was fixed.[^49]\
Gurbakhsh Singh, a Hundal, was of the village of Jhita;[^50]\
the swivel guns were handed over in his name. (63)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਜੋ ਨਿਸ ਸਿੰਘ ਬਾਹਿਰ ਰਹੈ ਜੋ ਬਿਨ ਪੁਛੈ ਘਰ ਜਾਇ ॥\
ਸੋਊ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਨਹਿਂ ਰਲੈ ਬਿਨ ਲਾਈ ਤਨਖਾਹਿ ॥੧੬॥

A Singh who stayed out for the night, or went home without asking,\
could not rejoin the Khalsa until a tankhāh had been set him. (64)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਜੋ ਘਰ ਤੇ ਹੁਇ ਆਵੈ ਕੋਇ ॥ ਬਖਸ਼ਾਇ ਰਲੈ ਖੜ ਆਪੇ ਸੋਇ ॥\
ਦਰਬ ਲਯਾਇ ਜੋ ਬਾਹਰੋਂ ਕੋਈ ॥ ਪਾਵੈ ਖਜ਼ਾਨੈ ਇਕ ਥਾਂ ਸੋਈ ॥੧੭॥

Anyone who came back after a visit home\
would stand up of his own accord, be forgiven, and rejoin them.\
Anyone who brought wealth in from outside\
put it into the treasury in the one place. (65)

ਕਿਛਕ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਨੇ ਕਟਯੋ ਅਰਾਮ ॥ ਕਈ ਸਿੰਘ ਗਏ ਮਿਲਨੇ ਧਾਮ ॥\
ਕਈਅਨ ਨਗਰਨ ਬੁੰਗੇ ਬਨਾਏ ॥ ਕਈ ਬੈਠ ਧ੍ਰਮਸਾਲ ਚਿਣਾਏ ॥੧੮॥

The Singhs passed some time at rest;\
many Singhs went to visit their homes.\
Many built bungas in their towns,\
and many settled down and had dharamsals[^51] built. (66)

ਕਈਅਨ ਖੇਤੀ ਜੁਗਤਿ ਕਰਾਈ ॥ ਕਈ ਲਏ ਘਰ ਨਾਰਨ ਪ੍ਰਚਾਈ ॥\
ਕਈ ਰਖੇ ਘਰ ਘੇਰ ਸੁ ਮਾਤ ॥ ਕਈ ਲਿਆਏ ਔਰ ਬਹੁ ਸਾਥ ॥੧੯॥

Many set their farming in order;\
many were won over at home by their wives;\
many were hemmed in and kept at home by their mothers;\
and many brought many more back with them. (67)

ਬਹੁਤ ਪੰਥ ਕੋ ਬਾਧਾ ਹੁਯੋ ॥ ਇਕ ਥਾਂ ਬਹੁਤੇ ਥੰਭਿ ਨ ਭਯੋ ॥\
ਤਉ ਨਿਬਾਬ ਚਿਤ ਕੀਯੋ ਬਿਚਾਰ ॥ ਕੋਊ ਜੁਗਤ ਬਣਾਈਐ ਜਿਤ ਹੋਇ ਸੰਭਾਰ ॥੨੦॥

The Panth grew very great,\
and so many could not be held in one place.\
Then the Nawab thought it over in his mind:\
*Some plan must be made by which they can all be cared for.* (68)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਉਸੀ ਦਿਨ ਸੱਦ ਖਾਲਸੋ ਅਕਾਲ ਬੁੰਗੈ ਤਖਤ ਮਕਾਨ ॥\
ਦੀਨੋ ਸਭਨ ਉਚਾਰ ਢਿਗ ਨਿਬਾਬੈ ਸਿੰਘ ਸੁਜਾਨ ॥੨੧॥

That same day he called the Khalsa together at the Akal Bunga, the seat of the Takht,\
and the wise Singh, the Nawab, set it all out before them. (69)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਭਾਈ ਮਨੀ ਸਿੰਘ ਦਰਬਾਰ ਪੁਜਾਰੀ ॥ ਲੀਨੋ ਸਬਹਨ ਮਧ ਬੈਠਾਈ ॥\
ਤੇਹਨ ਭੱਲੇ ਦੋਊ ਬਾਵੈ ਬੁਲਾਇ ॥ ਕਾਹਨ ਸਿੰਘ ਔ ਬਨੋਜ ਸਿੰਘ ਥਾਇ ॥੨੨॥

Bhai Mani Singh, the priest who served the Darbar,[^52]\
they seated in the midst of them all.\
They called the two Babas, the Trehan and the Bhalla,\
Kahan Singh and Binod Singh by name.[^53] (70)

ਸਭੀ ਪੰਥ ਜੇ ਨਾਮ ਲਿੱਖਯੈ ॥ ਗ੍ਰੰਥ ਵਧੈ ਔ ਅੰਤ ਨ ਪੱਯੈ ॥\
ਨਬਾਬ ਕਪੂਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਸਭ ਸੋਂ ਕਹੀ ॥ ਯਹੀ ਬਾਤ ਅਬ ਮੈਂ ਮਨ ਅਈ ॥੨੩॥

If I were to write the names of the whole Panth,\
this book would grow and never find its end.\
Nawab Kapur Singh said to them all,\
"This is the thing that has now come into my mind. (71)

ਅਗੈ ਪੰਥ ਥੋ ਪਿੰਡ ਪਿੰਡ ਫਿਰਤੋ ॥ ਆਪੋ ਅਪਨੀ ਗੁਜਰਹਿਂ ਕਰਤੋ ॥\
ਅਬ ਇਕ ਥਾਂ ਪੁਜੈ ਲੰਗਰ ਨਾਂਹੀ ॥ ਕੋਊ ਜੁਗਤਿ ਅਬ ਲਯੋ ਬਨਾਈ ॥੨੪॥

"Before, the Panth went about from village to village,\
and each band found its own living.\
Now, in one place, the laṅgar cannot feed us all;\
so let us make some plan now. (72)

ਸਿੰਘਨ ਦੱਯੈ ਜਥੋ ਬਣਾਇ ॥ ਦੇ ਦੇ ਖਰਚ ਉਸ ਦੱਯੈ ਪ੍ਰਚਾਇ ॥\
ਸਭੀ ਖਾਲਸੈ ਸੋ ਮੰਨ ਲਈ ॥ ਪਸੰਦ ਸਭਨ ਕੇ ਯਹਿ ਗਲ ਅਈ ॥੨੫॥

"Let the Singhs be formed into jathas,[^54]\
and let each be given its expenses, so that it is content."\
The whole Khalsa agreed to it;\
this counsel pleased them all. (73)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਪੰਜ ਡੇਰਨ ਬਿਧ ਰਚ ਦਈ ਪੰਜ ਨਿਸ਼ਾਨ ਬਨਾਇ ॥\
ਪੰਜੇ ਝੰਡੇ ਤੌ ਗਡੇ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਅਕਾਲ ਬੁੰਗੇ ਤੇ ਲਯਾਇ ॥੨੬॥

They laid down the order of five camps, and five standards were made;\
all five flags were brought and planted at Sri Akal Bunga. (74)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਪ੍ਰਥਮ ਸ਼ਹੀਦਨ ਔ ਨਿਹੰਗਨ ਫੜਾਯੋ ॥ ਦੀਪ ਸਿੰਘ ਸਿੰਘ ਕਰਮ ਸੁ ਨਾਯੋ ॥\
ਦੂਏ ਕਰਮ ਧਰਮ ਸਿੰਘ ਅੰਮ੍ਰਤਸਰੀਏ ॥ ਦਯੋ ਉਨੈ ਥੋਊ ਜਾਤ ਖਤਰੀਏ ॥੨੭॥

The first was put into the hands of the Shahīds and the Nihangs,[^55]\
Deep Singh and Karam Singh by name.\
The second went to Karam Singh and Dharam Singh of Amritsar;\
it was given to them, and they were Khatris by caste. (75)

ਤੀਜੋ ਚਕਯੋ ਬਾਬਨ ਬਡ ਅੰਸੀ ॥ ਤੇਹਣ ਭੱਲੇ ਥੇ ਆਦਿ ਗੁਰ ਅੰਸੀ ॥\
ਚੌਥੋ ਸਿੰਘ ਦਸੌਂਧਾ ਨਾਮ ॥ ਗਿੱਲ ਜੱਟ ਕੋਟ ਬੁਢੈ ਥੋ ਧਾਮ ॥੨੮॥

The third was taken up by the Babas of the great line,\
the Trehan and the Bhalla, of the blood of the first Gurus.\
The fourth went to a Singh named Dasaundha,\
a Gill Jatt, whose home was at Kot Buddha.[^56] (76)

ਪੰਜਵੋ ਰੰਘਰੇਟੋ ਬੀਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਨਾਇ ॥ ਹੁਤੋ ਤੇਰਾਂ ਸੈ ਘੋੜੇ ਵਾਇ ॥\
ਐਸੀ ਲੀਨੀ ਜੁਗਤ ਬਨਾਇ ॥ ਜੋ ਸੁਨੀ ਰਤਨ ਸਿੰਘ ਸੋ ਦਈ ਗਾਇ ॥੨੯॥

The fifth went to a Ranghreta[^57] named Bir Singh,\
who had thirteen hundred horse with him.\
Such was the plan they made;\
as Rattan Singh heard it, so he has sung it. (77)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਜੇ ਕੋ ਪੁੱਛੇ ਪ੍ਰਸ਼ਨ ਯਹਿ ਨਬਾਬ ਕਪੂਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਕੀ ਜਾਤ ॥\
ਕੌਣ ਪਿੰਡ ਔ ਗੋਤ ਕੋਇ ਸੋਈ ਸੁਨੋਂ ਅਬ ਬਾਤ ॥੩੦॥

If anyone should ask this question, what was the caste of Nawab Kapur Singh,\
what was his village and what his clan, then hear that story now. (78)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਫਰਜੁੱਲੇ ਖਾਂ ਥੋ ਮੁਗ਼ਲਹਿ ਨਾਮ ॥ ਉਨੈਂ੍ਹ ਬਸਾਯੋ ਫ੍ਰਜੁੱਲੇ ਗ੍ਰਾਮ ॥\
ਹੁਤੀ ਖਾਨੂ ਨਬਾਬ ਖਾਨੋ ਹਮਸ਼ੀਰਾ ॥ ਫਰਜੁਲੈ ਕੈ ਘਰ ਵਸਤੀ ਧੀਰਾ ॥੩੧॥

There was a Mughal named Farzulla Khan,\
and he had founded the village of Farzulla.[^58]\
Khano, the sister of the nawab Khan Bahadur,[^59]\
lived as a wife in Farzulla's house. (79)

ਹੁਤੀ ਹਮਾਇਤਨ ਬਡੀ ਕਮਜਾਤ ॥ ਉਨ ਬਹੁ ਸਿੰਘ ਥੇ ਕਰਾਏ ਘਾਤ ॥\
ਪੰਦ੍ਰਾਂ ਸੈ ਜੁ ਹੁਤੇ ਚਕਰੈਲ ॥ ਸੋ ਉਨ ਮਰਵਾਏ ਕਰ ਕਰ ਫੈਲ ॥੩੨॥

She stood behind the Turks, a wicked, low-born woman,\
and she had brought about the slaughter of many Singhs.\
Fifteen hundred who were in the rulers' service,\
she had them all killed by her schemes.[^60] (80)

ਤਹਿਂ ਕਪੂਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਹੁਤੇ ਪਿਤ ਮਾਤ ॥ ਬਿਰਕ ਗੋਤ ਔ ਜੱਟ ਥੀ ਜਾਤ ॥\
ਵਹਿ ਘਰ ਸੇ ਨਿਕਸਯੋ ਸਾਇਤ ਕਿਤ ਖਰੀ ॥ ਬਹੁਤ ਟਹਿਲ ਉਨ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਕਰੀ ॥੩੩॥

There lived Kapur Singh's father and mother;\
Virk was their clan, and Jatt their caste.[^61]\
He left that home at some blessed hour,\
and did much seva for the Singhs. (81)

ਉਸੀ ਟਹਲ ਤੇ ਯਹਿ ਪਦ ਪਾਯੋ ॥ ਸਰਬ ਪੰਥ ਕੋ ਨਵਾਬ ਸਦਾਯੋ ॥\
ਟਹਲ ਕਰੀ ਕਿਸ ਬ੍ਰਿਥੀ ਨ ਜਾਇ ॥ ਯਹਿ ਖਾਲਸੋ ਖੁਦ ਖੁਦੈ ਖੁਦਾਇ ॥੩੪॥

By that same seva he won this rank,\
and was called the Nawab of the whole Panth.\
No one's seva ever goes for nothing;\
this Khalsa is khud khudā, God's own self.[^62] (82)

ਦੋਹਰਾ *Dohra*

ਏਕ ਦਿਵਸ ਇਸ ਨਵਾਬ ਕੈ ਮਨ ਮੈਂ ਆਈ ਮੌਜ ॥\
ਔਰ ਸਿੰਘਨ ਪੌਰਖ ਕੀਏ ਮੈਂ ਨਿਜ ਕੀਓ ਨ ਕੌਜ ॥੩੫॥

One day a whim came into this Nawab's mind:\
*The other Singhs have done deeds of prowess; I have done no deed of my own.* (83)

ਚੌਪਈ *Chaupai*

ਚੜਯੋ ਨਿਬਾਬ ਕਿਹ ਖੇਲਨ ਸ਼ਿਕਾਰ ॥ ਸਾਥ ਕੁੱਲ ਲਏ ਬੀਸ ਅਸਵਾਰ ॥\
ਜਾਤ ਜਾਤ ਜਾ ਪੁਜਯੋ ਲਹੌਰ ॥ ਜਾਇ ਬੈਠਯੋ ਸੁ ਚਬੂਤਰੈ ਠੌਰ ॥੩੬॥

The Nawab rode out to hunt,\
and took with him only twenty horsemen in all.\
Riding on and on, he came all the way to Lahore,\
and went and sat down on the platform of justice.[^63] (84)

ਪਹਰ ਦੋਇ ਤਹਿਂ ਹੁਕਮ ਚਲਾਇ ॥ ਤੌ ਖਬਰ ਪੁੰਨੀ ਤੁਰਕ ਕਿਲੈ ਜੁ ਜਾਇ ॥\
ਤੌ ਖਿਜ਼ਰ ਖਾਨ ਦਯੋ ਨਿਬਾਬ ਚੜ੍ਹਾਇ ॥ ਪਕੜ ਕਪੂਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਮੋ ਪਹਿ ਲਿਯਾਇ ॥੩੭॥

For six hours he gave his commands there;\
then the news reached the Turks in the fort,\
and the nawab sent Khizar Khan out against him:\
"Seize Kapur Singh and bring him to me." (85)

ਕਪੂਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਮੁੜ ਪਿੱਛੈ ਧਯੋ ॥ ਨਿਗਾਰਨ ਡੱਗੈ ਲਾਇ ਮੁੜਯੋ ॥\
ਹੁਇ ਬਾਹਰ ਸਿੰਘ ਰਲ ਗਯੋ ਝਾੜੀਂ ॥ ਗਯੋ ਖੇਡ ਕਰ ਐਸ ਖਿਡਾਰੀ ॥੩੮॥

Kapur Singh turned and rode back,\
beating his war drums as he went;\
once outside the city, the Singh was lost in the bush;\
such a player was he, he played his game and was gone. (86)

ਇਸੀ ਭਾਂਤ ਗਏ ਕਿਛੁ ਦਿਨ ਬੀਤ ॥ ਦੰਗਾ ਰਹੈ ਪੰਥ ਹਰਦਮ ਚੀਤ ॥\
ਜੋ ਟਕਯਨ ਮੈਂ ਕਰੈ ਦੇਰ ਨਿਬਾਬ ॥ ਸਿੰਘ ਖਾਹਿਂ ਲੁਟ ਮੁਲਖ ਸ਼ਿਤਾਬ ॥੩੯॥

In this way some days went by,\
but fighting was in the Panth's mind every moment;\
if ever the nawab was slow with the money,\
the Singhs at once plundered the country and lived on it. (87)

### Notes

[^1]: ਬਿਵਾਸਤੇ, read as the war fought for the faith (ba-wāsta, "for the sake of"; BSS: for dīn and mazhab). Kulwant Singh: the combined forces of the bigoted Muslims. BSS places the battle at Bhilowal (Episode 89).
[^2]: Turks: the poet's word for the Muslim rulers and their armies.
[^3]: BSS: the martyred Singhs lay where they fell, as is the way of the shahīds, and not even a mother came near them; the Turks' dead were picked out and buried by their families.
[^4]: Aslam Khan: the poet's nawab of Lahore at this time.
[^5]: ਤਲਬੀ, the men sent to demand (talab) the revenue. BSS: a force of a thousand Pathans come from Delhi to collect the revenue owed; the men of the faith were the holy warriors and Aslam Khan's own Lahore troops.
[^6]: ਸਿਰ ਸਦਕੇ: given as an offering (sadqa) for the Shah's head, that is, they died for him.
[^7]: BSS: he hid that the campaign was his own holy war against the Singhs, and that the Delhi force had joined it and lent its guns.
[^8]: gur: ਗੁੜ, unrefined cane sugar.
[^9]: Subeg Singh: a Sikh in the service of the rulers at Lahore. BSS: he was the nawab's envoy, and went back and forth to the Panth.
[^10]: BSS: the Delhi force had been like a louse on his head, always making him scratch.
[^11]: estate: ਜਗੀਰ, jagir, a grant of land and its revenue.
[^12]: Amritsar: the poet's ਗੁਰ ਚੱਕ, Guru ka Chakk.
[^13]: BSS: it had come to him from Delhi or from somewhere else. robe of honour: siropāu (ਸਿਰੇ ਪਾਉ); khilat (ਖ਼ਿਲਤ) is the robe given with an office.
[^14]: written orders: ਪ੍ਰਵਾਨੋ, parwana.
[^15]: dīvān: the Khalsa's gathering, here an assembly for bāṇī and kīrtan.
[^16]: rabāb: the stringed instrument of kīrtan.
[^17]: bhang: ਸੁੱਖਾ, sukkhā, the Nihangs' name for it (BSS: the shahīdī degh, ground and made ready). The poet's ਮਾਜ ਪ੍ਰਸ਼ਾਦਿ is read with Kulwant Singh as meat.
[^18]: arrows: tīr · muskets: bandūk · chakra: ਚੱਕ੍ਰ, the steel throwing ring with a sharp edge, spun on the finger before it is thrown. BSS: they were practising.
[^19]: chaur: the whisk waved over the Guru, here waved over the Singhs. BSS: they had come from far away, and served one another.
[^20]: twigs: ਦਾਤਨ, dātan, a twig chewed to clean the teeth. BSS: one Singh stood holding the water while another cleaned his teeth.
[^21]: kachhehra: ਕੱਛ, the Sikh's breeches. BSS: as one bathed, another held out a fresh kachhehra and kept his waistband for him.
[^22]: Fateh: the Khalsa greeting, "Vahiguru ji ka Khalsa, Vahiguru ji ki Fateh" (the Khalsa belongs to Vahiguru, the victory belongs to Vahiguru).
[^23]: Tatt Khalsa: the true Khalsa. BSS: Subeg Singh, too, was of the Tatt Khalsa, and the Singhs answered him in its way, raising both hands above their heads.
[^24]: tankhāh: a penance set by the Khalsa for a breach of its discipline. BSS: a Singh who came back from home or from outside stood ten paces off, the Khalsa said an ardās for him, and then set him his penance.
[^25]: bhujaṅgī: a young Singh of the Khalsa.
[^26]: ਲੇਹੁ ਤੁਰਕਨ ਤੇ ਤੋੜ, read as settling with the Turks (Kulwant Singh: striking a deal). BSS: help the poor first, and break with the Turks later; a pact does not bind you for ever.
[^27]: ਕੋੜ, read with BSS as kohṛ, leprosy: the Patshah now paying tribute to the enemy he despised. Kulwant Singh reads it as a crore, ten million rupees, demanded from the Mughals.
[^28]: Darbara Singh: BSS: the Diwan, the leader of the Panth at that time, who had earlier served in Guru Gobind Singh's court.
[^29]: the bull: ਧਵਲ, dhaval, the white bull of the old belief that bears the earth on its horns.
[^30]: every saddle a throne: ਹੰਨੈ ਹੰਨੈ, from hannā, a saddle, the Guru's word that every horseman of the Khalsa shall be a Patshah.
[^31]: Subeg Singh is named from BSS; the verse names no speaker for lines 3 and 4. BSS: someone who clears the horses' dung, scours the pots or fetches wood for the laṅgar.
[^32]: Kapur Singh: Nawab Kapur Singh (died 1753), leader of the Khalsa after this episode.
[^33]: tegh: ਤੇਗ਼, the curved sword.
[^34]: the mark of the deer on the moon: the dark marks that Indian poets saw as a deer (mṛiga) on the moon's face.
[^35]: Sri Guru Granth Sahib, Raag Gauri, Bavan Akhari, Mahalla 5; Ang 255: *ṭahal mahal tā kau milai jā kau sādh kripāl* (he alone obtains seva and the Mansion of the Lord's presence, on whom the Holy One is merciful). The source prints ਤਾਕਉ as one word.
[^36]: ਅਵਾਜ਼ ਅਕਾਲੋਂ, a voice from Akal, or from the Akal Bunga. BSS: a leading Singh on the Akal Takht spoke up, pointing to the line of bāṇī.
[^37]: The source starts its verse numbers again at ॥੧॥ under this heading; the English numbering runs on, 49 to 87.
[^38]: ਘੂਰ, a threatening glare, read with Kulwant Singh as the threat Aslam Khan held up to Delhi. BSS reads it as the Panth glaring at Aslam Khan whenever the money was late.
[^39]: Akal Bunga: the Akal Takht, the Takht facing Sri Harimandar Sahib · bunga: a building for Sikhs to stay in near a gurdwara.
[^40]: sarovar of Amritsar: ਸਰ ਅੰਮ੍ਰਿਤ, the pool of amrit round Sri Harimandar Sahib.
[^41]: diwan: BSS: Diwan Darbara Singh.
[^42]: ardās: the Sikh prayer of supplication.
[^43]: laṅgar: the Guru's free kitchen · laṅgarī: its cook.
[^44]: lotus mark: ਪਦਮ, padam, a mark on the palm believed to bring endless plenty. BSS: however much he served, nothing ever ran out.
[^45]: Jassa Singh: Jassa Singh Ahluwalia (1718 to 1783), later leader of the Dal Khalsa. BSS: he kept the grain for the horses; Mata Sundri taught him kīrtan and sent him to serve the Panth.
[^46]: Kalal: the distillers' caste.
[^47]: field guns: ਰਹਕਲੈ, rahkalā, light guns on carts.
[^48]: long muskets: ਜੰਜਾਇਲ, janjāil (jazail), the long-barrelled musket · swivel guns: ਜੰਬੂਰ, jambūr. BSS: the big guns were left at Amritsar.
[^49]: ser: about 930 g. BSS: a ser each day, since only big, strong Singhs could carry the guns.
[^50]: Hundal: a Jatt clan · Jhita: the poet's ਝੀਤੈ, a village near Amritsar (unverified).

[^53]: Binod Singh of the Trehans, Guru Angad's clan, from Khadur Sahib; Kahan Singh of the Bhallas, Guru Amar Das's clan (BSS: both sent with the Panth by Guru Gobind Singh from Hazur Sahib, with drum and banner). The source prints ਬਨੋਜ for Binod.
[^54]: jatha: a band of Singhs under its own leader.
[^55]: Shahīds: the jatha of the martyrs (shahīd, martyr), the Singhs ready to die · Nihang: the Akalis, the Khalsa's warriors in blue. BSS adds Dial Singh among its leaders.
[^56]: Kot Buddha: BSS: Budhe ka Kot, beyond Patti toward Sabhrawan, a village of martyrs · Gill: a Jatt clan.
[^57]: Ranghreta: the Sikhs from the Chuhra caste, by tradition named the Guru's own sons. BSS: Bir Singh and Amar Singh led them; their thirteen hundred horse did not count the men on foot.
[^58]: Farzulla: Farzullapur near Lahore (BSS).
[^59]: ਖਾਨੂ ਨਬਾਬ ਖਾਨੋ: read with both commentators as Khano, the sister of the nawab Zakariya Khan, called Khan Bahadur. BSS: his younger sister.
[^60]: in the rulers' service: ਚਕਰੈਲ, from chākar, servant. BSS: she told her brother they passed his secrets to the Khalsa, and had them all shot.
[^61]: Virk: a Jatt clan.
[^62]: khud khudā: God himself, the Khalsa as the poet sees it.
[^63]: platform of justice: ਚਬੂਤਰਾ. BSS: the platform where justice was given and pay handed out; he sat there to give judgement.

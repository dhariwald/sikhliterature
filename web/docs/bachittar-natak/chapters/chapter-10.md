# Bachittar Natak, Chapter 10

## The Coming of the Khanzada

>**Questions**
>
>1. Who sent his son against Anandpur?
>2. Who woke the Guru?

ਚੌਪਈ ॥ *Chaupai*

ਬਹੁਤ ਬਰਖ ਇਹ ਭਾਂਤਿ ਬਿਤਾਏ ॥ ਚੁਨਿ ਚੁਨਿ ਚੋਰ ਸਬੈ ਗਹਿ ਘਾਏ ॥\
ਕੇਤਕ ਭਾਜਿ ਸਹਿਰ ਤੇ ਗਏ ॥ ਭੂਖ ਮਰਤ ਫਿਰਿ ਆਵਤ ਭਏ ॥੧॥

We passed many years in this way;\
we hunted out every thief, seized them and put them to death;\
a number of them fled from the town\
and, starving, came back again. (1)

ਤਬ ਲੌ ਖਾਨ ਦਿਲਾਵਰ ਆਏ ॥ ਪੂਤ ਆਪਨ ਹਮ ਓਰ ਪਠਾਏ ॥\
ਦ੍ਯੈਕ ਘਰੀ ਬੀਤੀ ਨਿਸਿ ਜਬੈ ॥ ਚੜਤ ਕਰੀ ਖਾਨਨ ਮਿਲਿ ਤਬੈ ॥੨॥

Then Dilawar Khan[^1] came\
and sent his son[^2] against us;\
when the night was not yet an hour old,[^3]\
the Khans gathered and rode against us. (2)

ਜਬ ਦਲ ਪਾਰ ਨਦੀ ਕੇ ਆਯੋ ॥ ਆਨ ਆਲਮੈ ਹਮੈ ਜਗਾਯੋ ॥\
ਸੋਰੁ ਪਰਾ ਸਭ ਹੀ ਨਰ ਜਾਗੇ ॥ ਗਹਿ ਗਹਿ ਸਸਤ੍ਰ ਬੀਰ ਰਿਸ ਪਾਗੇ ॥੩॥

When the army came up on the far side of the river,\
Alam[^4] came and woke us;\
an uproar went up and every man awoke,\
and the heroes seized their weapons, steeped in fury. (3)

ਛੂਟਨ ਲਗੀ ਤੁਫ਼ੰਗੈਂ ਤਬ ਹੀ ॥ ਗਹਿ ਗਹਿ ਸਸਤ੍ਰ ਰਿਸਾਨੇ ਸਬ ਹੀ ॥\
ਕ੍ਰੂਰ ਭਾਂਤਿ ਤਿਨ ਕਰੀ ਪੁਕਾਰਾ ॥ ਸੋਰੁ ਸੁਨਾ ਸਰਤਾ ਕੇ ਪਾਰਾ ॥੪॥

At once the muskets[^5] began to fire,\
and all of them took up their weapons and blazed with fury;\
they raised a terrible cry,\
and the uproar was heard across the river. (4)

ਭੁਜੰਗ ਪ੍ਰਯਾਤ ਛੰਦ ॥ *Bhujang Prayat Chhand*

ਬਜੀ ਭੇਰ ਭੁੰਕਾਰ ਧੁੰਕੇ ਨਗਾਰੇ ॥\
ਮਹਾਂ ਬੀਰ ਬਾਨੈਤ ਬੰਕੇ ਬਕਾਰੇ ॥\
ਭਏ ਬਾਹੁ ਆਘਾਤ ਨੱਚੇ ਮਰਾਲੰ ॥\
ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾ ਸਿੰਧ ਕਾਲੀ ਗਰੱਜੀ ਕਰਾਲੰ ॥੫॥

The war horns blared and the war drums boomed;[^6]\
the great heroes, bold bowmen, roared their challenge;\
arms struck blow on blow, and the horses danced;\
Kali,[^7] the Ocean of Mercy, thundered terribly. (5)

ਨਦੀਯੰ ਲਖਿਯੋ ਕਾਲ ਰਾਤ੍ਰੰ ਸਮਾਨੰ ॥\
ਕਰੇ ਸੂਰਮਾ ਸੀਤ ਪਿੰਗੰ ਪ੍ਰਮਾਨੰ ॥\
ਇਤੇ ਬੀਰ ਗੱਜੇ ਭਏ ਨਾਦ ਭਾਰੇ ॥\
ਭਜੇ ਖਾਨ ਖੂਨੀ ਬਿਨਾ ਸਸਤ੍ਰ ਝਾਰੇ ॥੬॥

The river looked to them like the night of death,[^8]\
and the cold left their warriors as numb as cripples;\
on this side the heroes roared, and the din was heavy,\
and the murderous Khans fled without striking a blow. (6)

ਨਰਾਜ ਛੰਦ ॥ *Naraj Chhand*

ਨਿੱਲਜ ਖਾਨ ਭੱਜਿਓ ॥\
ਕਿਨੀ ਨ ਸਸਤ੍ਰ ਸੱਜਿਓ ॥\
ਸੁ ਤਿਆਗ ਖੇਤ ਕੌ ਚਲੇ ॥\
ਸੁ ਬੀਰ ਬੀਰਹਾ ਭਲੇ ॥

The shameless Khan fled,\
and not one of them took up arms;\
they left the field and went,\
those fine heroes, those fine killers of heroes. (7)

ਚਲੇ ਤੁਰੇ ਤੁਰਾਇ ਕੈ ॥\
ਸਕੇ ਨ ਸਸਤ੍ਰ ਉਠਾਇ ਕੈ ॥\
ਨ ਲੈ ਹਥਿਆਰ ਗੱਜਹੀਂ ॥\
ਨਿਹਾਰ ਨਾਰਿ ਲੱਜਹੀਂ ॥੮॥

They spurred their horses and went,\
unable to lift a weapon;\
they took up no arms and raised no war cry,\
and women who saw them felt shame. (8)

ਦੋਹਰਾ ॥ *Dohra*

ਬਰਵਾ ਗਾਉਂ ਉਜਾਰ ਕੈ ਕਰੇ ਮੁਕਾਮ ਭਲਾਨ ॥\
ਪ੍ਰਭ ਬਲ ਹਮੈ ਨ ਛੁਇ ਸਕੈ ਭਾਜਤ ਭਏ ਨਿਦਾਨ ॥੯॥

They laid waste the village of Barwa and made camp at Bhalan;[^9]\
by the might of Prabhu[^10] they could not touch us, and in the end they fled. (9)

ਤਬ ਬਲ ਈਹਾ ਨ ਪਰ ਸਕੈ ਬਰਵਾ ਹਨਾ ਰਿਸਾਇ ॥\
ਸਾਲਿਨ ਰਸ ਜਿਮ ਬਾਨੀਯੋ ਰੋਰਨ ਖਾਤ ਬਨਾਇ ॥੧੦॥

Then, since their strength could not fall on us here, they struck Barwa in their rage,[^11]\
as a bania who craves the taste of meat curry makes it of stones and eats that.[^12] (10)

---
ਇਤਿ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਬਚਿਤ੍ਰ ਨਾਟਕ ਗ੍ਰੰਥੇ ਖਾਨਜਾਦੇ ਕੋ ਆਗਮਨ ਤ੍ਰਾਸਿਤ ਉਠ ਜੈਬੋ ਬਰਨਨੰ ਨਾਮ ਦਸਮੋ ਧਿਆਇ ਸਮਾਪਤਮ ਸਤੁ ਸੁਭਮ ਸਤੁ ॥੧੦॥ ਅਫਜੂ ॥੩੫੪॥

*Here ends the tenth chapter of the holy book Bachittar Natak, called the Account of the Coming of the Khanzada and His Flight in Terror. All is true, all is blessed. Chapter 10; verses so far, 354.*

*Sri Dasam Granth, Bachittar Natak, Sri Guru Gobind Singh Ji. Gurmukhi text of record from the Shabad OS database, Sri Dasam Granth, section 54. Punjabi commentary and notes from the Shiromani Steek of the Dasam Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji, part 1, published by Baba Teja Singh Ji Nihang Singh of Mehron, 1999, written Steek in the notes; the steek of Narain Singh consulted for meaning. Cite as Bachittar Natak 10.6.*

### Notes

[^1]: Dilawar Khan, governor of Lahore. Steek: from Alif Khan's report he judged that the hill rajas had fought at Nadaun with the Guru's help; Bhim Chand, taking counsel in secret, cleared himself and laid all the blame on the Guru, and so proved himself ungrateful. The Guru learned of it and set about his preparations at Anandpur: the fort of Anandgarh was finished, and he turned to the others.
[^2]: The Khanzada, the son of the Khan: Rustam Khan (Steek, Narain Singh). Steek: Dilawar Khan sent him to avenge the defeat at Nadaun, ordering him to attack the Guru at Anandpur first, for once the Guru was beaten all would be won. When he came near Anandpur the streams were running in flood, and he halted on the far side of the river.
[^3]: ਦ੍ਯੈਕ ਘਰੀ, one or two gharis, the ghari being 24 minutes.
[^4]: Alam Singh, the Guru's doorkeeper (Steek, Narain Singh). Steek: the Guru at once ordered the Singhs to make ready; the great drums were struck, the muskets fired blank, and the jaikaras rang out, and the Khan's army, already numb with the cold of the dark night, was driven by dread of the Guru's Singhs to run.
[^5]: ਤੁਫ਼ੰਗ *tufaṅg*, musket.
[^6]: ਭੇਰ *bher*, the war horn · ਨਗਾਰੇ *nagāre*, the great war drums.
[^7]: Kali, the dark goddess of death and war.
[^8]: Steek: as the enemy came over the river, the Singhs took up their weapons and roared, and to the enemy the river itself took on the form of death.
[^9]: Steek: Barwa and Bhalan are villages of the Nurpur police station in the Una tehsil of the Hoshiarpur district (Una is now in Himachal Pradesh).
[^10]: Prabhu, the Lord.
[^11]: The Shabad OS text prints ਤਬ, then; Narain Singh prints ਤਵ, your: by your might, Lord, they could not fall on us here.
[^12]: ਸਾਲਿਨ *sālan*, a meat curry · bania, the merchant caste, who keep from meat. Narain Singh: a bania who longs for meat but may not eat it makes his curry of stones; so they could not touch us, and plundered poor villagers instead.

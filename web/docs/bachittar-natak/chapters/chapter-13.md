# Bachittar Natak, Chapter 13

## The Coming of the Shahzada and the Ahadis

>**Questions**
>
>1. Whom did Aurangzeb send to Punjab?
>2. Baba's house and Babar's: kings of what?
>3. Name of the first ahadi?
>5. What did the ahadis do to those who fled?

#### ਸਹਜਾਦੇ ਕੋ ਆਗਮਨ ਮਦ੍ਰ ਦੇਸ ॥ *The coming of the Shahzada to the Madra country*

ਚੌਪਈ ॥ *Chaupai*

ਇਹ ਬਿਧਿ ਸੋ ਬਧ ਭਯੋ ਜੁਝਾਰਾ ॥ ਆਨ ਬਸੇ ਤਬ ਧਾਮ ਲੁਝਾਰਾ ॥\
ਤਬ ਅਉਰੰਗ ਮਨ ਮਾਹਿ ਰਿਸਾਵਾ ॥ ਮੱਦ੍ਰ ਦੇਸ ਕੋ ਪੂਤ ਪਠਾਵਾ ॥੧॥

In this way Jujhar was slain,\
and then the fighters came back and settled in their homes;\
then Aurang[^1] raged in his heart\
and sent his son to the Madra country.[^2] (1)

ਤਿੱਹ ਆਵਤ ਸਭ ਲੋਕ ਡਰਾਨੇ ॥ ਬਡੇ ਬਡੇ ਗਿਰ ਹੇਰ ਲੁਕਾਨੇ ॥\
ਹਮਹੂੰ ਲੋਗਨ ਅਧਿਕ ਡਰਾਯੋ ॥ ਕਾਲ ਕਰਮ ਕੋ ਮਰਮ ਨ ਪਾਯੋ ॥੨॥

At his coming all the people took fright\
and sought out the great hills and hid there;\
people tried hard to frighten us too,\
for they did not grasp the secret of the workings of Kal.[^3] (2)

ਕਿਤਕ ਲੋਕ ਤਜਿ ਸੰਗਿ ਸਿਧਾਰੇ ॥ ਜਾਇ ਬਸੇ ਗਿਰਵਰ ਜਹਿ ਭਾਰੇ ॥\
ਚਿਤ ਮੂਜੀਯਨ ਕੋ ਅਧਿਕ ਡਰਾਨਾ ॥ ਤਿਨੈ ਉਬਾਰ ਨ ਅਪਨਾ ਜਾਨਾ ॥੩॥

A number of people left our company\
and went to live where the great mountains are;\
the hearts of those wretches[^4] were badly afraid,\
and they did not think they would be safe with us. (3)

ਤਬ ਅਉਰੰਗ ਜੀਅ ਮਾਂਝ ਰਿਸਾਏ ॥ ਏਕ ਅਹਦੀਆ ਈਹਾਂ ਪਠਾਏ ॥\
ਹਮ ਤੇ ਭਾਜਿ ਬਿਮੁਖ ਜੇ ਗਏ ॥ ਤਿਨ ਕੇ ਧਾਮ ਗਿਰਾਵਤ ਭਏ ॥੪॥

Then Aurang raged within\
and sent an ahdi[^5] here;\
those who had turned their faces from us and fled,\
their houses he pulled down.[^6] (4)

ਜੇ ਅਪਨੇ ਗੁਰ ਤੇ ਮੁਖ ਫਿਰਹੈਂ ॥ ਈਹਾਂ ਊਹਾਂ ਤਿਨ ਕੇ ਗ੍ਰਿਹ ਗਿਰਿਹੈਂ ॥\
ਇਹਾਂ ਉਪਹਾਸ ਨ ਸੁਰ ਪੁਰ ਬਾਸਾ ॥ ਸਭ ਬਾਤਨ ਤੇ ਰਹੈ ਨਿਰਾਸਾ ॥੫॥

Those who turn their faces from their own Guru,\
their houses shall fall in this world and the next;\
here they are mocked, and they have no home in the city of the gods;\
they are left without hope in everything. (5)

ਦੂਖ ਭੂਖ ਤਿਨ ਕੋ ਰਹੈ ਲਾਗੀ ॥ ਸੰਤ ਸੇਵ ਤੇ ਜੋ ਹੈ ਤਿਆਗੀ ॥\
ਜਗਤ ਬਿਖੈ ਕੋਈ ਕਾਮ ਨ ਸਰਹੀਂ ॥ ਅੰਤਹਿ ਕੁੰਡ ਨਰਕ ਕੀ ਪਰਹੀਂ ॥੬॥

Pain and hunger cling to the man\
who has given up serving the saints;\
no work of theirs prospers in the world,\
and at the last they fall into the pit of hell. (6)

ਤਿਨ ਕੋ ਸਦਾ ਜਗਤ ਉਪਹਾਸਾ ॥ ਅੰਤਹਿ ਕੁੰਡ ਨਰਕ ਕੀ ਬਾਸਾ ॥\
ਗੁਰ ਪਗ ਤੇ ਜੇ ਬੇਮੁਖ ਸਿਧਾਰੇ ॥ ਈਹਾਂ ਊਹਾ ਤਿਨ ਕੇ ਮੁਖ ਕਾਰੇ ॥੭॥

The world mocks them always,\
and at the last they dwell in the pit of hell;\
those who turn their faces from the Guru's feet and go,\
their faces are blackened in this world and the next. (7)

ਪੁਤ੍ਰ ਪਉਤ੍ਰ ਤਿਨ ਕੇ ਨਹੀਂ ਫਰੈਂ ॥ ਦੁਖ ਦੈ ਮਾਤ ਪਿਤਾ ਕੋ ਮਰੈਂ ॥\
ਗੁਰ ਦੋਖੀ ਸਗ ਕੀ ਮ੍ਰਿਤ ਪਾਵੈ ॥ ਨਰਕ ਕੁੰਡ ਡਾਰੇ ਪਛੁਤਾਵੈ ॥੮॥

Their sons and grandsons do not flourish;\
they die bringing grief to mother and father;\
the slanderer of the Guru dies a dog's death\
and repents when he is thrown into the pit of hell. (8)

ਬਾਬੇ ਕੇ ਬਾਬਰ ਕੇ ਦੋਊ ॥ ਆਪ ਕਰੇ ਪਰਮੇਸਰ ਸੋਊ ॥\
ਦੀਨ ਸਾਹ ਇਨ ਕੋ ਪਹਿਚਾਨੋ ॥ ਦੁਨੀ ਪੱਤਿ ਉਨ ਕੌ ਅਨੁਮਾਨੋ ॥੯॥

The house of the Baba and the house of Babar,[^7] both of them,\
the Supreme Lord himself made;\
know the one as the kings of faith,\
and take the other as the lords of the world. (9)

ਜੋ ਬਾਬੇ ਕੇ ਦਾਮ ਨ ਦੈਹੈਂ ॥ ਤਿਨ ਤੇ ਗਹਿ ਬਾਬਰ ਕੇ ਲੈਹੈਂ ॥\
ਦੈ ਦੈ ਤਿਨ ਕੌ ਬਡੀ ਸਜਾਇ ॥ ਪੁਨਿ ਲੈਹੈਂ ਗ੍ਰਿਹ ਲੂਟ ਬਨਾਇ ॥੧੦॥

Those who will not give the Baba his money,\
Babar's men shall seize it from them,\
and punish them heavily again and again,\
and then plunder their homes as well. (10)

ਜਬ ਹ੍ਯੈਹੈਂ ਬੇਮੁਖ ਬਿਨਾ ਧਨ ॥ ਤਬ ਚੜਿਹੈਂ ਸਿਖਨ ਕਹ ਮਾਂਗਨ ॥\
ਜੇ ਜੇ ਸਿਖ ਤਿਨੈ ਧਨ ਦੈਹੈਂ ॥ ਲੂਟ ਮਲੇਛ ਤਿਨੂ ਕੌ ਲੈਹੈਂ ॥੧੧॥

When those who turned their faces away are left without money,\
they will set out to beg from the Sikhs;\
and every Sikh who gives them money,\
the barbarians[^8] will plunder him. (11)

ਜਬ ਹੁਇ ਹੈ ਤਿਨ ਦਰਬ ਬਿਨਾਸਾ ॥ ਤਬ ਧਰਿਹੈ ਨਿਜ ਗੁਰ ਕੀ ਆਸਾ ॥\
ਜਬ ਤੇ ਗੁਰ ਦਰਸਨ ਕੌ ਐਹੈਂ ॥ ਤਬ ਤਿਨ ਕੋ ਗੁਰ ਮੁਖ ਨ ਲਗੈਹੈਂ ॥੧੨॥

When their wealth is gone,\
then they will set their hopes on their own Guru;\
when they come for a sight of the Guru,\
the Guru will not let them near his face. (12)

ਬਿਦਾ ਬਿਨਾ ਜੈਹੈਂ ਤਬ ਧਾਮੰ ॥ ਸਰਿਹੈ ਕੋਈ ਨ ਤਿਨ ਕੋ ਕਾਮੰ ॥\
ਗੁਰ ਦਰ ਢੋਈ ਨ ਪ੍ਰਭ ਪੁਰ ਵਾਸਾ ॥ ਦੁਹੂੰ ਠਉਰ ਤੇ ਰਹੇ ਨਿਰਾਸਾ ॥੧੩॥

Then they will go home without the Guru's leave,\
and no work of theirs will prosper;\
no shelter at the Guru's door, no home in the city of Prabhu:\
they are left without hope in both places. (13)

ਜੇ ਜੇ ਗੁਰ ਚਰਨਨ ਰਤ ਹ੍ਯੈਹੈਂ ॥ ਤਿਨ ਕੋ ਕਸਟ ਨ ਦੇਖਨ ਪੈਹੈਂ ॥\
ਰਿਧ ਸਿਧ ਤਿਨ ਕੇ ਗ੍ਰਿਹ ਮਾਹੀਂ ॥ ਪਾਪ ਤਾਪ ਛ੍ਯੈ ਸਕੈ ਨ ਛਾਹੀਂ ॥੧੪॥

All who are steeped in love of the Guru's feet\
shall never have to look on suffering;\
riches and powers[^9] are in their homes,\
and sin and sorrow cannot touch their shadow. (14)

ਤਿਹ ਮਲੇਛ ਛ੍ਯੈਹੈ ਨਹੀਂ ਛਾਹਾਂ ॥ ਅਸਟ ਸਿਧ ਹ੍ਯੈ ਹੈ ਘਰਿ ਮਾਹਾਂ ॥\
ਹਾਸ ਕਰਤ ਜੋ ਉਦਮ ਉਠੈਹੈਂ ॥ ਨਵੋ ਨਿਧਿ ਤਿਨ ਕੇ ਘਰਿ ਐਹੈਂ ॥੧੫॥

No barbarian shall touch their shadow;\
the eight powers[^10] shall be in their homes;\
whatever they take up, even in jest,\
the nine treasures shall come to their homes. (15)

ਮਿਰਜਾ ਬੇਗ ਹੁਤੋ ਤਿਹ ਨਾਮੰ ॥ ਜਿਨ ਢਾਹੇ ਬੇਮੁਖਨ ਕੇ ਧਾਮੰ ॥\
ਸਬ ਸਨਮੁਖ ਗੁਰ ਆਪ ਬਚਾਏ ॥ ਤਿਨ ਕੇ ਬਾਰ ਨ ਬਾਂਕਨ ਪਾਏ ॥੧੬॥

Mirza Beg[^11] was the name of the man\
who pulled down the houses of those who had turned away;\
all who stood facing the Guru, the Guru himself saved,\
and not one hair of theirs was harmed. (16)

ਉਤ ਅਉਰੰਗ ਜੀਅ ਅਧਿਕ ਰਿਸਾਯੋ ॥ ਚਾਰ ਅਹਦੀਯਨ ਅਉਰ ਪਠਾਯੋ ॥\
ਜੇ ਬੇਮੁਖ ਤਾਂ ਤੇ ਬਚਿ ਆਏ ॥ ਤਿਨ ਕੇ ਗ੍ਰਿਹ ਪੁਨਿ ਇਨੈ ਗਿਰਾਏ ॥੧੭॥

Over there Aurang raged still more\
and sent four more ahdis;\
those who had turned away and escaped the first,\
their houses these men pulled down again. (17)

ਜੇ ਤਜਿ ਭਜੇ ਹੁਤੇ ਗੁਰ ਆਨਾ ॥ ਤਿਨ ਪੁਨਿ ਗੁਰੂ ਅਹਦੀਅਹਿ ਜਾਨਾ ॥\
ਮੂਤ੍ਰ ਡਾਰ ਤਿਨ ਸੀਸ ਮੁੰਡਾਏ ॥ ਪਾਹੁਰਿ ਜਾਨਿ ਗ੍ਰਿਹਹਿ ਲੈ ਆਏ ॥੧੮॥

Those who had run away, leaving the Guru's protection,\
now took the ahdis for their Guru;\
the ahdis poured urine on them and shaved their heads,\
and they took it home as though it were pāhul.[^12] (18)

ਜੇ ਜੇ ਭਾਜ ਹੁਤੇ ਬਿਨੁ ਆਇਸੁ ॥ ਕਹੋ ਅਹਦੀਅਹਿ ਕਿਨੈ ਬਤਾਇਸੁ ॥\
ਮੂੰਡ ਮੂੰਡਿ ਕਰਿ ਸਹਿਰ ਫਿਰਾਏ ॥ ਕਾਰ ਭੇਟ ਜਨੁ ਲੈਨ ਸਿਧਾਏ ॥੧੯॥

All those who had run away without leave,\
tell me, who pointed them out to the ahdis?[^13]\
The ahdis shaved their heads and paraded them through the town,\
as though they were going round to collect offerings.[^14] (19)

ਪਾਛੇ ਲਾਗਿ ਲਰਿਕਵਾ ਚਲੇ ॥ ਜਾਨੁਕ ਸਿੱਖ ਸਖਾ ਹੈਂ ਭਲੇ ॥\
ਛਿੱਕੇ ਤੋਬਰਾ ਬਦਨ ਚੜਾਏ ॥ ਜਨੁ ਗ੍ਰਿਹ ਖਾਨ ਮਲੀਦਾ ਆਏ ॥੨੦॥

Boys went trailing after them,\
as though they were their good Sikhs and companions;\
muzzles and nosebags[^15] were pulled over their faces,\
as though they had come home to eat malīdā.[^16] (20)

ਮਸਤਕ ਸੁਭੇ ਪਨਹੀਯਨ ਘਾਇ ॥ ਜਨੁ ਕਰਿ ਟੀਕਾ ਦਏ ਬਨਾਇ ॥\
ਸੀਸ ਈਟ ਕੇ ਘਾਇ ਕਰੇਹੀ ॥ ਜਨੁ ਤਿਨੁ ਭੇਟ ਪੁਰਾਤਨ ਦੇਹੀ ॥੨੧॥

Their foreheads were adorned with the marks of shoes\
as though a tilak had been put on them;\
they were wounded on the head with bricks\
as though they were being paid the offerings owed of old. (21)

ਦੋਹਰਾ ॥ *Dohra*

ਕਬਹੂੰ ਰਣ ਜੂਝਿਓ ਨਹੀ ਕਛੁ ਦੈ ਜਸੁ ਨਹਿ ਲੀਨ ॥\
ਗਾਂਵ ਬਸਤ ਜਾਨਿਯੋ ਨਹੀ ਜਮ ਸੋ ਕਿਨ ਕਹਿ ਦੀਨ ॥੨੨॥

He never once fought in battle, never won fame by giving anything,\
no one knew him even in the village where he lived: who told Yama[^17] about him? (22)

ਚੌਪਈ ॥ *Chaupai*

ਇਹ ਬਿਧਿ ਤਿਨੋ ਭਯੋ ਉਪਹਾਸਾ ॥ ਸਭ ਸੰਤਨ ਮਿਲਿ ਲਖਿਓ ਤਮਾਸਾ ॥\
ਸੰਤਨ ਕਸਟ ਨ ਦੇਖਨ ਪਾਯੋ ॥ ਆਪ ਹਾਥ ਦੈ ਨਾਥ ਬਚਾਯੋ ॥੨੩॥

In this way they were made a mockery,\
and all the saints together watched the show;\
the saints were not made to look on suffering,\
for the Lord gave his own hand and saved them. (23)

ਚਾਰਣੀ ॥ ਦੋਹਰਾ ॥ *Charani, Dohra*

ਜਿਸ ਨੋ ਸਾਜਨ ਰਾਖਸੀ ਦੁਸਮਨ ਕਵਨ ਬਿਚਾਰ ॥\
ਛ੍ਯੈ ਨ ਸਕੈ ਤਿਹ ਛਾਹਿ ਕੌ ਨਿਹਫਲ ਜਾਇ ਗਵਾਰ ॥੨੪॥

Whom the Friend protects, what can an enemy do to him?\
He cannot touch his shadow; the fool goes away with nothing. (24)

ਜੋ ਸਾਧੂ ਸਰਣੀ ਪਰੇ ਤਿਨ ਕੇ ਕਵਨ ਬਿਚਾਰ ॥\
ਦੰਤ ਜੀਭ ਜਿਮ ਰਾਖਿ ਹੈ ਦੁਸਟ ਅਰਿਸਟ ਸੰਘਾਰ ॥੨੫॥

Those who take refuge with the holy, what need they fear?\
He keeps them as the tongue is kept among the teeth, and destroys the wicked and every harm. (25)

---
ਇਤਿ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਬਚਿਤ੍ਰ ਨਾਟਕ ਗ੍ਰੰਥੇ ਸ਼ਾਹਜ਼ਾਦੇ ਵ ਅਹਿਦੀਆ ਗਮਨ ਬਰਨਨੰ ਨਾਮ ਤ੍ਰੌਦਸਮੋ ਧਿਆਇ ਸਮਾਪਤਮ ਸਤੁ ਸੁਭਮ ਸਤੁ ॥੧੩॥ ਅਫਜੂ ॥੪੬੦॥

*Here ends the thirteenth chapter of the holy book Bachittar Natak, called the Account of the Coming of the Shahzada and the Ahdis. All is true, all is blessed. Chapter 13; verses so far, 460.*

### Notes

[^1]: Aurang, the poet's short form for Aurangzeb · Shahzada, a Patshah's son, here Prince Muazzam, later the Patshah Bahadur Shah (Narain Singh).
[^2]: The Madra country (ਮੱਦ੍ਰ ਦੇਸ), the old name for the Punjab. Steek: when news of Husaini's death reached Lahore, Dilawar Khan wrote to Delhi of the Guru's growing power and his own losses; Aurangzeb was busy putting down Bijapur in the Deccan, and by royal order Prince Muazzam came to Lahore in Sammat 1752 (1695 CE).
[^3]: Kal (ਕਾਲ), the Lord as all-conquering Time.
[^4]: ਮੂਜੀ, a wretch, a vile person; Narain Singh reads cowards.
[^5]: ahdi (ਅਹਦੀਆ), an officer of the Patshah's own corps of gentlemen troopers, sent on special errands; Narain Singh: an official.
[^6]: Narain Singh reads the one who raged at v. 4 and v. 17 as Aurangzeb's son, Bahadur Shah; the verse names Aurang.
[^7]: the Baba, Guru Nanak, and the Gurus' house after him · Babar, Babur, founder of the Mughal line, and the Patshahs after him. Steek: the prince's chief secretary, Nand Lal, had long heard of the Guru, and told him how Guru Nanak had given Babur his kingdom and how Humayun had come to Guru Angad's court; with that the poison Dilawar Khan had poured into the prince's mind left him, and he stayed at Lahore and sent Mirza Beg.
[^8]: barbarians, ਮਲੇਛ *mlechh*.
[^9]: ਰਿਧ ਸਿਧ, riddhi siddhi, riches and powers.
[^10]: the eight powers (ਅਸਟ ਸਿਧ), the eight siddhis of the yogis; Steek: it lists eighteen powers in all, among them taking any form, growing great or small at will, hearing and seeing from far off, and entering another body, of which eight can meet every need · the nine treasures (ਨਵੋ ਨਿਧਿ), the nine treasures of Kuber, all wealth.
[^11]: Narain Singh: Mirza Jafar Beg, a good-hearted officer who, on reaching Anandpur and hearing about the Guru, came to love the Guru's house. Steek: this is why the prince and the ahdis did the Guru no harm, but sought out and punished those who had turned their faces from him.
[^12]: pāhul, the rite of initiation; the line mocks them for taking the ahdis' urine as if it were the Guru's pāhul.
[^13]: Read here as a question; Narain Singh reads that the ahdis asked them who else was with them, and paraded them when they told.
[^14]: ਕਾਰ ਭੇਟ, offerings collected for the Guru, as the masands went round collecting them.
[^15]: ਛਿੱਕੇ, a muzzle of netting · ਤੋਬਰਾ, a horse's nosebag; Narain Singh: nosebags of horse dung.
[^16]: malīdā (ਮਲੀਦਾ), a sweet of crumbled bread with ghee and sugar; Narain Singh: churma.
[^17]: Yama, the god of death.
